Idea. When a business owns a van, the van's cost stays in its cost account. The depreciation charged each year is collected in a separate account, and it builds up year after year. Cost minus that total gives the carrying value. That separate account is the provision for depreciation account. It holds the accumulated depreciation for a class of assets, such as motor vehicles. Its normal balance is a credit.

Method. Start with the opening balance, on the credit side, as balance brought down on the first day of the year. Here it is ten thousand. If an asset is sold, its depreciation leaves this account. Work it out to the date of sale, using the time policy, and debit it, with the word disposal as the detail. Here it is three thousand. Then work out this year's charge on the assets still owned, and credit it with the detail income statement. Here it is four thousand. Finally, balance it. The credits total fourteen thousand, the debits three thousand. The balance carried down, eleven thousand, goes on the debit side, the totals agree, and the balance comes down on the credit side next year.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. We must prepare Alex's motor vehicles provision for depreciation account for the year ended 30 April 2024, balance it, and bring the balance down. Four marks. The opening balance is eighty-five thousand. On 30 September 2023, Alex sold a vehicle that cost thirty-six thousand, with a residual value of eight thousand. Depreciation is twenty per cent a year, straight line, with none in the year of disposal. The sold vehicle: thirty-six thousand minus eight thousand is twenty-eight thousand. Twenty per cent of that is five thousand six hundred a year. It was charged for two years, so eleven thousand two hundred goes to disposal. The vehicles kept all year cost one hundred and seventy-two thousand minus thirty-six thousand: one hundred and thirty-six thousand. No residual value is given for them, so twenty per cent of that is twenty-seven thousand two hundred. Now the account. Eighty-five thousand opening balance on the credit side. Eleven thousand two hundred to disposal on the debit side. Twenty-seven thousand two hundred from the income statement on the credit side. The credits total one hundred and twelve thousand two hundred. Take away eleven thousand two hundred: the balance is one hundred and one thousand, carried down on the debit side and brought down on the credit side on 1 May 2024. Common mistake. A common mistake is putting the year's charge on the debit side. The charge adds to the provision, so it is a credit. Another is writing depreciation or bank as the detail. The details are the other accounts: income statement and disposal.
