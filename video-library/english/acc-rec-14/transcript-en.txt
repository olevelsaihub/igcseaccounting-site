Idea. You start a lemonade stand. You have a table worth forty dollars and lemons worth ten. You borrowed fifteen from your mum. Your own stake in the stand is fifty minus fifteen: thirty-five dollars. Items like these are first written in the general journal. It records what fits no other book: opening balances, a non-current asset bought on credit, or assets the owner brings in. Each line has a date, an account and an amount.

Method. In an opening journal, every asset is a debit. Every liability is a credit. A bank overdraft is owed to the bank, so it is a credit too. Equity is the balancing figure: total assets minus total liabilities. It is the owner's capital, so it is a credit. Then check that the debits equal the credits. For other one-off entries, debit the account that gains value. Machinery bought on credit: debit machinery, credit the supplier. Never purchases, which is only for goods to resell.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2024. Sunal has never kept full records. Prepare an opening journal entry on the first of July 2023. A narrative is required. Four marks. Here are Sunal's assets and liabilities. Sort them. The bank overdraft, trade payables and other payables are liabilities. The other five are assets. Assets total one hundred and six thousand, six hundred and forty-two. Liabilities total six thousand, six hundred and forty-two. So equity is exactly one hundred thousand dollars. Now the journal. The five assets go in the debit column. The three liabilities and equity go in the credit column. Both columns total one hundred and six thousand, six hundred and forty-two. Finally the narrative: being the assets, liabilities and equity at the first of July 2023, to open the books. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is debiting the bank overdraft, as if it were money in the bank. It is owed to the bank, so it is a credit. Another is debiting purchases for a non-current asset bought on credit. Debit the asset account.
