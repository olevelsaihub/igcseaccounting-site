Idea. When you buy three notebooks at two dollars each, the bill says six dollars. A business invoice works the same way, one line at a time. An invoice shows the seller, the customer, the date and an invoice number. Then come the line totals, a subtotal, the trade discount, and the total the customer owes.

Method. Step one: each line total is quantity times unit price. Step two: add the lines for the subtotal. Step three: trade discount is a percentage of the subtotal. Step four: take it off for the total. Cash discount is only a term, such as five per cent for payment within thirty days. Do not deduct it, because the customer has not paid early yet. A credit note is laid out the same way. It uses the same trade discount as the sale, so the customer is credited the net amount.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2025. We must complete an invoice where shown. Five marks. Novura Trading sells two kinds of cable to All Media. We must fill in the gaps, one to five: two line totals, the subtotal, the trade discount, and the total. Note the terms at the bottom. Line one: a hundred and fifty of three core cable at five dollars is seven hundred and fifty dollars. Line two: four hundred at two dollars twenty is eight hundred and eighty. Add the lines. Seven hundred and fifty plus eight hundred and eighty is one thousand six hundred and thirty: the subtotal. Trade discount is twenty per cent of the subtotal: three hundred and twenty-six. Take it off: the total is one thousand three hundred and four dollars. The five per cent for thirty days is a cash discount term, so it is not deducted. Common mistake. A common mistake is taking off the cash discount as well. The total stays one thousand three hundred and four. Another is working out trade discount on one line only. Use the subtotal.
