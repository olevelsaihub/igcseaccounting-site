Idea. A sports shop buys football boots from a supplier. The supplier's catalogue price is the list price. Because the shop is a trade customer, the supplier takes off a trade discount straight away. Say the list price is two hundred dollars, with ten per cent trade discount. Two hundred minus twenty is one hundred and eighty. That is the net invoice amount, and it is what both businesses record. Cash discount is a reward for paying within a time limit. It is worked out on the net amount, not the list price. Pay in time and you pay one hundred and seventy-one. Pay late and you pay the full one hundred and eighty.

Method. Step one, take trade discount off the list price. Step two, check the payment date against the cash discount period. Step three, if it is in time, deduct cash discount from the net amount. If not, pay the net amount in full. To find a rate, divide the discount by the amount it came from, and multiply by one hundred. Thirty dollars off six hundred is five per cent.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2023, Paper 1. Calculate the amount paid if payment was made on the third of March 2023. One mark. The invoice is dated the first of March: eighteen bikes at three hundred dollars, ten per cent trade discount, and five per cent cash discount for payment within seven days. Eighteen times three hundred is five thousand four hundred. Trade discount is five hundred and forty. The invoice total is four thousand eight hundred and sixty. Seven days from the first of March means paying by the eighth. The third of March is in time, so cash discount is earned. Cash discount is five per cent of four thousand eight hundred and sixty, which is two hundred and forty-three. The amount paid is four thousand six hundred and seventeen. On the ninth, it would be the full four thousand eight hundred and sixty. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is working out cash discount on the list price. It comes off the net amount, after trade discount. Another is recording the amount after cash discount in the day book. The day book records the net invoice amount. Cash discount is recorded only when payment is made.

Answer. Forty times twenty-five is one thousand. Trade discount is two hundred, so the amount recorded is eight hundred dollars. Ten days from the second of June is the twelfth.
