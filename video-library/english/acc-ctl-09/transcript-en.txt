Idea. Your class counts the money for a school trip and it is ten dollars short. Until you find out why, you write the ten dollars on a note marked unexplained. A suspense account is that note. When a trial balance does not agree, the difference goes into a suspense account. It goes on the side that is short. Here the debit total is two hundred short, so suspense is a debit of two hundred.

Method. Step one: find the difference between the two totals, and enter it on the smaller side as balance brought down. Step two: ignore errors that did not affect the trial balance, such as omission, commission and principle. They never touch suspense. Step three: write the correcting journal and take its suspense leg. If the journal credits suspense, the entry goes on the credit side, with the other account's name as the detail. Step four: total both sides. When every error is corrected, the totals agree and nothing is left.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2019. Yasin's trial balance does not balance. We must prepare the suspense account. Six marks. The totals are debit forty-one thousand nine hundred and seventy, and credit forty-two thousand five hundred and fifty. Six errors were found. Debit is short by five hundred and eighty, so suspense opens with a debit balance of five hundred and eighty. Error one: the sales day book was overcast by two hundred, so the journal debits sales and credits suspense. Error three: a payment to Raul was not posted to his account. Debit Raul, credit suspense, four hundred and fifty. Error four: discount received of eighty was debited to discount allowed. Both accounts need a credit of eighty, so suspense gets two debits of eighty. Error six: rent of four hundred and thirty was posted as three hundred and forty. Rent needs ninety more: debit rent, credit suspense ninety. Both sides now total seven hundred and forty. The account has cleared. Common mistake. A common mistake is opening suspense on the larger side. It always goes on the side that is short. Another is putting in errors that did not affect the trial balance, such as the motor repairs principle error.
