Idea. A juice stall owner wants to know if this year was better than last. Profit alone does not tell her. Profitability means how well a business turns its sales, and the money invested in it, into profit. Gross profit percentage changes with selling prices and the cost of the goods. Profit for the year as a percentage of revenue also shows how well expenses are controlled. Return on capital employed shows how well the capital earns profit.

Method. For each ratio, say whether it rose or fell, quoting the figures. Give a likely reason. Then weigh the ratios against each other, and finish with a clear judgement. Here is a useful pattern. Gross profit percentage stays at thirty per cent, but profit for the year falls from twelve to eight per cent of revenue. So expenses have grown faster than sales.

Worked example. Here is a real question from January 2023. We must evaluate the change in a business's profitability from 2021 to 2022, using two ratios. Five marks. Read the table. Profit for the year as a percentage of revenue rose from fifteen to eighteen per cent. Return on capital employed fell from seven to five per cent. When ratios move in opposite directions, the answer must weigh them up. Profit as a percentage of revenue rose by three percentage points. Each dollar of sales now leaves more profit, which suggests expenses are better controlled. Return on capital employed fell by two percentage points. The capital in the business is earning less profit, so resources are used less efficiently. Perhaps more capital was put in. Now weigh them. Return on capital employed is the overall return on the owner's money, and it fell. So, overall, profitability worsened. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is only describing the numbers. Always add a reason. Another is bringing in liquidity. The current ratio is about paying debts, not about profit.
