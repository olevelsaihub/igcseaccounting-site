Idea. If you write the wrong mark in your homework diary, a good teacher does not scribble it out. They add a clear correction. Accountants do the same: they never rub out an error. The correction is written in the journal. The account to debit comes first. The account to credit goes on the line below, with each amount in its own column.

Method. For each error, write what was done. Then write what should have been done. The correction is only the difference between the two. If the error left the debits equal to the credits, correct it between two ordinary accounts. If only one account is wrong, the other leg is the suspense account. Check the amount. For a wrong figure, use the difference. For a complete reversal or a wrong-side posting, use double the amount: once to cancel, once to put it right.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2025. Brandon's trial balance does not balance. Prepare journal entries to correct five errors. Ten marks. Here are the five errors. Read each one and ask: what was done, and what should have been done? Error one. Motor expenses was undercast by two hundred, so its debit is too low and nothing else is wrong. Debit motor expenses two hundred, credit suspense. Error two. A motor vehicle costing two thousand was put in motor expenses. Move it: debit motor vehicles, credit motor expenses. Both sides were posted, so no suspense. Error three. A receipt from Palmer was credited to Parker. Put Parker's account back with a debit of sixty, and credit Palmer sixty. Error four. Wages of one hundred and fifty-four were posted as one hundred and forty-five. Wages is nine short, on one side only. Debit wages nine, credit suspense. Error five. Stationery of forty was debited to bank and credited to stationery, the reverse. Use double: debit stationery eighty, credit bank eighty. Common mistake. A common mistake is correcting a complete reversal with the single amount. That only cancels the error. You need double. Another is using suspense for an error that kept the totals equal, such as an error of principle.

Your turn. Now you try. Write the journal entries to correct these three errors. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. One: both are expenses, so move it: debit insurance, credit rent, three hundred. Two: a complete reversal, so double: debit cash, credit sales, one hundred and fifty. Three: purchases is fifty too high on the debit side, so debit suspense fifty and credit purchases fifty. Remember: what was done, what should have been done, then post the difference, or double for a reversal. Now try the practice questions.
