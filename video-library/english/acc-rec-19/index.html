Normal balances: debit side or credit side

Idea. Think of a school tuck shop. Its fridge, its stock and its cash are things it has. A loan from the school is something it owes. Things a business has normally end with a debit balance. Things it owes normally end with a credit balance. Debit balances are assets, expenses, drawings and returns inwards. Credit balances are liabilities, equity, income, returns outwards, and provisions, such as the provision for depreciation. The bank account can be on either side. A debit balance means money in the bank. A credit balance means an overdraft: money owed to the bank. Cash can only have a debit balance, because you cannot hold less than no cash.

Method. First, classify the account: asset, liability, equity, income, expense, drawings, a return, or a provision. Then apply the rule, and tick exactly one column. Then check the traps. Carriage inwards and carriage outwards are both expenses, so both are debits. Discount allowed is an expense, a debit. Discount received is income, a credit. A provision is a credit, even though it relates to an asset.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2023. Tick the side of the trial balance for each of five accounts. Five marks, one for each row. Carriage inwards is the cost of bringing goods in. Carriage outwards is the cost of delivering goods to customers. Both are expenses, so both are debits. Equity is what the business owes the owner, so it is a credit. Other receivables, such as rent paid in advance, are owed to the business: an asset, so debit. Other payables, such as wages still owed, are owed by the business: a liability, so credit. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is putting carriage outwards on the credit side because it sounds like out. It is still an expense, so it is a debit. Another is reading a credit bank balance as money in the bank. A credit bank balance is an overdraft.

Your turn. Now you try. Jamal runs a phone repair shop. Decide whether each of his five balances is a debit or a credit. Pause and decide.

Answer. Returns outwards are goods sent back to suppliers: credit. A bank overdraft is a liability: credit. Drawings: debit. A provision for depreciation: credit. Rent received is income: credit. Remember: classify the account, apply the rule, and check the traps: carriages, discounts, provisions and the bank. Now try the practice questions.
