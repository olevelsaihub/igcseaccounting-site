Idea. Some suppliers say: pay your bill early, and you can take a little off. That reduction is a cash discount. We give it to customers who pay us early, and suppliers give it to us when we pay them early. A three-column cash book has a discount, a cash and a bank column on each side. When Lee pays us early, the discount allowed sits on the debit side, next to the money received. When we pay Ola early, the discount received sits on the credit side. The discount columns are only a memo. They are not part of the double entry inside the cash book, so they are just totalled at the end of the period, never balanced.

Method. Step one: read which side of the cash book the question names. The debit side holds discount allowed. The credit side holds discount received. Step two: post the total to the same side of the matching nominal account. The debit total is debited to discount allowed. The credit total is credited to discount received. Step three: if a figure is asked, read off the one total that matches the discount named. Never add the two totals, or take one from the other.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. Where are the total discounts on the credit side of a cash book posted? One mark. The credit side is where payments are recorded. We pay suppliers, and when we pay early, they give us discount. So the credit-side column is discount received. The column is only a memo, so its total goes to the nominal ledger on the same side: the credit side. So we credit the discount received account. Option A has the right side but the wrong discount. D has the right discount but the wrong side. C is wrong on both. The answer is B. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is reversing the side: debiting discount received. The credit-side total is credited. Another is treating the discount columns as money. Discount is never added to the cash or bank balance.
