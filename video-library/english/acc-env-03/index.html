Idea. Imagine every football referee used different rules. Nobody could compare one match with another. Accountants agree on shared rules too, so accounts can be trusted and compared. These rules are called accounting concepts. You need six, plus going concern: accruals, business entity, consistency, materiality, money measurement, and prudence.

Method. Learn one key idea for each. Accruals: record income and expenses in the period they belong to. Business entity: the business is separate from its owner. Consistency: use the same method every year. Materiality: very small items, like a stapler, can simply be treated as an expense. Money measurement: record only what has a money value. Prudence: do not overstate profit or assets. Going concern: the business will keep trading. In a question, find the key idea in the statement, match it to one concept, and write the name exactly. If a description is asked, explain it in your own short sentence.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2025. It gives three descriptions, and we must name the accounting concept for each. Three marks. Row one says low-value items do not need separate recording. The key idea is too small to matter. That is materiality. Row two says only items that can be measured in money are recorded. The key idea is a money value. That is money measurement. Row three says the business is completely separate from its owner. That is business entity. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is mixing up business entity and going concern. Business entity means the owner is kept separate. Going concern means the business keeps trading. Another is describing a concept with its own name, like consistency means being consistent. Say what it actually does.

Your turn. Now you try. Read the three statements, and name the accounting concept for each. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. One: the holiday is kept out of the shop's books, so the owner is separate. That is business entity. Two: the same method every year is consistency. Three: the cautious figure is prudence.
