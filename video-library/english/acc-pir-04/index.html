Idea. Partners make a profit together. But who gets what? They follow their partnership agreement: first some extras for each partner, then the rest is split in an agreed ratio. The appropriation account shows exactly how the profit is shared. Start with profit for the year. Add interest on drawings, because partners pay it. Take off interest on capital and salaries. What is left, the residual profit, is shared in the profit-sharing ratio.

Method. Work out each partner's figures. Interest on drawings is a percentage of total drawings, including goods taken. Interest on capital is a percentage of each capital. A monthly salary is multiplied by twelve. Share the residual profit in the ratio, and check the shares add back to it. Interest on a partner's loan is not in this account: it is an expense, taken off before profit for the year.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2023. Prepare Belen and Charo's appropriation account for the year ended thirty-first December 2022. Eight marks. They share profits equally. Interest on capital is five per cent of opening capital, interest on drawings is eight per cent of total drawings, and Charo gets two thousand dollars a month. Profit for the year was sixty-six thousand two hundred. Now look at the drawings. Charo also took goods worth two thousand for her own use. Goods taken count as drawings too. Belen drew twenty thousand. Charo drew thirty-six thousand in cash, plus two thousand in goods: thirty-eight thousand. At eight per cent, that is one thousand six hundred and three thousand and forty: four thousand six hundred and forty to add. Interest on capital: five per cent of eighty thousand is four thousand, and of ninety thousand is four thousand five hundred. Charo's salary is two thousand times twelve: twenty-four thousand. Residual profit: sixty-six thousand two hundred, plus four thousand six hundred and forty, minus eight thousand five hundred, minus twenty-four thousand, is thirty-eight thousand three hundred and forty. Shared equally, that is nineteen thousand one hundred and seventy each. Here is the finished account. Interest on drawings is added, interest on capital and the salary are taken off, and the shares add back to the residual. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is taking interest on drawings off. Partners pay it to the business, so it is added. Another is using the monthly salary as the yearly figure. Multiply by twelve.
