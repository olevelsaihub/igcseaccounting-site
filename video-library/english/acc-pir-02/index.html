Partnership agreements and what happens without one

Idea. Two friends start a car-wash business. One puts in more money. The other works more hours. How should they share the profit? Without rules agreed at the start, they may argue. A partnership agreement is a written document of the rules the partners have agreed. It usually covers the profit-sharing ratio, interest on capital and on drawings, partners' salaries, and interest on partners' loans. If there is no agreement, section twenty-four of the Partnership Act of eighteen ninety applies. Profits and losses are shared equally. There are no salaries, no interest on capital, and no interest on drawings. A partner's loan earns five per cent a year.

Method. For an advise question, make each point, then develop it: say what it leads to. Explain what happens without an agreement. Then end with a clear decision. The Act's rules can be unfair. Ali puts in thirty thousand and Bea ten thousand. Under the Act, a profit of twenty thousand is split ten thousand each, with no interest on Ali's extra capital.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2024. Haitai and Ming did not draw up a formal partnership agreement. Advise them whether they should. Five marks. First point: an agreement sets out what each partner is entitled to and responsible for. So if they disagree, they can check the document to settle it. Second point: written terms stop arguments starting. Fewer arguments means business decisions can be made more quickly. Third point: without an agreement, the Partnership Act of eighteen ninety applies. Profits are shared equally, with no salary or interest on capital, even if one partner works or invests more. Decision: I advise Haitai and Ming to draw up a formal partnership agreement. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is thinking the Act gives five per cent interest on capital. The five per cent is only on partners' loans. Another is thinking the Act shares profit in the ratio of capital. It shares profit equally.
