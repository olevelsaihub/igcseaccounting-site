Idea. Imagine a shop that lets a customer take goods today and pay next month. That is selling on credit. Until the customer pays, they owe the shop money. They are a trade receivable. Credit control means managing credit customers so that they pay in full, and on time. Selling on credit attracts more customers, so revenue and profit may rise. But cash comes in later, and some customers may never pay. Those irrecoverable debts reduce profit.

Method. To get customers to pay on time: check their credit history first, set credit limits, offer cash discount for early payment, send regular statements and reminders, charge interest on late payment, and stop supplying customers who do not pay. To judge a proposal, give one advantage and one disadvantage, each with its effect on customers, revenue, profit or cash. Relate them to the trader, then finish with a clear decision.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2022, Paper 1. Martina only sells for cash and wants to offer credit terms. Evaluate whether she should proceed. Five marks. The advantage. Credit may attract new customers who prefer to pay later. So Martina's revenue and profit could increase. The disadvantage. Some customers may not pay, so there is a risk of irrecoverable debts. Writing them off reduces profit, and cash also arrives later. Decision: Martina should go ahead, but check customers' credit records and set credit limits to keep the risk low. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is giving only one side, or forgetting the decision. An evaluation needs both sides and a clear conclusion. Another is offering trade discount to speed up payment. Trade discount comes off the list price. It is cash discount that rewards paying early.

Your turn. Now you try. Ravi sells building supplies on credit, but some builders never pay. He wants to sell for cash only. Should he? Pause and decide.

Answer. The advantage of cash only: no more irrecoverable debts, so profit is no longer reduced by write-offs. Cash also arrives at once. The disadvantage: builders expect credit, so many may buy from a rival instead. Ravi's revenue and profit could fall. Decision: keep selling on credit, but tighten credit control with credit limits, credit checks, and no more goods for builders who do not pay. Either decision is fine if it follows from your points. Remember: an advantage with its effect, a disadvantage with its effect, then a decision. Now try the practice questions.
