Preparing a bank reconciliation statement

Idea. You write a cheque to a friend on Friday. Your notebook already shows the money gone. But the bank only takes it out when your friend pays it in. For a few days, the two records differ. A bank reconciliation statement explains these timing differences. There are two: unpresented cheques, which the bank has not paid yet, and outstanding lodgements, which the bank has not added yet.

Method. Start from the updated cash book balance. Add unpresented cheques, because the bank has not taken that money out yet. Deduct outstanding lodgements, because the bank has not added them yet. An overdraft is a negative balance, so write it in brackets and use the same rules. An overdraft of five hundred, plus an unpresented cheque of three hundred, minus an outstanding lodgement of one hundred, gives an overdraft of three hundred. The result must equal the bank statement balance in the question. If you start from the bank statement instead, the signs reverse.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. It follows on from Nabil's updated cash book. Prepare a bank reconciliation statement at thirty April. Four marks. The bank statement shows a credit balance of three hundred and eighty-six. That is our target. Fatima's cheque of two hundred and forty-eight, and cash sales of eight hundred and ninety banked, are the timing differences. Start with the updated cash book balance of one thousand and twenty-eight. Fatima's cheque has not been paid by the bank yet, so add two hundred and forty-eight. The cash sales of eight hundred and ninety are not on the statement yet, so deduct them. One thousand and twenty-eight plus two hundred and forty-eight, minus eight hundred and ninety, is three hundred and eighty-six. It matches the bank statement. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is swapping the signs when starting from the cash book. Unpresented cheques are added, outstanding lodgements deducted. Another is putting standing orders or bank charges in the statement. They go in the cash book first.
