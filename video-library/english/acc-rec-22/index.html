Is it capital or revenue expenditure?

Read the narration

Idea. When you buy a bicycle, you pay its price, and maybe a delivery charge to get it home. Later you pay to repair a puncture and to insure it. The first costs get you the bike. The later costs keep it running. Businesses sort costs in the same way. Capital expenditure buys a non-current asset, gets it ready for use, or improves it. Revenue expenditure runs the business and keeps assets working.

Method. For each item, ask: does it buy, prepare or improve a non-current asset? If yes, it is capital. If it is a cost of using, running or repairing, it is revenue. Tick one column only. Watch for pairs. The first sign on a new van is capital, but repainting it later is revenue. Legal fees on buying premises are capital, but loan interest is revenue. An extension is capital, a repair is revenue. If totals are asked, list the capital items and add them, then do the same for revenue. Show your working.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. Tick whether each item is capital or revenue expenditure, when buying a motor vehicle. Five marks, one for each row. The cost of the vehicle buys a non-current asset, so it is capital. The delivery cost gets the vehicle to the business, ready to use, so it is capital too. The extended warranty pays for repair cover in future years, while the vehicle is being used. That is a running cost, so it is revenue. Fuel is used up running the vehicle, and insurance is a regular running cost. Both are revenue. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is treating delivery or installation as revenue. These costs get a new asset ready for use, so they are capital. Another is treating insurance or fuel as capital because it was paid on the same day as the van. The date does not matter. They are running costs.

Your turn. Now you try. Lee is opening a café. Decide whether each cost is capital or revenue, then total each group. Pause and decide.

Answer. The machine, its installation and the new shelving are capital. The repair, the loan interest and the coffee beans are revenue. Capital: three thousand plus two hundred plus four hundred, three thousand six hundred dollars. Revenue: eighty plus one hundred and fifty plus five hundred, seven hundred and thirty dollars. Remember: if it buys, prepares or improves a non-current asset, it is capital. If it runs or repairs, it is revenue. Tick one column, then total each group. Now try the practice questions.
