Idea. A teacher collecting money for a school trip keeps a list of what each student still owes. She also keeps one running total on a separate sheet. If the list adds up to the total, she knows the list is right. A control account does this in accounting. It is a summary account that holds the totals of all the customer accounts, or all the supplier accounts, in one place. It is written up from totals in the books of prime entry, such as the sales day book and the cash book, not from the customer accounts themselves. That makes it an independent check.

Method. First, learn the main benefits. It checks the arithmetic of the ledger. It gives a ready total for the financial statements. It deters fraud. And it helps find errors faster. Next, develop each benefit with because, or so that. For fraud, say why: a different person keeps the control account, so a false entry would make the totals differ. Finally, end with a judgement or advice addressed to the named trader.

Worked example. Here is a real question from January 2023. Beth thinks her control account takes too long. Explain to Beth the benefits of keeping it. Five marks. Benefit one: it checks the arithmetic of the receivables ledger. Its balance is compared with the total of all the customer balances. If they agree, Beth knows the ledger was added up correctly. Benefit two: it discourages fraud. Someone other than the ledger clerk keeps it, so a dishonest change to a customer's account would cause a difference that gets noticed. Conclusion: the benefits are worth the time, so Beth should keep the control account. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is describing what goes into a control account. The question asks why it is kept. Another is saying it proves every entry is correct. It cannot reveal a posting to the wrong customer's account.

Your turn. Now you try. Explain one benefit to Sami, with a reason, and give him your advice. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. One good answer: it gives a ready total of trade payables, so Sami can prepare his trial balance and statement of financial position quickly, without adding up three hundred accounts. So he should keep it. Remember: name the benefit, develop it with because, and finish with advice. Now try the practice questions.
