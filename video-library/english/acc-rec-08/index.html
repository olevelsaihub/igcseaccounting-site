Why the petty cash in the box differs from the book

Idea. Many businesses keep a little cash in a box for small payments, such as stamps or a taxi fare. Each payment has a voucher and is written in the petty cash book. The balance in the petty cash book should equal the cash counted in the box. Here the book says forty-six dollars, but the box holds only forty. Six dollars is missing. Why?

Method. First, think of a recording error. A voucher was entered at the wrong amount, or not entered at all. Or a voucher was lost, so a payment was never recorded. Second, think of loss or theft. Cash may have been dropped, lost, or taken from the box. Third, think of timing. The top-up from the main cashier may be in the book but not yet in the box. Or cash was paid out before the voucher was written. Write each cause as its own clear sentence.

Worked example. Here is a real question from January 2023, Paper 1. State two reasons why the balance in the petty cash book may be different to the amount in the petty cash box. Two marks. Cause one, a recording problem. A petty cash voucher has been lost, so a payment was never entered in the book. Cause two, a loss. Cash has been stolen from the petty cash box. Check: one recording cause and one loss cause, two different ideas. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is giving two versions of the same cause. An error in the book and a wrong entry in the book are one idea. Another is giving a benefit of keeping a petty cash book, or writing just a mistake. Give a cause, and say where it happened.

Your turn. Now you try. Zara's petty cash book shows sixty-five dollars, but the box holds fifty-two. Work out the difference and give three different reasons. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Sixty-five minus fifty-two is thirteen. So thirteen dollars is missing from the box. Now one reason from each group. A thirteen-dollar taxi voucher was never entered. Cash was lost or stolen. Or cash was paid out before the voucher was written. Remember: when the book and the box differ, think of recording, loss and timing, and write each cause as its own sentence.
