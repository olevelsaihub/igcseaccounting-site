Why businesses charge depreciation

Idea. Say you buy a laptop for six hundred dollars, and you will use it for three years. It is fairer to count two hundred dollars of its cost in each year you use it, rather than all six hundred in the first year. That is the purpose of depreciation. It spreads the cost of a non-current asset over the years of its useful life. Each year's share is an expense in the income statement, so it reduces profit. Be careful. Depreciation does not save cash to replace the asset, and it does not show what the asset could be sold for.

Method. To explain why we charge it, name a concept. First, accruals, also called matching. The asset helps earn revenue for several years, so its cost is charged across those same years. Second, prudence. Without depreciation, profit would be shown too high, and so would the value of the assets. Depreciation stops them being overstated. Depreciation affects both statements. Each year's charge reduces profit. In the statement of financial position, the asset is shown at its carrying value: cost minus all the depreciation so far. After two years, the laptop's carrying value is two hundred dollars.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. Explain, referring to an accounting concept, one reason why a business must account for depreciation. Two marks. First, name the concept. We will choose the accruals concept, also called matching. Then apply it. A non-current asset is used to earn revenue over several years, so depreciation charges part of its cost in each of those years. That matches the cost of using the asset with the revenue it helps to earn. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying depreciation provides money to buy a replacement. No cash is set aside. It is only a book entry. Another is naming the concept but not saying how depreciation applies it. That loses the second mark.

Your turn. Now you try. Ravi does not want to charge depreciation on his new van. Explain, using prudence, why he should. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Prudence says profit and assets must not be overstated. Without depreciation, Ravi's profit for the year, and the van's value in the statement of financial position, would both be shown too high. Remember: name the concept, accruals or prudence, then say what depreciation does under it. Now try the practice questions.
