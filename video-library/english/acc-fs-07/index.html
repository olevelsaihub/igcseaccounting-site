Idea. Two shops each made ten thousand dollars profit. Shop A sold fifty thousand dollars of goods, Shop B two hundred thousand. Shop A kept twenty cents of every dollar of sales, Shop B only five. Ratios compare figures so we can judge performance. There are two families. Profitability ratios show how well a business makes profit, and are written as percentages. Liquidity ratios show whether it can pay short-term debts, and are written as a number to one.

Method. Gross profit percentage is gross profit divided by revenue, times one hundred. Profit for the year as a percentage of revenue uses profit for the year on top. Return on capital employed is profit for the year divided by capital employed, times one hundred. The current ratio is current assets divided by current liabilities. The liquid ratio takes inventory away from current assets first, then divides by current liabilities. Write both as a number to one. Pick the figures with care. Use trade receivables after the provision. Leave out long-term loans: they are not current liabilities. Then round to the decimal places asked.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. Calculate Bob's current ratio and liquid ratio, stating the formula used, to two decimal places. Four marks. Here is the information Bob gave, as a table. We need the inventory, receivables, cash, payables and the allowance for doubtful debts. The bank loan is repayable in 2028. First, total the current assets. Take the allowance of three hundred and forty-six off trade receivables. The total is nineteen thousand seven hundred and sixty-two. Current liabilities are trade payables, four thousand three hundred and twenty, plus other payables, two hundred and eleven: four thousand five hundred and thirty-one. The bank loan is repayable in 2028, so it is left out. Current ratio: nineteen thousand seven hundred and sixty-two divided by four thousand five hundred and thirty-one is four point three six one five. To two decimal places, four point three six to one. Liquid ratio: take inventory away, leaving eleven thousand two hundred and thirty-one. Divide by four thousand five hundred and thirty-one: two point four eight to one. Common mistake. A common mistake is leaving inventory in for the liquid ratio. Then it is just the current ratio again. Another is using the wrong form. Liquidity ratios are written as a number to one. Percentages are for profitability ratios.

Return on capital employed: twelve thousand divided by sixty thousand, times one hundred, is twenty per cent. Current assets are eighteen thousand, so the current ratio is three to one. Without inventory, nine thousand is left, so the liquid ratio is one point five to one. Remember: write the formula, pick the right figures, then give the answer in the right form.
