Partners' current and capital accounts

Idea. A partner earns from the business during the year: interest, perhaps a salary, and a share of the profit. The partner also takes money out: drawings. A current account keeps track of both. Each partner has two accounts. The capital account holds the money invested, and usually stays the same. The current account records what the partner earns and takes, and changes every year.

Method. What the partner earns goes on the credit side: interest on capital, salary, share of profit, and loan interest. What the partner takes goes on the debit side: drawings and interest on drawings. Take the figures from the appropriation account. Post each to its side. Then balance: the balance carried down goes on the smaller side, and is brought down on the other side, the next day.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2022. Prepare Ming's current account for the year ended thirty-first March 2022, balance it, and bring the balance down. Eight marks. Ming's capital is fifty thousand, the current account starts at nine hundred credit, and drawings were twenty-six thousand. Interest on capital is eight per cent, interest on drawings nine per cent, and Ming's salary is eighteen thousand. Interest on drawings is nine per cent of twenty-six thousand: two thousand three hundred and forty. Interest on capital is eight per cent of fifty thousand: four thousand. Ming's share of profit, from the corrected profit, is eight thousand four hundred and forty-five. The credit side: the opening balance of nine hundred, then interest on capital, salary and share of profit. These are what Ming earned. The debit side: drawings of twenty-six thousand, and interest on drawings of two thousand three hundred and forty. These are what Ming took. Credits total thirty-one thousand three hundred and forty-five. Debits are twenty-eight thousand three hundred and forty. The difference, three thousand and five, is the balance carried down, on the debit side. Bring it down on the credit side on the first of April 2022. The business owes Ming three thousand and five dollars. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is putting drawings on the credit side. The partner takes them, so they are a debit. Another is misreading a debit balance. It does not mean a profit. It means the partner took out more than they earned.
