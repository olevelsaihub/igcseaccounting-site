Idea. When you shop online, you get an order confirmation, a bill, and a receipt. Businesses that trade on credit send each other documents like these too. To name a document, ask three questions. Who sends it? What does it show? When is it sent?

Method. First, the buyer sends a purchase order asking for goods. Nothing is recorded yet, because no sale has happened. Then the seller sends a sales invoice, showing the goods, the price, any trade discount, and the amount owed. If goods come back, the seller sends a credit note, which reduces what the customer owes. At the end of the month, the seller sends a statement of account: invoices, returns, payments, and the balance still owed. The customer sends a remittance advice with a payment, showing which invoices are being paid. The bank sends a bank statement, listing every receipt and payment, including interest and charges. Then write the document's standard name, or one purpose that fits only that document.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2025. Which business document shows the sales returns and payments made during a period? The choices are credit note, debit note, sales invoice and statement of account. One mark. Test each option. A credit note shows one return only. A debit note is raised by the buyer to ask for a credit, and lists no payments. A sales invoice shows one sale only. A statement of account covers a whole period: invoices, returns and payments, ending with the balance due. So the answer is D. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is confusing a sales invoice with a statement of account. An invoice is for one sale. A statement sums up the whole period. Another is saying a credit note is sent by the customer. It is sent by the seller.

Your turn. Now you try. Leo pays his supplier and sends a document listing the invoices he is paying. Name it. Then name the document Leo sent before the goods arrived, asking for them. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Part a: it is sent by the customer, with the payment, and shows which invoices are paid. So it is a remittance advice. Part b: the buyer, Leo, sends it before the sale, asking for goods. So it is a purchase order. Nothing is entered in the books yet, because no sale has happened. Remember: who sends it, what it shows, and when. Now try the practice questions.
