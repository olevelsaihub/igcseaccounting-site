Idea. Imagine keeping every receipt in a shoebox. To know how you are doing, you would have to sort the whole box. A tidy notebook, written up every day, answers the question at once. A full set of accounting records means every transaction is recorded by double entry, in the books of prime entry and the ledgers. Incomplete records keep only some of this. Full records bring many benefits. Statements can be prepared at any time. The owner makes better decisions. Customers and suppliers can be controlled. A trial balance can be drawn up, and banks trust the figures.

Method. First, give the number of reasons asked for, usually two. State each reason clearly, in one phrase. Then develop each reason with because, or so that, to show its effect. The point earns one mark. The development earns the second.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. Thelma, a sole trader, kept only incomplete records. Discuss two reasons why a business should maintain full accounting records. Four marks. First reason: with full records, Thelma could produce financial statements whenever she needs them, even every month. Because the figures are already in the books, nothing has to be pieced together. Second reason: full records help the owner make better decisions, since she is working from complete, current figures, not guesses. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is listing reasons without developing any. A list earns only the identification marks. Another is writing the same reason twice in different words.
