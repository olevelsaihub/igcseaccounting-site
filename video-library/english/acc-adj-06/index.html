Disposal of non-current assets: cost, disposal and profit or loss on sale

Idea. A business sells its old van. It rarely gets exactly the van's carrying value, the value left in the books. The difference is a profit or loss on disposal. Say the van cost ten thousand dollars, and six thousand dollars of depreciation has been charged so far. Its carrying value is four thousand. If it sells for five thousand, that is a profit of one thousand dollars. In the ledger, we use a disposal account. The van's cost goes on the debit side. Its depreciation and the money received go on the credit side. The gap is the profit or loss.

Method. First, find the cost, the dates and the proceeds. Then work out the depreciation on that asset up to the sale, following the time policy. Balance the disposal account. If the credits are bigger, the gap goes on the debit side: a profit. If the debits are bigger, it goes on the credit side: a loss. Either way, the detail is income statement.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2023. A vehicle bought in 2020 for eighteen thousand dollars is sold in March 2022 for twelve thousand. A new one costs twenty-one thousand five hundred. Depreciation is twenty-five per cent reducing balance: a full year in the year of purchase, none in the year of disposal. We must prepare the motor vehicles cost account and the disposal account for 2022. Nine marks. In 2020, twenty-five per cent of eighteen thousand is four thousand five hundred. In 2021, twenty-five per cent of thirteen thousand five hundred is three thousand three hundred and seventy-five. In 2022, none. That is seven thousand eight hundred and seventy-five to date. So the carrying value is ten thousand one hundred and twenty-five. Twelve thousand minus that is a profit of one thousand eight hundred and seventy-five. Now the cost account. The opening balance and the new vehicle are debits. The cost of the vehicle sold, eighteen thousand, leaves on the credit side, to disposal. The debits total ninety-eight thousand one hundred. Take away eighteen thousand: the balance is eighty thousand one hundred, brought down on 1 January 2023. In the disposal account, the cost of eighteen thousand is a debit. The depreciation and the twelve thousand from the bank are credits. The credits are bigger, so the gap, one thousand eight hundred and seventy-five, is a profit, on the debit side. Common mistake. A common mistake is putting a profit on the credit side. In the disposal account, a profit is the debit balancing figure. Another is entering the carrying value as one figure. Enter the cost and the depreciation separately.
