Calculating the provision for irrecoverable debts

Idea. A business is owed money by many credit customers. From experience, it knows a small part of it will probably never be collected. So it keeps a provision for irrecoverable debts: an estimate of that loss. Say customers owe ten thousand dollars, and the business uses a rate of five per cent. The provision is five per cent of ten thousand: five hundred dollars. Each year the provision is worked out again. Only the change goes to the income statement. If it grows from five hundred to six hundred and fifty dollars, the increase of one hundred and fifty is an expense.

Method. Here is the method. First, take any debt to be written off away from trade receivables. Multiply what is left by the rate: that is the new provision. Then compare it with the old provision. Finally, say clearly which way it moved. If the new provision is bigger, it is an increase, which reduces profit. If it is smaller, it is a decrease, which is added to profit.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. We must calculate the change in Shahid's provision, and say clearly whether it is an increase or a decrease. Three marks. The provision is four per cent. Last year it was one thousand two hundred and five dollars. Trade receivables are twenty-eight thousand seven hundred and fifty, but that includes a debt of four hundred and fifty that will never be paid. Step one: take the irrecoverable debt away. Twenty-eight thousand seven hundred and fifty minus four hundred and fifty is twenty-eight thousand three hundred. Step two: four per cent of twenty-eight thousand three hundred is one thousand one hundred and thirty-two. That is the new provision. Step three: the old provision was one thousand two hundred and five. The new one is smaller, so the provision has decreased by seventy-three dollars. Common mistake. A common mistake is using the receivables before the write-off. That gives one thousand one hundred and fifty, and the wrong change. Another is giving the new provision when the question asks for the change, or leaving out the word increase or decrease.

Your turn. Now you try. Priya's provision is three per cent of trade receivables. Calculate the change and say which way it moved. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Forty-six thousand minus one thousand is forty-five thousand. Three per cent of that is one thousand three hundred and fifty. That is bigger than one thousand two hundred, so the provision has increased by one hundred and fifty dollars. Remember: take off the write-off, apply the rate, then compare with the old provision and name the direction. Now try the practice questions.
