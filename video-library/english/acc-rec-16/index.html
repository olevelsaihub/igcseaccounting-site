Idea. Imagine you buy a bike for two hundred dollars. You pay one hundred and fifty from your own savings and borrow fifty from your sister. The bike's value equals what you borrowed plus what you put in. A business is the same. What it owns are assets. What it owes are liabilities. What the owner has put in is equity, also called capital. So assets equal liabilities plus equity.

Method. Write the equation down first. To find a missing figure, rearrange it so the unknown is on its own. Liabilities equal assets minus equity. Equity equals assets minus liabilities. But equity minus liabilities never equals assets. Then put in the two figures you know and calculate. Always check that the equation still balances. Every transaction has a dual effect: two items change, and the equation still balances. Buy a machine on credit for five thousand dollars. Assets go up five thousand, and liabilities go up five thousand.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2024. Complete the table: there is one missing figure in each row. Three marks. Here is the table, in dollars, with a question mark where each figure is missing. Row one gives liabilities of twenty thousand and equity of sixty thousand. Assets equal liabilities plus equity: eighty thousand dollars. Row two gives assets of one hundred and fifty thousand and equity of ninety-five thousand. Liabilities equal assets minus equity: fifty-five thousand dollars. Row three gives assets of eighty thousand and liabilities of eighteen thousand. Equity equals assets minus liabilities: sixty-two thousand dollars. Check each row: assets equal liabilities plus equity every time. That is one mark for each missing figure. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is adding when you should subtract. To find liabilities, take equity away from assets. Another is giving only one effect of a transaction. Two items always change.

Your turn. Now you try. Tariq's business has assets of sixty-four thousand dollars and liabilities of twenty-two thousand. Find his equity, then the effect of paying a supplier. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Equity equals assets minus liabilities: sixty-four thousand minus twenty-two thousand is forty-two thousand dollars. Paying the supplier reduces the bank, an asset, by three thousand. It also reduces trade payables, a liability, by three thousand. Sixty-one thousand equals nineteen thousand plus forty-two thousand. Remember: write the equation, rearrange for the unknown, and for a transaction name two effects and check it still balances. Now try the practice questions.
