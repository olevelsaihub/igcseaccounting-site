Idea. The provision for irrecoverable debts is an estimate of debts that may never be paid. It is kept in its own ledger account. Because it reduces an asset, trade receivables, it normally has a credit balance. Each year the provision is adjusted, and only the change goes to the income statement. An increase is credited. It is an expense. A decrease is debited. It adds to profit. Either way, the detail is income statement.

Method. Here is the method. Credit the opening balance. Work out the new provision: the rate times trade receivables after write-offs. Post the change to the income statement. Then carry the new provision down on the debit side, total both sides, and bring it down on the credit side.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2023. We must prepare Xi's provision for irrecoverable debts account for the year ended 30 April 2023, balance it, and bring the balance down. Four marks. On 1 May 2022 the provision was seven hundred and forty-four dollars, on receivables of eighteen thousand six hundred. On 30 April 2023, receivables are twenty-one thousand five hundred, and a debt of seven hundred and fifty is written off. Seven hundred and forty-four divided by eighteen thousand six hundred is four per cent. Receivables after the write-off are twenty thousand seven hundred and fifty. Four per cent of that is eight hundred and thirty. That is up by eighty-six. Open the account. Last year's provision, seven hundred and forty-four, goes on the credit side as balance brought down, on 1 May 2022. The provision rises by eighty-six, so credit eighty-six on 30 April 2023, with the detail income statement. An increase is an expense. The new provision, eight hundred and thirty, is carried down on the debit side. Both sides now total eight hundred and thirty. Bring it down on the credit side on 1 May 2023. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is sending the whole new provision to the income statement. Only the change goes there. Another is putting a decrease on the credit side. A decrease is a debit. And never post a written-off debt into this account.

Your turn. Now you try. Ben's provision is three per cent of trade receivables. Prepare his provision account for 2025. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Twenty-five thousand five hundred minus five hundred is twenty-five thousand. Three per cent of that is seven hundred and fifty. That is smaller than nine hundred, so this time it is a decrease of one hundred and fifty. Credit the opening nine hundred. Debit the decrease of one hundred and fifty to the income statement, and the new provision of seven hundred and fifty as balance carried down. Both sides total nine hundred. Bring seven hundred and fifty down on the credit side. Remember: opening balance on the credit side, only the change to the income statement, then the new provision carried down and brought down.
