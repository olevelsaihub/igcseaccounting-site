Idea. Every balance in the books goes to one of two places. Income and expenses for the year go to the income statement. What the business owns or owes at the year end goes to the statement of financial position. Assets are split into non-current, kept and used for more than a year, and current, which turn into cash soon. Liabilities are current if due within twelve months, and non-current if due later.

Method. Ask three questions. Is it income or an expense for the year? Then it goes to the income statement. Is it owned? Used for over a year means non-current. Is it owed? Due within a year means current. Spending follows the same rule. Buying a van is capital expenditure: the van is an asset in the statement of financial position. Its fuel and repairs are revenue expenditure: expenses in the income statement. Two items catch students out. Accumulated depreciation reduces the asset, so it sits with non-current assets. The provision for irrecoverable debts reduces trade receivables, so it sits under current assets. Neither is a liability.

Worked example. Here is a real question from January 2020. Jeff's year ends on thirty-first December 2019. Tick where each of six items is shown in the statement of financial position. Six marks, one for each row. Accumulated depreciation reduces the machinery, so it goes under non-current assets. The bank loan is repayable in 2026, more than a year after the year end, so it is a non-current liability. Work in progress is goods partly made: it is part of inventory, so a current asset. A bank overdraft can be called in at any time, so it is a current liability. Other receivables, such as expenses paid in advance, are short-term amounts due to the business: current assets. The provision for irrecoverable debts comes off trade receivables, so it is under current assets too. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is calling the provision a liability. It is a deduction from trade receivables, so it belongs with current assets. Another is putting irrecoverable debts written off in the statement of financial position. A debt written off is an expense of the year, so it goes to the income statement.
