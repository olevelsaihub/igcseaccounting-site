Idea. A trader buys goods ready to sell, so its cost of sales uses purchases. A manufacturer makes its own goods, so it uses production cost from the manufacturing account instead. Only finished goods inventories go in the income statement. Raw materials and work in progress stay in the manufacturing account. Shared costs, such as rent, are split. Say rent is ten thousand, eighty per cent factory. Eight thousand goes to the manufacturing account, and the office share, two thousand, goes to the income statement.

Method. Write the heading and the revenue. Then build cost of sales: opening finished goods, plus production cost, plus any finished goods bought in, less closing finished goods. Revenue minus cost of sales is gross profit. Next, the expenses. Adjust each shared expense for any amount owing or prepaid first. Then take only the office share. Do the same for depreciation. Finally, total the administrative and selling expenses and subtract them from gross profit to reach profit for the year.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2025, Paper 2. Prepare Josh's income statement for the year ended thirty-first March 2025. Thirteen marks. Here is the table of Josh's balances, part one. And part two. From the data, use only the finished goods inventories: twenty thousand five hundred at the start and thirty-one thousand one hundred and ten at the end. Josh also bought finished goods for fourteen thousand. Cost of sales: twenty thousand five hundred, plus production cost of three hundred and forty-nine thousand eight hundred and eighty-two, plus fourteen thousand, less thirty-one thousand one hundred and ten. That is three hundred and fifty-three thousand two hundred and seventy-two. Revenue is five hundred and two thousand five hundred. Take away cost of sales: gross profit is one hundred and forty-nine thousand two hundred and twenty-eight. Now the shared costs, twenty per cent to the office. Electricity has one thousand four hundred owing, so add it first. Rent has eight thousand nine hundred prepaid, so take it off first. Depreciation is fifteen per cent reducing balance on the carrying value. Add administrative expenses, carriage outwards and administrative wages. The expenses total sixty-nine thousand two hundred and seventy-eight, so profit for the year is seventy-nine thousand nine hundred and fifty. Common mistake. A common mistake is using raw materials or work in progress inventories in cost of sales. Only finished goods inventories belong here. Another is taking twenty per cent of the rent before taking off the prepayment. Adjust first, then apportion.
