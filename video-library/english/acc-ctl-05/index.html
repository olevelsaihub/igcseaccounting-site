Which items go in a control account, and on which side

Idea. If a friend borrows money from you, they owe you more when they borrow again, and less when they pay you back. A trade receivables control account tracks this for all credit customers together. Customers owe us, so anything that raises the debt is a debit, and anything that lowers it is a credit. It mirrors a customer's own account. Trade payables control is the mirror image. We owe suppliers, so anything that raises what we owe is a credit, and anything that lowers it is a debit.

Method. First, which account: receivables or payables? Next, does the item involve a credit customer or supplier at all? If not, it is no entry. Then ask: does the debt go up or down? Watch for items that never go in. Cash sales and cash purchases create no debt. Provision for irrecoverable debts is not entered. And the other ledger's discounts and returns belong in the other account. Then recheck unusual items. A contra happens when a firm is both our customer and our supplier. We set the smaller amount off: a credit in receivables control and a debit in payables control.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2025. Tick where each item goes in a trade receivables control account: debit, credit, or no entry. Five marks. Cash sales: the customer pays at once, so there is no debt. No entry. A contra with payables cancels part of what a customer owes. The debt falls, so credit. Discount allowed lets a customer pay less. The debt falls: credit. Interest charged to a customer means they owe more: debit. Returns outwards are goods we send back to suppliers, so they belong in payables. No entry. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is putting a contra on the debit side of receivables control. It lowers what the customer owes, so it is a credit. Another is treating a dishonoured cheque as a receipt. The money never arrived, so the customer owes it again. It is a debit.

Your turn. Now you try. This time it is the trade payables control account. Debit, credit or no entry? Pause and decide.

Answer. Credit purchases raise what we owe: credit. Discount allowed is a customer item: no entry. Payments lower what we owe: debit. Interest charged by a supplier raises it: credit. Cash purchases create no debt: no entry. Remember: which account, does it belong, and does the debt go up or down. Now try the practice questions.
