Idea. Imagine you start a lemonade stall with fifty dollars of your own money. The stall now owes you fifty. If it makes a profit, it owes you more. If you take money out for yourself, it owes you less. The equity account keeps track of this. Here is an equity account. On the credit side: the opening capital, any extra capital the owner pays in, and the profit for the year. These increase what the business owes the owner. On the debit side: drawings, which is cash or goods the owner takes, and a loss for the year, if there is one. These reduce what the business owes the owner.

Method. Step one: remember that only four items go into equity. Capital paid in and profit for the year are credits. Drawings and a loss for the year are debits. Step two: cross out anything that is only a line inside the income statement, such as gross profit, or a profit or loss on disposal. Why? Profit for the year is the final figure at the bottom of the income statement. Gross profit is only part of the way down, before the expenses. Only the bottom line moves to equity.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2025. Which is shown in the equity account? One mark. Option A, gross profit, is only a line part-way down the income statement, before the expenses. It is not transferred to equity. Options B and D, a loss or a profit on disposal, are an expense and an income in the income statement. They reach equity only as part of the profit for the year. Option C, profit for the year, is the final result of the income statement, and it is credited to the owner's equity account. The answer is C. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is choosing gross profit. It is only part of the way down the income statement. Another is putting drawings or a loss on the credit side. They reduce equity, so they are debits.

Your turn. Now you try. Priya owns a gift shop. For each item, decide: debit, credit, or not in her equity account at all. Pause and decide.

Answer. The extra capital goes on the credit side. The goods she took are drawings, and with the loss for the year, they go on the debit side. Gross profit does not go into equity at all. Remember: credit capital and profit for the year, debit drawings and a loss, and leave everything else in the income statement. Now try the practice questions.
