Idea. A delivery business buys a van. It will use that van for years. Every week it also pays for fuel and the driver's wages, and those are used up straight away. These are two very different kinds of spending. Capital expenditure is money spent buying or improving non-current assets that the business will use for more than one year. It is shown in the statement of financial position. Revenue expenditure is money spent on the everyday running of the business. Its benefit is used up within the current year, so it is an expense in the income statement.

Method. To define either term, first say what the money is spent on. Then develop it: how long the benefit lasts, or which financial statement shows it. For a difference question, write both halves in one sentence, joined by whereas. Each half earns a mark. If an example is asked, give a specific item for each. A van or a machine for capital. Wages, fuel or repairs for revenue.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2025. Explain the term revenue expenditure. Two marks. First the point. Revenue expenditure is money spent on the everyday running costs of the business, such as wages, fuel and repairs. Then develop it. Its benefit is used up within the current year, so it is recorded as an expense in that year's income statement. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is defining capital expenditure as money the owner puts into the business. That is capital, which is something different. Another is saying revenue expenditure is money earned. Expenditure always means money spent.
