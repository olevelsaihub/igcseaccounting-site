Idea. A game is only fair if everyone plays by the same rules. Accountants also follow rules, called accounting concepts. When a question describes a treatment, your job is to name the rule behind it. Three concepts are about what goes in the books. The owner's private items are drawings: business entity. Cheap items such as a calculator are expenses, not assets: materiality. Staff skills have no reliable money value, so they are not shown: money measurement. Three more are about how figures are worked out. Using the same method every year: consistency. Valuing inventory at the lower of cost and net realisable value, or making a debt provision: prudence. Adjusting for amounts owing or paid in advance: accruals.

Method. Here is the method. Ask what the treatment is achieving. A private item? Business entity. A cheap item? Materiality. The same method? Consistency. Not overstating? Prudence. Owing or prepaid? Accruals. Then write the exact name. Depreciation links to accruals, prudence and consistency. Irrecoverable debts and provisions link to prudence and accruals. If a question asks for two concepts, give two different ones.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2022. We must identify the concept used in each of three scenarios. Three marks. Row one. The trader uses the same depreciation method each year. Same method every year means consistency. Row two. The trader adjusts for amounts paid in advance. That puts each expense in the right year: accruals, or matching. Row three. Stationery is recorded as an expense, not an asset, because it is cheap. Low value means materiality. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is choosing consistency for inventory valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value. That is prudence: it stops assets being overstated. Another is choosing materiality when staff skills are left off the statement of financial position. The reason is that skills have no reliable money value: money measurement.

Your turn. Now you try. Name the concept behind each of these three treatments. Pause and decide.

Answer. The chocolates are a private item: business entity. The stapler is cheap: materiality. The jackets are valued at the lower amount, so assets are not overstated: prudence. Remember: ask what the treatment achieves, match the clue, and write the exact concept name. Now try the practice questions.
