Idea. A shop that sells on credit may make dozens of sales a day. Instead of opening the ledger every time, it lists them in a day book, and posts the total once. There are four books: sales, purchases, sales returns, and purchases returns. They list only goods bought or sold on credit, and returns of them. Cash sales and cash purchases go in the cash book.

Method. Step one: pick out the credit items for each book. Step two: work out the list price less trade discount. Only the net amount is entered. For a return, use the trade discount of the original batch. Write the date, the name and the net amount on each line. At the month end, total the book with a date.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. George buys goods from three suppliers and returns some to White. We must prepare the purchases day book and the purchases returns day book. Five marks. Read the data carefully. White and Green give trade discount, but Black does not. The return on the twenty-eighth is from White's batch of April the tenth. April the tenth, White: one thousand dollars less twenty per cent. That is one thousand minus two hundred, eight hundred dollars. April the seventeenth, Black: seven hundred and fifty, with no trade discount. April the twenty-second, Green: three thousand less ten per cent, two thousand seven hundred. Total for the month: eight hundred, plus seven hundred and fifty, plus two thousand seven hundred, is four thousand two hundred and fifty. The return on the twenty-eighth came from the April tenth batch, so it gets twenty per cent off too. Two hundred less forty is one hundred and sixty, which is also the total. Common mistake. A common mistake is entering a return at list price. Take off the same trade discount as the original goods. Another is putting cash sales or cash purchases in a day book. They go in the cash book.
