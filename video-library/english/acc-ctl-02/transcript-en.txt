Idea. Picture a pair of scales. Every transaction is recorded twice: once as a debit and once as a credit, for the same amount. So, on any date, all the debit balances should weigh exactly the same as all the credit balances. A trial balance is a list of every ledger balance on one date, in two columns: debit and credit. Here, bank and equipment are debits, capital is a credit, and both totals are five thousand dollars. To choose the column, remember: assets, expenses and drawings are debits. Liabilities, equity, income and provisions are credits.

Method. Go down the list one account at a time. Decide its side, then copy the amount into that column without changing it. Add each column, and write both totals on the same line. Take extra care with the tricky items. A bank overdraft is a credit, because the business owes the bank. Provisions are credits. Returns inwards is a debit, but returns outwards is a credit. Receivables control is a debit, payables control a credit. If the totals differ and the question tells you to, add a suspense account on the smaller side. Here the credit side is four hundred short, so suspense goes in the credit column.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. Vaughn gives eighteen balances at 30 April 2024. We must prepare the trial balance. Eleven marks. Advertising is an expense, so debit. The bank loan is a liability, so credit. The bank overdraft is money owed to the bank, so credit too. Carriage inwards and drawings are debits. Equity is a credit. General expenses, opening inventory, machinery at cost and premises are debits. The provision for depreciation is a credit, and so is the mortgage, a long-term loan. Purchases, returns inwards and wages are debits. Revenue is a credit. Trade payables control is what Vaughn owes, a credit. Trade receivables control is what customers owe him, a debit. Now add each column. The eleven debits total four hundred and twenty-three thousand and eighty dollars. The seven credits total the same. They agree, so no suspense account is needed. Common mistake. A common mistake is putting the bank overdraft in the debit column, because bank is usually a debit. An overdraft is money owed, so it is a credit. Another is putting a provision on the debit side. Provisions for depreciation and for irrecoverable debts are always credits.
