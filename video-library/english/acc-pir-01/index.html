Should a trader form a partnership (or an LLP)?

Read the narration

Idea. Imagine you run a small bakery on your own. You want a bigger oven, but you have no spare money, and you already work every hour. A friend offers to join you. Together you would form a partnership: two or more people running a business together and sharing its profits and losses. A partner brings capital, new skills and ideas, and shares the work and the risk. But the profits must be shared, the owner loses full control, and partners may disagree.

Method. First, note who is deciding. Then write each point with its effect: the point, then which means. Do this for one or two advantages and one or two disadvantages. Finish with a decision that follows from your strongest point. In an ordinary partnership, each partner has unlimited liability: their personal possessions can be taken to pay business debts. A limited liability partnership, an LLP, is a separate legal entity, so partners can lose only what they invested.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2020. Gurpreet is a sole trader, thinking about forming a partnership with another sole trader. Evaluate whether Gurpreet should form a partnership. Five marks. For: the partner would bring new capital, which could be used to expand the business. The partner would also bring extra skills and knowledge, so the business may be run better. Against: Gurpreet would have to share the profit, instead of keeping all of it. And Gurpreet could no longer make every decision alone, because decisions must be agreed with the partner. Decision: Gurpreet should form a partnership, because the extra capital and skills could help the business grow enough to make up for sharing the profit. Either decision earns the mark if it is supported. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying an ordinary partnership has limited liability. Only an LLP does. Another is listing advantages and stopping there. Evaluate needs both sides and a final decision.

Your turn. Now you try. Read about Lina's hair salon, then evaluate whether she should take Sara as a partner. Pause and decide.

Answer. For: Sara's twenty thousand dollars pays for the second salon, her beauty skills add new services, and the workload is shared. Against: Lina must share the profits, and she loses full control, so they may disagree. Decision: Lina should accept, because without the capital she cannot open the second salon at all. To protect her own possessions, she could form an LLP. Remember: points for, points against, each with its effect, then a clear decision. Now try the practice questions.
