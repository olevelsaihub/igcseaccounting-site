Idea. You keep a note of your spending, but your banking app shows a different balance. Maybe a payment has not cleared, or the bank took a fee you forgot. A business has the same problem. A bank reconciliation statement explains why the cash book balance differs from the bank statement balance on the same date. It is prepared by the business, not by the bank. And it is not a ledger account, so it is not part of the double entry.

Method. Learn the benefits. It brings the cash book up to date. It finds errors, made by the business or by the bank. It shows timing differences and very old cheques. And it helps deter fraud. Develop each one. For example: it brings the cash book up to date, because standing orders and bank charges first appear on the bank statement. Then end with advice to the trader.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. Explain to Numan two benefits of a monthly bank reconciliation, and advise him whether to continue. Five marks. Benefit one: it keeps Numan's cash book up to date. Comparing it with the bank statement shows items such as standing orders, direct debits and bank charges that he has not yet recorded. Benefit two: it helps him find errors, made in his own cash book or by the bank, so they are corrected quickly and his balance is accurate. Advice: Numan should continue to prepare one every month. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying the bank prepares the reconciliation. The business prepares it, from its own cash book and the bank statement. Another is describing how to prepare the statement when the question asks why. Give benefits, not steps.

Answer. One good answer: it helps deter fraud, because the bank statement is an independent record. Any payment Leila did not authorise would show up as a difference. So she should keep doing it every month. Remember: name the benefit, develop it with because, and finish with clear advice. Now try the practice questions.
