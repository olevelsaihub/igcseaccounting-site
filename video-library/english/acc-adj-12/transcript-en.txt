Idea. You use electricity all through December, but the bill is paid in January. Which month did that cost belong to? December, the month you used it. This is the accruals, or matching, concept. Income and expenses are recorded in the year they belong to, not when the cash moves. So an expense still owing at the year end is added to the year's expense. An expense paid in advance is taken off. A second concept is business entity. The business is separate from its owner. If the owner takes goods or cash for personal use, that is drawings, not a business expense.

Method. Here is the method. Name the concept. Say what it means in one sentence. Apply it to the item in the question. Then say what would go wrong without the adjustment. For an evaluate question, end with a conclusion. For example, a business pays thirteen thousand dollars of rent this year, but one thousand is for next year. This year's expense is only twelve thousand. Without the adjustment, profit would be one thousand dollars too low.

Worked example. Here is a real question from January 2020. We must evaluate why a business needs to account for other receivables and other payables. Five marks. First, know the terms. Other receivables are expenses paid in advance and income still owing to us. Other payables are expenses owing and income received in advance. Name the concept: accruals, or matching. It means the income statement shows only the income earned and the expenses used up in this year, whatever the date of payment. Now the effects. Each year's profit can be compared fairly with other years. And the financial statements give a true and fair view of the business. Conclusion: the adjustments are necessary, because without them, profit for the year and the current assets and current liabilities would be wrong. Common mistake. A common mistake is naming prudence for accruals and prepayments. The concept is accruals, or matching. Another is calling the owner's goods a business expense. They are drawings, because the business and the owner are separate.
