Idea. Think of a bakery. It buys flour, mixes and bakes it, then sells loaves of bread. A business that turns materials into products is called a manufacturer. At the end of the year, the bakery may have something at every stage. So a manufacturer holds three kinds of inventory: raw materials, work in progress and finished goods. Raw materials and work in progress are used in the manufacturing account. Finished goods are used in the income statement, because they are ready to sell.

Method. Step one. Write one sentence for each inventory, saying what stage of production it is at. Not yet started, partly made, or complete. Step two. When the question asks for the difference, link your sentences with words such as whereas. Step three. For a why question, give a reason and then develop it. For example, a manufacturer may buy in finished goods because it cannot make enough itself.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2023, Paper 2. Explain the difference between the three types of inventories. Three marks, so one for each inventory. Picture a furniture maker. Wood not yet cut is raw materials. A chair half built at the year end is work in progress. A finished chair in the showroom is finished goods. Now write it. Raw materials are inputs bought to be made into products, whereas work in progress is only partly made at the year end, and finished goods are complete and ready to sell. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is calling work in progress goods waiting to be sold. Those are finished goods. Work in progress is still being made. Another is describing raw materials as any goods bought. Say that they will be turned into products.

Your turn. Now you try. Kemi makes leather bags. Name the inventory for each item, then explain one reason why she might buy in finished bags. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The hides are not yet used, so they are raw materials. Bags without handles are partly made: work in progress. Packed bags are complete: finished goods. For part b, give a reason and develop it. Demand for her bags may be higher than her workshop can make, so she buys some in and does not lose sales. Remember: not started is raw materials, partly made is work in progress, and complete is finished goods. Now try the practice questions.
