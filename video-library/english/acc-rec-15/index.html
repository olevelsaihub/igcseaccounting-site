Which ledger holds which account

Idea. Imagine a corner shop that lets regular customers pay at the end of the month. The owner keeps one notebook for people who owe the shop, one for people the shop owes, and one for everything else. A business ledger is split in the same way. The receivables ledger, also called the sales ledger, holds an account for each credit customer. The payables ledger, or purchases ledger, holds an account for each credit supplier. The nominal, or general, ledger holds all the other accounts. The nominal ledger holds sales, purchases, returns, expenses, income, non-current assets, equity, loans and drawings. So the sales account itself is not in the sales ledger. It is in the nominal ledger.

Method. First, ask: is this account a person or a thing? A customer or supplier we trade with on credit is a person. Sales, rent or a van is a thing, so it is nominal. If it is a person, ask who owes whom. A credit customer owes us, so the account is in the receivables ledger. We owe a credit supplier, so that account is in the payables ledger. Then rule out the traps. Day books and the general journal are books of prime entry. They list transactions first, but no accounts are kept in them. For a question about a transaction, check two things: is it on credit, and is it goods? A cash sale, or selling an old van on credit, never goes in the receivables ledger.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2024. Where is the account of a credit customer kept? One mark. A credit customer buys now and pays later, so they owe us money. Money owed to us is a trade receivable. So the account is kept in the receivables ledger. Now rule out the others. A, the general journal, is a book of prime entry. B, the general ledger, holds nominal accounts. C, the payables ledger, holds suppliers. The answer is D. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is swapping the ledgers. A supplier is someone we pay, so their account is in the payables ledger. Another is putting the sale of an old asset on credit in the receivables ledger. Only credit sales of goods go there.

Your turn. Now you try. Lina runs a bakery. Decide which ledger holds each of her four accounts. Pause and decide.

Answer. Millpro is a credit supplier: Lina owes them, so payables ledger. Rent is a thing, so nominal ledger. Café Rio is a credit customer, so receivables ledger. And sales is a thing too, so nominal ledger, not the sales ledger. Remember: person or thing, then who owes whom, and never choose a book of prime entry. Now try the practice questions.
