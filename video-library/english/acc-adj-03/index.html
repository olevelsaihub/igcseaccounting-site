Choosing between straight line and reducing balance, and changing method

Idea. A new car loses a lot of value in its first year, and less each year after. Some assets, like office shelving, are used evenly for years. So businesses can choose how to spread the cost. Here, reducing balance at twenty per cent on a twenty thousand dollar van: the charge starts high and falls. Straight line gives the same charge every year. Twenty per cent of the twenty thousand dollar cost is four thousand, every year. After year one, it is higher than reducing balance. So which suits which asset? Reducing balance suits assets that lose most value early, or need more repairs as they age, such as vehicles. Straight line suits assets used evenly over their life.

Method. To discuss a method, first say how it charges depreciation. Then give the effect on profit for the year, and on the carrying value in the statement of financial position. In later years, reducing balance gives a lower charge than straight line. So profit is higher, and so is the carrying value. Straight line is the opposite. If a change is proposed, bring in the consistency concept. A method should be kept from year to year, so results can be compared. Then give a clear decision.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2025. Shauna uses reducing balance, and is thinking of changing to straight line. Advise her whether to change, discussing both methods. Five marks. Her current method, reducing balance, gives the biggest charge in the first year, then smaller charges each year. So in later years, the expense is smaller and profit for the year is higher. Straight line gives the same charge every year. After the first year, that charge would usually be bigger, so profit would be lower, and so would the carrying value of the asset and the net assets. The advice: Shauna should not change. Keeping reducing balance also follows the consistency concept, so her yearly results can be compared fairly. Common mistake. A common mistake is describing the methods without saying what happens to profit or the carrying value. Another is ending with no decision, or forgetting consistency when a change is proposed.

Your turn. Now you try. Tom uses straight line for his delivery vans, but the vans lose most of their value early. Advise Tom whether to change. Pause and decide.

Answer. Straight line gives an equal charge, so it understates the early loss in value. Reducing balance gives high early charges that fall later, which matches how his vans lose value. Early profits would be lower, and later profits higher. Consistency says he should not change often, so his years can be compared. But here there is a good reason. So sensible advice is: change once, to reducing balance, and keep it from then on. Remember: describe each method's pattern, give its effect on profit and carrying value, bring in consistency, and make a clear decision. Now try the practice questions.
