Idea. Your phone has a passcode, and your photos may be backed up to the cloud. A business protects its accounting records on computer in the same kinds of way. Data is at risk in two main ways. It can be lost or damaged. Or it can be seen or changed by people who should not have access. That is called unauthorised access.

Method. Step one. Decide what the question is about: stopping access, stopping loss, or both. Step two. To stop access, use passwords, a firewall, anti-virus software, encryption, or locked rooms. To stop loss, take regular backups and keep copies off-site or in the cloud. Step three. Write one different measure for each mark, in a few words. No long explanation is needed for a state question.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2025, Paper 1. State three ways that data may be protected when using a computerised accounting system. Three marks. First, stop outsiders getting in through the internet. Install a firewall. Second, stop harmful programs damaging or stealing data. Use anti-virus software. Third, stop unauthorised people using the system. Protect it with strong passwords. Check: three different ideas. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is giving the same method twice in different words. Passwords and secret login codes are one idea, so they earn one mark. Another is writing something vague, such as keep it safe, or giving a benefit of computers instead. Name a specific measure.

Your turn. Now you try. Lena runs a travel agency. Read her problems, then state one way to protect her data from each risk. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The staff problem is access, so protect the accounts with a strong password. The hard drive problem is loss, so back up the data regularly and keep a copy off-site or in the cloud.
