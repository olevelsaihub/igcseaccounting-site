Matching each transaction to its source document (and book)

Idea. If a teacher asks how you know something, you show your evidence. Accountants need evidence too. The original paper behind a transaction is its source document. Credit sales and purchases are evidenced by invoices. Goods returned are evidenced by a credit note. Money is different. A cheque we write is evidenced by its counterfoil. Cash paid or received, by a receipt. Credit transfers, interest and bank charges, by the bank statement. Small cash spending, by a petty cash voucher.

Method. Step one: is it a sale, a purchase, a return, or money in or out? Step two: is it on credit, or by cash or bank? Step three: name the document the business itself would hold. If the book is asked too, match it separately: day books for credit goods, the cash book for money, and the journal for the rest. A non-current asset on credit has a purchase invoice, but goes in the journal.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2025. We must state the source document for each transaction. The first is done for us: the sale on credit has a sales invoice. The others are a credit transfer received, a return to a supplier, an asset bought on credit, bank interest, and a cheque for personal use. Five marks. A receipt by credit transfer goes straight into the bank. The customer sends a remittance advice, and the bank statement shows it. A return to a credit supplier: the supplier sends us a credit note. A non-current asset on credit: the supplier's invoice, which to us is a purchase invoice. Bank interest: only the bank knows the amount, so the bank statement. A cheque for personal use: we keep the cheque counterfoil. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is answering from the wrong side. When the business buys goods, its document is a purchase invoice, not a sales invoice. Another is writing invoice for a cash sale. Invoices are for credit. A cash sale is evidenced by a receipt or till roll.

Your turn. Now you try. State the source document for each of Noor's four transactions. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. One: goods bought on credit, so a purchase invoice. Two: Noor sends her customer a credit note, and keeps a copy. Three: only the bank knows its charge, so the bank statement. Four: small cash spending, so a petty cash voucher. Remember: what happened, credit or money, then the document the business holds. Now try the practice questions.
