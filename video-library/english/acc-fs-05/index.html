Idea. Imagine taking a photo of a business on the last day of its year. It shows what the business owns, what it owes, and what belongs to the owner. That photo is the statement of financial position. Assets come first: non-current, then current. Below them come equity and liabilities. The two totals must always be equal.

Method. Show each non-current asset at cost, minus accumulated depreciation, to give its carrying amount. Accumulated depreciation is last year's balance plus this year's charge. Current assets are inventory, trade receivables minus the provision, amounts prepaid, and bank and cash. Current liabilities are due within a year: trade payables, amounts owing, and a bank overdraft. Closing equity is opening equity, plus profit for the year, minus drawings.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2025. We must prepare Taylor's statement of financial position at thirty-first March 2025. Twelve marks. Profit for the year, from part a, is one hundred and nine thousand nine hundred and sixty. Here is Taylor's trial balance, part one. And part two. Both totals are seven hundred thousand. Read the additional information. The wages of two hundred and thirty-one thousand cover only eleven months. So one month is twenty-one thousand, and March's twenty-one thousand is still owing. Fixtures: ten per cent of fifty thousand is five thousand, so accumulated depreciation is fifteen thousand. Vehicles use reducing balance: twenty thousand cost, less seven thousand two hundred already charged, leaves twelve thousand eight hundred. Twenty per cent of that is two thousand five hundred and sixty, so nine thousand seven hundred and sixty in total. In this question, land and buildings are not depreciated. The carrying amounts are thirty-five thousand and ten thousand two hundred and forty, so non-current assets total two hundred and ninety-five thousand two hundred and forty. The provision is five per cent of twelve thousand, six hundred, so receivables are eleven thousand four hundred. With inventory and cash in hand, current assets are forty-seven thousand seven hundred and twenty. Total assets: three hundred and forty-two thousand nine hundred and sixty. Now look at cash at bank. It is a credit balance. That means Taylor owes the bank: an overdraft of two thousand two hundred, a current liability. Closing equity is two hundred and fifty thousand, plus profit, minus fifty thousand drawings: three hundred and nine thousand nine hundred and sixty. Current liabilities total thirty-three thousand. Together that is three hundred and forty-two thousand nine hundred and sixty, the same as total assets. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is using last year's accumulated depreciation. Add this year's charge first. Another is putting a credit bank balance under current assets. It is an overdraft, so it is a current liability.
