Idea. A phone shop buys and sells all year. At the end, the owner asks: did I make a profit? The income statement answers that, for a period such as the year ended thirty-first December. It has two steps. Revenue minus the cost of the goods sold gives gross profit. Then gross profit, plus any other income, minus the expenses, gives profit for the year.

Method. Write the heading first. Then read the additional information and note every change next to the balance it affects, before you write a single line. Cost of sales is opening inventory, plus purchases, minus returns outwards, plus carriage inwards, minus goods the owner took, minus closing inventory. For expenses, add any amount owing and take off any amount paid in advance, so only this year's cost is charged. Depreciation is an expense. Buying a vehicle is not: it is a non-current asset.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. Tang is a sole trader. We must prepare his income statement for the year ended thirty-first December 2023. Twenty marks. Here is the table of Tang's balances, part one. And part two. Read the additional information first. Closing inventory, rates owing, insurance paid in advance, goods Tang took, a new vehicle hidden in motor expenses, and two depreciation rates. Revenue is one hundred and fifty thousand. For cost of sales, start with opening inventory and purchases, take off returns outwards, add carriage inwards, then take off the goods Tang took and closing inventory. Cost of sales is eighty-one thousand six hundred and nine. Gross profit is one hundred and fifty thousand minus eighty-one thousand six hundred and nine: sixty-eight thousand three hundred and ninety-one. Now adjust the expenses. Rates: add the five hundred and seventy owing, giving two thousand. Insurance: take off the six hundred prepaid, giving three thousand. Motor expenses: take out the fifteen thousand vehicle, leaving seven thousand eight hundred and ninety. Depreciation. Fixtures, straight line: ten per cent of ten thousand is one thousand. Vehicles, reducing balance: fifty thousand cost, minus five thousand already charged, is forty-five thousand. Twenty-five per cent of that is eleven thousand two hundred and fifty. List all eight expenses. They total thirty-two thousand eight hundred and ninety. Profit for the year is sixty-eight thousand three hundred and ninety-one minus that: thirty-five thousand five hundred and one. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is adding a prepayment. Money paid in advance belongs to next year, so take it off. It is an amount owing that you add. Another is mixing up carriage. Carriage inwards, on goods coming in, belongs in cost of sales. Carriage outwards, on deliveries to customers, is an expense.

Your turn. Now you try. Read the details for Mia's café, then work out her gross profit and her profit for the year. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Cost of sales is four thousand, plus twenty-five thousand, plus one thousand carriage inwards, minus five thousand: twenty-five thousand. So gross profit is thirty-five thousand. Wages are thirteen thousand, rent is five thousand five hundred, and depreciation is two thousand. Expenses total twenty thousand five hundred, so profit for the year is fourteen thousand five hundred. Remember: adjust first, find gross profit, then take off the expenses to reach profit for the year. Now try the practice questions.
