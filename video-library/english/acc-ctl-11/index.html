Idea. Your family probably pays a phone bill or a gym fee straight from a bank account, without handing over any cash. Banks offer several ways to pay, and each one has its own name. A standing order is set up by the payer, who tells the bank to pay a fixed amount at regular times. A direct debit is collected by the payee, the business being paid, and the amount can change. A credit transfer is money paid straight into the business's account by someone else. A dishonoured cheque is one the bank refuses to pay, for example because there is not enough money in the account. Two terms are about timing. An unpresented cheque is in the cash book, but the bank has not paid it yet. An outstanding lodgement is money paid in and recorded, but not yet on the bank statement.

Method. To define a term, give its key features. For a payment method, say who starts it, and whether the amount is fixed or can vary. For a timing term, say what is in the cash book and what the bank has not done yet. A bank statement is written from the bank's side. A credit balance means the bank owes the business: there is money in the account. A debit balance means an overdraft.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2023. State two characteristics of a direct debit. Two marks. Feature one: the amount paid can vary each time. That is the big difference from a standing order. Feature two: the payment is started by the payee, the business receiving the money, not by the payer. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is describing a direct debit as a fixed amount set by the payer. That is a standing order. Another is reading a credit balance on the bank statement as an overdraft. On the statement, credit means money in the bank.

Your turn. Now you try. Name the bank term in each case. Pause and decide.

Answer. One: Zara starts it and the amount is fixed: a standing order. Two: the payee collects a varying amount: a direct debit. Three: recorded by Zara, not yet paid by the bank: an unpresented cheque. Remember: for each term, say who starts it, whether it is fixed, and what the bank has or has not done.
