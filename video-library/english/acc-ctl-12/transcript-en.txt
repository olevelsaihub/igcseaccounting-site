Idea. You track your pocket money in a notebook. Then your bank app shows a fee you did not know about. You add the fee to your notebook, so your balance is right. Items only on the bank statement go into the cash book. Items only in the cash book, unpresented cheques and outstanding lodgements, do not. They go in the reconciliation.

Method. Start with the opening balance. Debit means money in the bank. Credit means an overdraft. Money in, such as credit transfers and interest received, goes on the debit side. Money out, such as standing orders, direct debits, bank charges and dishonoured cheques, goes on the credit side. A dishonoured cheque is a credit, because the money we recorded as received never arrived. Correct any cash book errors by the difference only. Then balance and bring down.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2024. Nabil's cash book shows a debit balance of one thousand four hundred and ten. Prepare the updated cash book and bring the balance down. Seven marks. Five items appeared only on the bank statement: these go in. Two appeared only in the cash book: Fatima's cheque and the cash sales banked. Leave those out. Debits total one thousand five hundred and twenty-three. Credits are four hundred and ninety-five. The balance carried down is one thousand and twenty-eight. Bring one thousand and twenty-eight down on the debit side on the first of May. Nabil has that much in the bank. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is entering unpresented cheques or outstanding lodgements in the cash book. They are already there. Another is debiting a dishonoured cheque. The money never arrived, so it comes out of the bank balance: credit.
