Idea. When you buy a drink with cash, two things happen: you get a drink, and your cash goes down. Every business transaction is like this. It is recorded twice, once as a debit and once as a credit, for the same amount. Debit the account that receives value: an asset that goes up, an expense, or drawings. Credit the account that gives value: an asset that goes down, income, a liability that goes up, or equity that goes up. Say a shop buys goods to sell for three hundred dollars, paying from the bank. Purchases receives the goods, so debit purchases. The bank gives the money, so credit bank.

Method. First, name the two accounts the transaction touches. Then ask which one receives value: debit it. And which one gives value: credit it. Next, check where the money came from: business cash, the business bank, or the owner's own pocket. If the owner pays a business cost with private money, credit equity, because the owner has put more in. Finally, pick the option with your debit and your credit in the right columns. Many wrong options use the same two accounts, swapped.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2022. A trader takes goods from the business for personal use. Which account is debited, and which is credited? The owner is taking value out of the business for personal use. That is drawings, so debit drawings. The goods were bought earlier and recorded in purchases. They will not be sold now, so reduce purchases by crediting it. Do not credit sales: nothing was sold, and no income was earned. So the answer is A: debit drawings, credit purchases. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is crediting sales for goods the owner takes. No sale happened. Another is crediting bank when the owner paid with private money. The owner has put more in, so credit equity.

Your turn. Now you try. Nadia runs a flower shop. For each of the three transactions, decide the account to debit and the account to credit. Pause and decide.

Answer. The van is an asset the business receives: debit motor vehicles, and the bank gives, so credit bank. Cash taken by the owner: debit drawings, credit cash. Electricity is an expense: debit electricity, credit cash. Remember: name the two accounts, debit the one that receives, credit the one that gives, and check where the money came from.
