Idea. Think about your school and the corner shop near your home. A state school is run by the government and paid for by taxes. The corner shop is owned by one person, who wants to make money. The part of the economy run by the government is the public sector. Its main aim is to give a service to the community. The private sector is owned by private individuals, and its main aim is to make a profit. A sole trader is one person who owns the business. They put in the capital, take all the decisions, and keep all of the profit.

Method. Step one: who owns it, the government or private individuals? Step two: link the owner to the aim. Government means a service. Private owners mean profit. For a sole trader, think about the one owner. Each advantage is a separate point: easy and cheap to set up, full control, quick decisions, and the profit is not shared. When a question asks for a difference, cover both sides in one sentence. Say what one does, then use whereas for the other. Each side earns a mark.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2022. It asks for one difference between the objectives of private sector and public sector organisations. Two marks. Start with the private sector. It is owned by private individuals, so its main aim is to make a profit for its owners. Now the public sector. It is owned and funded by the government, so its main aim is to provide a service to the community. Join them in one sentence. Private sector organisations aim mainly to make a profit, whereas public sector organisations aim to serve the community. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is describing only one side. That loses half the marks. Another is saying the public sector is owned by the public, or by shareholders. It is owned by the government. And a public limited company is in the private sector.

Your turn. Now you try. Amira runs a bakery on her own, as a sole trader. State two advantages to her of being a sole trader. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Think about the one owner. Amira keeps all the profit, and she has full control of the bakery. Quick decisions, or being cheap to set up, would also score. Any two different points earn the marks. Remember: find the owner, link it to the aim, and give both sides with whereas. Now try the practice questions.
