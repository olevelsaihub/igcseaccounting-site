Idea. Imagine adding up a hundred sales by hand every month. It is slow, and easy to make a mistake. A computer can do the same sums in a second. Accounting software records each transaction once, completes the double entry, updates the ledgers and produces reports. A spreadsheet uses formulae, so totals update as soon as a figure changes. The benefits are speed, accurate calculations, up-to-date information and easy reports. The drawbacks are the cost of hardware and software, training, the risk of losing data or being hacked, and errors when wrong figures are typed in.

Method. Here is the method for advise or evaluate. Say exactly what the trader plans. Give an advantage and develop it. Give a disadvantage and develop it. Finish with a clear decision and a reason. Developing a point means adding its effect, using words like which means, or because. For example: software is faster, which means the owner saves time every month.

Worked example. Here is a real question from June 2025. Rio is thinking of using spreadsheets to manage his receivables and payables. We must advise him. Five marks. The advantage. Rio can set up formulae so each customer's and supplier's balance is worked out automatically. This saves him time and reduces arithmetic mistakes. The disadvantage. Rio may not know how to use spreadsheets, so he may need training. That costs time and money, which adds to his expenses and reduces his profit. Now decide. The time saved continues every month for years, but training is a one-off cost. So advise Rio to start using spreadsheets. Advising against is also fine, if you give a reason. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is listing only advantages, with no effects and no decision. That caps the mark. Another is saying a computer removes all errors. If a wrong figure is typed in, the answer is still wrong.

Your turn. Now you try. Amara owns a small bakery and keeps her accounts by hand. Should she buy accounting software? Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Advantage: the software does the double entry and the totals for her, so she makes fewer mistakes and her figures are up to date. Disadvantage: one thousand two hundred dollars is a big outlay for a small bakery, and the training course adds to her expenses, which lowers her profit. Decision: she makes many mistakes now, and the cost is paid once. So advise Amara to buy the software. Remember: an advantage with its effect, a disadvantage with its effect, then a decision with a reason. Now try the practice questions.
