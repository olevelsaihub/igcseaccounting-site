Idea. A cake seller keeps only her bank statements. Cash in and cash out is not the same as profit. To find her profit, we first rebuild her sales and costs, then prepare an income statement. The income statement has the same shape as always. Revenue minus cost of sales is gross profit. Add other income and take off expenses to reach profit for the year.

Method. Step one: derive revenue and purchases. Take the cash figure, take off the opening balance, and add the closing balance. Step two: cost of sales is opening inventory, plus purchases, minus closing inventory. Step three: adjust each expense. Add amounts owing at the end. Take off amounts prepaid at the end. And calculate depreciation as an expense.

Worked example. Here is a real question from January 2023. Prepare Yamini's income statement for the year ended thirtieth September 2022. Twelve marks. First, the balances Yamini gave, at the start and the end of the year. The provision for depreciation at the end is still to be calculated. Here is her bank summary. Receipts from customers, interest received, and payments for drawings, office expenses, suppliers and wages. Revenue is one hundred and seventy-eight thousand seven hundred and fifty. Purchases are ninety-two thousand seven hundred and fifty, minus fifteen thousand five hundred, plus twelve thousand seven hundred and fifty: ninety thousand dollars. Cost of sales is twenty-five thousand seven hundred and fifty, plus ninety thousand, minus twenty-seven thousand four hundred and sixty: eighty-eight thousand two hundred and ninety. Gross profit is ninety thousand four hundred and sixty. Depreciation is twenty per cent of the carrying amount, forty thousand: eight thousand. Office expenses are twenty-six thousand seven hundred and fifty, minus four hundred prepaid. Wages are thirty-three thousand, plus five hundred owing. Add interest received of one hundred and ten. Expenses total sixty-seven thousand eight hundred and fifty. Profit for the year is twenty-two thousand seven hundred and twenty. Drawings are left out. Common mistake. A common mistake is using cash paid to suppliers as purchases. Adjust it for the opening and closing payables. Another is putting drawings in the expenses. Drawings are the owner taking money out. They are never an expense.
