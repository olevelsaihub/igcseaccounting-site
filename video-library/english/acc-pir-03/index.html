Why partners get interest, salaries and loan interest

Idea. Two friends start a café. Amir puts in fifty thousand dollars. Bella puts in ten thousand, but works much longer hours. Splitting the profit half and half would not feel fair to either of them. So partners agree extra rewards, and charges, before the profit is shared. Interest on capital rewards partners for the money they invested. Interest on drawings is charged to stop partners taking too much out. A salary rewards extra work. And interest on a partner's loan rewards money lent to the business.

Method. Name the purpose in one phrase: reward, discourage, or compensate. Then add the effect on the business or the partner. Each linked point earns a mark, so write one point per mark. Interest on a partner's loan is different. It is an expense, charged before profit for the year, and credited to the partner's current account. Interest on capital and salaries are shares of profit, shown in the appropriation account. With more than one owner, partners need an appropriation account to share the profit, a capital account for each partner's investment, and a current account for what each partner earns and takes out.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2021. Explain why a partnership charges interest on drawings. Three marks, so we need three linked points. First, the purpose. Charging interest makes taking money out cost the partner something, so partners are discouraged from taking large drawings. Second, the effect. Less money leaves the business, so more cash stays inside it. That helps its cash flow. Third, a further effect. With more cash, the business can pay its bills when they fall due, so its liquidity improves. This explanation is by olevels dot com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is saying interest on drawings is paid to the partner. It is charged to the partner, so it adds to the profit to be shared. Another is treating loan interest as an appropriation. It is an expense, charged before profit for the year.

Your turn. Now you try. Read about Jaya and Tom, then explain why their agreement includes interest on capital and a salary for Tom. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Interest on capital rewards Jaya for investing four times as much as Tom. Without it, her extra money would earn her nothing extra, so the sharing is fairer. A salary rewards Tom for the extra work he does, running the shop full-time. So each partner's reward matches what they put in. Remember: give the purpose, then the effect, one linked point for each mark. Now try the practice questions.
