Idea. You own a bike worth three hundred dollars and a phone worth two hundred. You owe your sister one hundred. What is really yours? Five hundred minus one hundred: four hundred dollars. A business is the same. What it owns are assets. What it owes are liabilities. The difference is equity, the owner's capital. When a trader keeps no full records, we list the assets and liabilities at a date. That list is a statement of affairs.

Method. First, sort each balance into assets or liabilities. Careful: a bank overdraft is money owed to the bank, so it is a liability. Next, use the carrying amount for non-current assets: cost minus the depreciation so far. Then add up total assets and total liabilities separately, and take one from the other. That is equity. To find profit, compare closing equity with opening equity. Add back drawings, because they took equity out. Take off new capital, because it was put in, not earned.

Worked example. Here is a real question from November 2023. Ed is a sole trader. Calculate his equity at the first of May 2022. Three marks. Here are Ed's balances. Fixtures and fittings cost one hundred and twenty-three thousand five hundred, with depreciation of fifty-five thousand five hundred and seventy-five. The carrying amount is one hundred and twenty-three thousand five hundred minus fifty-five thousand five hundred and seventy-five: sixty-seven thousand nine hundred and twenty-five. Add the assets: fixtures, bank, inventory and trade receivables. Total assets are one hundred and seventy-one thousand, one hundred and five dollars. Liabilities are other payables, one thousand four hundred, and trade payables, twenty-five thousand one hundred: twenty-six thousand five hundred in total. Equity is one hundred and seventy-one thousand, one hundred and five, minus twenty-six thousand five hundred: one hundred and forty-four thousand, six hundred and five dollars. Common mistake. A common mistake is adding non-current assets at cost. Use the carrying amount. Another is forgetting drawings when finding profit. Drawings lowered closing equity, so add them back.

Remember: assets minus liabilities gives equity. The change in equity, plus drawings, minus new capital, gives profit. Now try the practice questions.
