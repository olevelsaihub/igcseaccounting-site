  3. Part (b)(ii) · 1 mark · State · Partners' current and capital accounts · A-Grade Plan · practice
    Read part (b)(ii) as text

    (ii) State one reason why a debit balance may arise on a partner’s current account. (1)

    Automatic text extract; the original image above is the authority.

    Hint for part (b)(ii)

    Think about what each side of a current account records: credits are amounts earned by the partner, debits are amounts taken. A debit balance means the partner has taken out more than they earned; say it in one clear sentence.

  4. Part (c) · 5 marks · Evaluate · Evaluating profitability using ratios · A-Grade Plan · practice
    Read part (c) as text

    (c) Evaluate the change in profitability of the business over the two years and state whether the partners are correct. (5)

    Automatic text extract; the original image above is the authority.

    Hint for part (c)

    Quote each ratio for both years and give a reason for each change, not just a description. Weigh the ratios against each other, keep liquidity out of a profitability answer, and end with a judgement that answers the question or the claim directly.

    (d) State, indicating with a tick, (), the effect on working capital of each of the following transactions. (6) Transaction Increase Decrease No effect Purchase of a non‑current asset by cash Sale of a non‑current asset on credit Non‑current asset taken by owner for own use Purchase of inventory on credit Sale of inventory for cash Inventory taken by the owner for own use (Total for Question 2 = 25 marks)

    Automatic text extract; the original image above is the authority.

    Hint for part (d)

    For each transaction, write the two accounts it changes and tick one box per row. Profit only moves if income or an expense changes, so drawings, capital or buying a non-current asset do not count; for working capital, a swap between two current items has no effect.
