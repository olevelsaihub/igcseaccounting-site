Knowledge card

Types of error and whether the trial balance shows them

Written

Six error types leave debits and credits equal, so the trial balance still agrees. Name each by asking: recorded? right figure? sides swapped? wrong account?

1

Which error is it, and does the trial balance show it?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

5 errors to find

Each mistake is named. Find out why each is right.

  1. Same class of account
  2. Wrong class of account
  3. Wrong figure from the start
  4. Right accounts, sides swapped
  5. Two mistakes cancel out

Same class of account

Why
Amir and Amin are both customer accounts: right amount, wrong account.
Write it as
Error of commission.

Wrong class of account

Why
A van is a non-current asset, not an expense: a different kind of account.
Write it as
Error of principle.

Exam tipAn asset posted to an expense is always principle.

Wrong figure from the start

Why
The mistake was made in the book of prime entry, so both sides carry $250.
Write it as
Error of original entry.

Right accounts, sides swapped

Why
Cash and sales are correct, but cash should be debited and sales credited.
Write it as
Complete reversal of entries.

Two mistakes cancel out

Why
Sales (credit) and wages (debit) are both $100 too high, so the totals rise equally.
Write it as
Compensating errors.

4 errors to find

Which errors will the trial balance reveal, and which not?

  1. Omission is hidden
  2. One-sided posting
  3. Wrong amount, one side
  4. Overcast total

Omission is hidden

Why
Nothing was posted, so neither side is missing.
Remember
A transaction not in the books at all cannot be shown.

One-sided posting

Why
Bank was credited $450 but wages never debited, so credits are $450 bigger.
Result
The trial balance does not agree.

Wrong amount, one side

Why
Bank credit $380 but rent debit $830: only one side is wrong.
Compare
Not original entry: that has the same wrong figure on both sides.

Overcast total

Why
Sales total is $200 too high; customer accounts were posted correctly.
Result
Credit total $200 too big, so the trial balance disagrees.

4

The method

  1. Was it recorded?

    Nothing entered: omission. Equal mistakes cancelling: compensating.

  2. Right amount, wrong account?

    Same class: commission. Different class: principle.

  3. Wrong figure or sides?

    Wrong figure in the day book: original entry. Sides swapped: complete reversal.

  4. Revealed or not?

    If only one side is wrong, the trial balance will not agree.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Asset as an expense

    A non-current asset in an expense account is principle, not commission.

  • ✗
    Wrong amount, one side only

    Not original entry: a one-sided error the trial balance shows.

  • ✗
    Swapped sides

    Swapped sides with correct accounts: complete reversal, not principle.

  • ✗
    Omission is hidden

    A transaction left out completely does not upset the totals.

Worked explanation

  1. 'A purchase invoice was not entered in the day book.' Nothing was recorded, so it is omission.
  2. 'A cash purchase was debited to the cash account and credited to the purchases account.' The right accounts are used but the sides are swapped: complete reversal.
  3. 'Goods purchased on credit from Tessa had been posted to the account of Nessa.' Wrong account, but both are supplier accounts (same class): commission.
  4. 'The purchase of a motor vehicle had been entered in the motor expenses account.' A non-current asset was treated as an expense (different class): principle.
  5. 'The total of a sales invoice was incorrectly entered in the day book.' The wrong amount was used at the prime entry stage: original entry.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correctly named error, five in total. The name alone is enough; 'error of' is not needed.
