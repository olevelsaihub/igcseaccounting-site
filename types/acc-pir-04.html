Knowledge card

Partnership appropriation account

Layout

The appropriation account shares the profit for the year: add interest on drawings, deduct interest on capital and salaries, then split the residual profit in the ratio.

1

Follow the profit as it is shared

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

3 features to find

Find the corrections that turn the draft profit into the profit for the year.

  1. Loan interest first
  2. Missed expense
  3. The figure to share

Loan interest first

Why it matters
Ben lent 20 000 at 5%: 20 000 x 5% = 1 000. Loan interest is an expense, so it comes off before sharing.
Double entry
Debit the income statement, credit Ben's current account.

Exam tipLoan interest never goes in the appropriation account.

Missed expense

Why it matters
Any expense left out of the draft profit must be deducted first.

The figure to share

Check
58 000 - 1 000 - 3 000 = 54 000.
Why it matters
The appropriation account starts from this corrected figure.
0 of 3 found

5 features to find

Find which items are added, which are deducted, and how the remainder is shared.

  1. Interest on drawings added
  2. Interest on capital deducted
  3. Salary for a full year
  4. Residual profit
  5. Shares add back

Interest on drawings added

Working
Ali 12 000 x 5% = 600; Ben 16 000 x 5% = 800; total 1 400.
Why added
Partners pay it to the business, so there is more profit to share.

Exam tipInclude goods taken for own use in drawings before you calculate.

Interest on capital deducted

Working
Ali 50 000 x 6% = 3 000; Ben 30 000 x 6% = 1 800; total 4 800.
Why deducted
It is a share of profit given to the partners first, not an expense.

Salary for a full year

Working
1 500 x 12 = 18 000.
Why deducted
It is paid to Ben out of profit before the rest is shared.

Residual profit

Working
54 000 + 1 400 - 4 800 - 18 000 = 32 600.
Why it matters
Only this remainder is split in the ratio.

Shares add back

Working
Ali 32 600 x 3/5 = 19 560; Ben 32 600 x 2/5 = 13 040.
Check
19 560 + 13 040 = 32 600.

2

The residual profit

Residual profit = Profit for the year + Interest on drawings − Interest on capital − Salaries

Then share it in the profit-sharing ratio.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find RP:RP = PFY + IOD − IOC − S

To find PFY:PFY = RP − IOD + IOC + S

Try itProfit for the year is $40 000; drawings interest $1 000, capital interest $5 000, Lee's salary $12 000. Share equally.

  1. Add drawings interest: 41 000.
  2. Less capital interest: 36 000.
  3. Less salary: 24 000 residual profit.
  4. Share equally: 24 000 / 2 = $12 000 each.

5

The method

  1. Start with the profit

    Use the profit for the year, corrected first if told to.

  2. Adjust for interest

    Add interest on drawings, including goods taken; deduct interest on capital.

  3. Deduct salaries

    Use the salary for the full period, for example monthly amount x 12.

  4. Share the residual

    Split the residual profit in the ratio and check the shares add back.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Deducting interest on drawings

    It is added, because partners pay it to the business.

  • ✗
    Monthly salary

    Multiply a monthly salary by 12 for a year.

  • ✗
    Loan interest in the account

    Interest on a partner's loan is an expense, not an appropriation.

  • ✗
    Sharing the wrong figure

    Share the residual profit, not the profit for the year.

What a complete response includes

Marks go to each partner's interest on drawings and interest on capital, the salary, the residual profit and each partner's share. Later figures can earn own-figure marks if they follow correctly from an earlier slip.

Worked explanation

  1. Profit for the year is $66 200.
  2. Total drawings: Belen $20 000 cash; Charo $36 000 cash + $2 000 goods = $38 000.
  3. Interest on drawings at 8%: Belen 20 000 x 8% = $1 600; Charo 38 000 x 8% = $3 040; total $4 640 (added).
  4. Interest on capital at 5% on opening capital: Belen 80 000 x 5% = $4 000; Charo 90 000 x 5% = $4 500; total $8 500 (deducted).
  5. Salary for Charo: $2 000 x 12 = $24 000 (deducted).
  6. Residual profit: 66 200 + 4 640 - 8 500 - 24 000 = $38 340.
  7. Equal shares: 38 340 / 2 = $19 170 each; check 19 170 + 19 170 = 38 340.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each partner's interest on drawings, one for each partner's interest on capital, one for the salary, one for the residual profit and one for each partner's share (8 in total). The residual and shares are marked on your own figures if an earlier figure is wrong. Showing the subtotal of $70 840 is optional.
