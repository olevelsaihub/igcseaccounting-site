Why accruals, prepayments and owner's own use are adjusted (concepts)

Explaining or evaluating why amounts owing and paid in advance are adjusted at the year end (accruals concept), and why goods or cash taken by the owner must be recorded as drawings (business entity concept).

★★☆☆☆ · 7 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Knowledge card

Why accruals, prepayments and owner's own use are adjusted (concepts)

Written

The accruals concept means income statements show only the income and expenses that belong to that year, whenever cash moves. The business entity concept makes goods the owner takes drawings.

Which year does it belong to?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

3 features to find

Find the three features that show the accruals concept at work.

  1. Belongs to next year
  2. Charge matches the year
  3. Shown as a current asset

Belongs to next year

Why it matters
The January 2026 rent was paid early. It is a cost of next year, not this year.
Write it as
The prepaid rent relates to the next financial year.

Charge matches the year

Why it matters
Only the 12 months that belong to 2025 are charged: $13 000 - $1 000 = $12 000.
Write it as
The income statement includes only the expense incurred in the year.

Shown as a current asset

Why it matters
The landlord owes the shop a month's use of the premises.
Without it
Profit would be understated and current assets would be missing.

3 features to find

Find the three features that show the business entity concept.

  1. Personal use
  2. Purchases reduced
  3. Recorded as drawings

Personal use

Why it matters
The business is treated as separate from its owner, so her personal use is not a business cost.
Write it as
Under the business entity concept, goods for the owner's own use are drawings.

Purchases reduced

Working
$40 000 - $600 = $39 400.
Why it matters
Cost of sales shows only goods used by the business, so profit is not understated.

Recorded as drawings

Why it matters
Drawings reduce the owner's capital, not the business's profit.

Exam tipNever call goods taken by the owner a business expense.

The method

  1. Name the concept

    Owing or in advance: accruals (matching). Owner's own use: business entity.

  2. Say what it means

    Give one sentence on what the concept requires.

  3. Apply it

    Link to the item; say what is wrong without it: profit, assets or liabilities.

  4. Conclude if asked

    For Evaluate, finish with why the adjustment is necessary.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Prudence

    Accruals and prepayments are the accruals concept, not prudence.

  • ✗
    Owner's goods as an expense

    Goods taken by the owner are drawings, not a business expense.

  • ✗
    Name only

    Apply the concept to the item, or development marks go.

  • ✗
    No conclusion

    An Evaluate answer needs a final judgement.

Worked explanation

  1. Other receivables include expenses paid in advance and income owing; other payables include expenses owing and income received in advance.
  2. Name the concept: accruals (matching).
  3. Apply: only the income and expenses that belong to this year go into this year's income statement.
  4. Effect 1: the results can be compared fairly from one year to the next.
  5. Effect 2: the statements give a true and fair view of the business's position.
  6. Conclusion: the adjustments make profit and the current assets and liabilities more accurate.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Accounting for other receivables and other payables applies the accruals (matching) concept (1). Under this concept, the income statement includes only the income earned and the expenses incurred in this year, whatever the date of payment (1). This means each year's profit can be compared fairly with other years (1), and the financial statements give a true and fair view of the business's financial position (1). Conclusion: it is necessary because, without these adjustments, profit for the year and the current assets and current liabilities would be misstated (1).

How the marks are earned

One mark for the general reason (the accruals concept), three for developing it (what it requires, comparability, true and fair view) and one for the conclusion. Other valid developments, such as users relying on the figures, are accepted.
