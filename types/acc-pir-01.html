Should a trader form a partnership (or an LLP)?

Written

A partner brings more capital and new skills, but profits are shared and control is lost. Ordinary partners have unlimited liability; only an LLP protects their personal possessions.

4 points to find

Find two points for and two points against, each with its effect.

  1. More capital
  2. New skills
  3. Profits shared
  4. Loss of control

More capital

Point
A partner brings new capital.
Effect
The money can be used to expand the business.

New skills

Point
A partner brings skills and ideas.
Effect
The business may be run better or reach new customers.

Profits shared

Point
Profits must be shared with the partner.
Effect
Hana no longer keeps all of the profit.

Loss of control

Point
Decisions must be agreed between the partners.
Effect
Hana cannot decide alone, and partners may disagree.

Exam tipWrite each point, then "which means..." to show its effect. The effect earns the second mark.

3 points to find

Find the three features that show the extra protection of an LLP.

  1. Unlimited liability
  2. Separate legal entity
  3. Lose only the investment

Unlimited liability

Meaning
Partners can be made to pay all the business debts.
Write it as
Their personal possessions can be taken to pay business debts.

Separate legal entity

Meaning
The LLP exists in law apart from its partners.

Lose only the investment

Meaning
Each partner can lose only the amount invested in the LLP.
Write it as
Partners' personal assets are protected.

Exam tipNever say an ordinary partnership has limited liability. Only an LLP does.

The method

  1. Who decides?

    Note who the decision is for: a sole trader, existing partners, or an LLP choice.

  2. Points for and against

    Give one or two points each way, each followed by which means....

  3. LLP questions

    Explain limited liability and that it protects the partners' personal assets.

  4. Give a decision

    End with a one-sentence decision linked to your strongest point.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    One side only

    An evaluate answer needs points for and against.

  • ✗
    No decision

    Without a final decision, the conclusion mark is lost.

  • ✗
    Limited liability mix-up

    Only an LLP has limited liability.

  • ✗
    No development

    Add the effect to each point, or only half the marks come.

What a complete response includes

Short items give one mark per relevant point. Longer items give a mark for each identified benefit or drawback and a further mark for showing its effect, with a final mark for a decision that follows from the argument.

Worked explanation

  1. Gurpreet is a sole trader; the question is whether to take on a partner.
  2. Advantage 1: a partner brings new capital. Advantage 2: a partner brings extra skills and knowledge.
  3. Disadvantage 1: Gurpreet would have to share the profits. Disadvantage 2: Gurpreet would no longer make every decision alone.
  4. Weigh them up and give a decision, e.g. that the extra money and skills outweigh sharing profit.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

If Gurpreet takes a partner, the partner will put new capital into the business, which could be used to expand it. The partner will also bring additional skills and expertise, so the business may be run better. On the other hand, Gurpreet would have to share the profit with the partner instead of keeping all of it. Gurpreet would also lose full control, because decisions would have to be agreed with the partner. Decision: Gurpreet should form a partnership, because the extra capital and skills could help the business grow enough to make up for sharing the profit.

How the marks are earned

Two marks are available for points in favour (extra capital; extra skills or expertise) and two for points against (sharing profit; losing control of decisions). One mark goes to a decision. Either decision (form or do not form) is accepted if it is supported. Other sensible points, such as shared workload or the risk of disagreements, can also be credited.
