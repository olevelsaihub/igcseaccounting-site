Knowledge card

The principles of professional ethics

Written

There are five principles of professional ethics: integrity is honest, objectivity is unbiased, professional competence is skilled, confidentiality keeps secrets and professional behaviour obeys the law.

1

Which principle is it?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

5 principles to find

Find the key word in each situation and name the principle.

  1. Honest: integrity
  2. Unbiased: objectivity
  3. Skilled: competence
  4. Secrets: confidentiality
  5. Law: behaviour

Honest: integrity

Key word
Honest, straightforward, truthful.
Also accepted
Honesty

Unbiased: objectivity

Key word
Bias, conflict of interest, pressure.
Write it as
Objectivity

Exam tipHonest is integrity; free from bias is objectivity. These two are often mixed up.

Skilled: competence

Key word
Knowledge, skills, care.
Write it as
Professional competence (and due care)

Secrets: confidentiality

Key word
Private information, permission.
Exception
Information may be shared if the law requires it.

Law: behaviour

Key word
Laws, regulations, reputation of the profession.
Not
Competence, which is about skill.

3 principles to find

Find the items that are not principles of professional ethics.

  1. A real principle
  2. Accounting concept
  3. Not a principle

A real principle

Why
It sounds like a concept, but it is an ethics principle.
Meaning
Client information is not shared without permission.

Accounting concept

Why
Prudence is about how figures are valued, not behaviour.
Do this
Remove accounting concepts first.

Not a principle

Why
Duality is not one of the five principles.
Remember
Integrity, objectivity, competence, confidentiality, behaviour.

Does it score?

Exam question

An accountant must not allow bias or pressure from others to affect her judgement. Name the principle of professional ethics described. (1 mark)

Tap an answer to see if it earns the mark.

4

The method

  1. Learn the key words

    Integrity: honest; objectivity: unbiased; competence: skilled; confidentiality: secrets; behaviour: law.

  2. Find the key word

    Look for words such as honest, bias, knowledge or private information.

  3. Write the full name

    Write the principle's name, for example professional behaviour.

  4. Which is not

    Remove accounting concepts and invented words first.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Integrity or objectivity

    Honest is integrity; free from bias is objectivity.

  • ✗
    Confidentiality is real

    Confidentiality is a principle, even though it sounds like a concept.

  • ✗
    Concepts are not ethics

    Prudence and materiality are accounting concepts.

  • ✗
    Law is behaviour

    Obeying the law is professional behaviour, not competence.

What a complete response includes

One mark for each correctly named principle. Naming an accounting concept instead of an ethics principle earns nothing; 'honesty' is accepted as an alternative to integrity.

The principles of professional ethics · June 2025 · Paper 1 · Question 13 · part (b) · 2 marks

(b) State which principle of professional ethics is being described. (2) Description Principle of professional ethics Accountants must be free from bias/conflict of interest. Accountants must operate in a straightforward/honest manner.

View full original question and diagrams
Hint for part (b)

Spot the key word in each description and match it to its principle; write the full name. Do not confuse honesty with freedom from bias.

Your answer

Write your answer first. Feedback and progress stay on this device.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Row 1 says accountants must be free from bias or conflict of interest. The key word is 'bias', which points to objectivity.
  2. Row 2 says accountants must act in a straightforward and honest way. The key word is 'honest', which points to integrity.
  3. Write each principle name in the table.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Accountants must be free from bias/conflict of interest -> Objectivity Accountants must operate in a straightforward/honest manner -> Integrity

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correct principle, 2 marks in total. Naming an accounting concept would score nothing.
