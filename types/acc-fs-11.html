Knowledge card

Preparing and correcting a manufacturing account

Layout

The manufacturing account builds up production cost in layers: raw materials consumed plus direct costs gives prime cost, then add factory overheads, then adjust for work in progress.

  • The manufacturing account works out the cost of making the goods finished in the year (production cost).
  • Cost of raw materials consumed = opening raw materials + purchases (+ carriage inwards) - closing raw materials.
  • Prime cost = raw materials consumed + direct (production) wages + direct expenses such as royalties.
  • Factory overheads are indirect factory costs: supervisor wages, factory rent, power, insurance, and depreciation of factory machinery, each adjusted for accruals and prepayments and apportioned where shared.
  • Production cost = prime cost + factory overheads + opening work in progress - closing work in progress.
  • Office costs, selling costs and carriage outwards do not belong in the manufacturing account; they go to the income statement.
  • Production cost is carried to the income statement in place of purchases.

The calculation

Production cost = Prime cost + Factory overheads + Opening WIP − Closing WIP

Prime cost = Raw materials consumed + Direct wages + Direct expenses

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find PC:PC = P + FO + opening WIP − closing WIP

To find P:P = PC − FO − opening WIP + closing WIP

  • Work in progress is adjusted after the overheads, not inside prime cost.
  • Office costs and carriage outwards go to the income statement.
  • Adjust a shared cost for an accrual or prepayment before taking the factory share.

Try itPrime cost is $155 000 and factory overheads are $74 300. Work in progress was $9 000 at the start and $11 500 at the end. Find the production cost.

  1. Prime cost plus overheads: 155 000 + 74 300 = 229 300.
  2. Add opening work in progress: 229 300 + 9 000 = 238 300.
  3. Subtract closing work in progress: 238 300 − 11 500 = 226 800.
  4. Production cost = $226 800.

5

The method

  1. Heading

    Write the name and Manufacturing account for the year ended the date.

  2. Raw materials consumed

    Opening inventory + purchases + carriage inwards - closing inventory.

  3. Prime cost

    Add direct wages and royalties, and label the subtotal.

  4. Overheads

    List each factory overhead, adjusted for accruals and prepayments, then take the factory share; include factory depreciation.

  5. Total and add

    Total the overheads and add them to prime cost.

  6. Work in progress

    Add opening and subtract closing work in progress, then label production cost.

  7. Corrections

    For a correction question, start from the draft figure and add or subtract each error.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    WIP in the wrong place

    Adjust work in progress after the overheads, opening added and closing subtracted.

  • ✗
    Office or selling costs

    Office costs and carriage outwards belong in the income statement.

  • ✗
    Supervisor and royalties swapped

    Supervisor wages are an overhead; royalties are part of prime cost.

  • ✗
    Apportioning too early

    Adjust for the accrual or prepayment first, then apportion.

  • ✗
    Finished goods inventory

    Finished goods inventories go in the income statement, not here.

What a complete response includes

Marks are given for the cost of raw materials consumed, for prime cost, for each correctly adjusted and apportioned overhead, for the work in progress adjustment, and for production cost (own figure). Tick tables give a mark per item; correction items give a mark per adjustment and one for the corrected figure.

Worked explanation

  1. Raw materials consumed: 21 200 + 198 045 - 23 600 = 195 645.
  2. Prime cost: 195 645 + 89 700 production wages + 41 300 royalties = 326 645.
  3. Rent: 46 000 x 80% = 36 800. General expenses: 36 000 x 90% = 32 400. Insurance: 7 800 x 80% = 6 240.
  4. Factory power: 38 200 paid + 3 300 owing = 41 500.
  5. Supervisor wages: 24 000 paid - 830 paid in advance = 23 170.
  6. Machinery depreciation: 15% straight line on the closing cost of 128 000 (a full year on new machines) = 19 200. Office equipment depreciation goes to the income statement.
  7. Factory overheads: 36 800 + 32 400 + 41 500 + 6 240 + 23 170 + 19 200 = 159 310.
  8. Prime cost plus overheads: 326 645 + 159 310 = 485 955.
  9. Work in progress fell from 19 100 to 18 400, so add 700: production cost 486 655.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the cost of raw materials consumed and one for prime cost. Each of the six overheads (rent, general expenses, power, insurance, supervisor's wages, machinery depreciation) earns a mark. The prime cost plus overheads subtotal is an own figure, the work in progress adjustment earns a mark, and production cost is an own figure.
