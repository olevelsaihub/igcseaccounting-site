Knowledge card

Why businesses charge depreciation

Written

Depreciation spreads the cost of a non-current asset over the years it earns revenue. It follows the accruals (matching) and prudence concepts. It does not save cash or show market value.

1

What does depreciation actually do?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 features to find

Compare charging the whole cost at once with charging depreciation each year.

  1. Year 1 hit too hard
  2. No cost in later years
  3. Cost matched to use
  4. Same total cost

Year 1 hit too hard

Problem
The whole cost falls in one year, but the van earns revenue for five years.
Effect
Profit for year 1 would be far too low.

No cost in later years

Problem
The van is still used in years 2 to 5, but no cost is charged.
Effect
Profit in those years would be overstated, which breaks prudence.

Cost matched to use

Concept
Accruals (matching): each year carries part of the cost.
Write it as
The cost is charged in the same years as the revenue the van helps to earn.

Same total cost

Point
Depreciation does not change the total cost. It changes when the cost is charged.
0 of 4 found

3 features to find

Find what depreciation does and does not tell you.

  1. Asset not overstated
  2. Not a selling price
  3. No cash saved

Asset not overstated

Concept
Prudence: the van is not shown at its full cost after two years of use.
Write it as
Depreciation stops non-current assets being overstated.

Not a selling price

Point
$18 000 is cost less depreciation. It is not what the van would sell for.

Exam tipNever write that depreciation shows the market value of the asset.

No cash saved

Point
$12 000 of depreciation has been charged, but the bank holds only $2 500.
Why
Depreciation is a book entry. It does not put money aside to replace the van.

Does it score?

Exam question

Explain, using an accounting concept, one reason for charging depreciation. (2 marks)

Decide first: would this answer earn the mark? Then tap it to check.

Tap an answer to see if it earns the mark.

4

The method

  1. Name the concept

    Choose accruals (matching) or prudence, or the one named.

  2. Accruals: link to revenue

    Say it earns revenue over several years, so its cost is spread across them.

  3. Prudence: no overstating

    Say that without depreciation, profit and asset value would be too high.

  4. Multiple choice

    Pick "spread the cost over its useful life"; reject cash and market value.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Replacement fund

    Depreciation does not save cash to replace the asset.

  • ✗
    Market value

    Carrying value is not what the asset would sell for.

  • ✗
    Concept with no link

    Explain how depreciation applies the concept, or the mark is lost.

  • ✗
    Causes instead of purpose

    Wear and tear explains falling value, not why it is charged.

What a complete response includes

In explain questions, one mark for the purpose or the concept and one for linking it to depreciation (matching cost with the years of use, or avoiding overstated profit and assets). MCQs reward the spreading-the-cost idea.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. The question wants one reason and an accounting concept. That points to 2 marks: 1 for the concept, 1 for the explanation.
  2. Choose the accruals (matching) concept.
  3. Explain what it means here: a machine or vehicle helps earn revenue for several years, so its cost should be charged as an expense across those years, not all in the year it was bought.
  4. Write it as one or two clear sentences.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Concept: the accruals (matching) concept. Reason: a non-current asset is used to earn revenue over several years, so depreciation charges part of its cost in each of those years. This matches the cost of using the asset with the revenue it helps to earn in the same period.

How the marks are earned

One mark for naming a suitable concept and one mark for explaining how depreciation applies it. Prudence is an accepted alternative: depreciation stops profit for the year and the value of non-current assets being overstated.
