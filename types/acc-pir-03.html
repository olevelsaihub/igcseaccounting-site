Why partners get interest, salaries and loan interest

Written

Each item in a partnership agreement has a purpose: interest on capital rewards investment, a salary rewards extra work, and interest on drawings discourages excess withdrawals.

1

Why is each item in the agreement?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

3 purposes to find

Find the purpose of each item in the agreement.

  1. Rewards investment
  2. Discourages drawings
  3. Rewards extra work

Rewards investment

Why
Raj put in three times Sana's capital.
Effect
Rewards each partner for capital invested, which is fair.

Discourages drawings

Why
Interest on drawings is charged to the partner.
Effect
Discourages drawings, so more cash stays in the business.

Rewards extra work

Why
Sana may run the shop day to day while Raj works part time.
Effect
A salary rewards extra work or responsibility.

3 purposes to find

Find how a partner's loan and its interest are treated.

  1. Why lend, not invest
  2. Loan interest is an expense
  3. Profit after loan interest

Why lend, not invest

Why
A loan is repaid later and earns a fixed interest rate.

Loan interest is an expense

Why
Interest is charged before profit for the year: 10 000 x 6% = 600.
Double entry
Debit the income statement, credit Sana's current account.

Exam tipNever in the appropriation account.

Profit after loan interest

Why
48 600 - 600 = 48 000, the figure shared in the appropriation account.

3 purposes to find

Find why each partnership account is needed.

  1. Shares the profit
  2. Fixed capital record
  3. Day-to-day amounts

Shares the profit

Why
More than one owner, so profit must be divided.
Effect
Shows how profit for the year is shared.

Fixed capital record

Why
Each partner's investment is kept separate.
Effect
Used for interest on capital.

Day-to-day amounts

Why
Profit shares, salaries and drawings change yearly.
Effect
Shows what the business owes each partner, apart from capital.

4

The method

  1. Name the purpose

    Name the item's purpose: reward, discourage or compensate.

  2. Add the effect

    Say what follows, for example less cash taken out means better cash flow.

  3. Chain the points

    For 3 marks, add a further link, such as better cash flow leading to better liquidity.

  4. Match the marks

    Give one valid point per mark; more words on one idea earn nothing extra.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Interest on drawings is paid

    It is charged to the partner, not paid.

  • ✗
    Loan interest as appropriation

    Interest on a partner's loan is an expense in the income statement.

  • ✗
    "To earn interest"

    Say it rewards the partner for the capital they invested.

  • ✗
    Naming accounts only

    Say why each account is needed, not just its name.

Worked explanation

  1. Purpose: charging interest makes partners think twice before taking drawings.
  2. Effect on the business: less money leaves the business, so more cash stays inside it.
  3. Further effect: with more cash available, the business can pay its debts more easily (better liquidity).
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Charging interest on drawings makes taking money out cost the partner something, so partners are discouraged from taking large drawings. As a result, more cash is kept in the business, which helps its cash flow. This in turn means the business is better able to pay its bills when they fall due, so its liquidity improves.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each valid linked point up to three: discouraging excessive drawings, keeping more cash in the business (better cash flow), and better liquidity. Other sensible points, such as making the share of profit fairer between partners who draw different amounts, are accepted.
