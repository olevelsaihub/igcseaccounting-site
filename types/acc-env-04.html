Knowledge card

Spotting the concept behind an accounting treatment

Written

Behind every accounting treatment there is a concept it follows. Ask what the treatment protects against: the owner's private spending, overstating profit, a tiny item, a changed method, the wrong year or something with no money value.

  • Business entity: owner's private items (a private lunch, a personal computer, goods or stationery taken for personal use) are treated as drawings, not business expenses.
  • Materiality: cheap items such as a calculator, a brush or stationery are treated as expenses, not non-current assets, even if they last more than a year.
  • Consistency: the same depreciation method (or other method) is used every year.
  • Prudence: inventory is valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value; provisions for irrecoverable debts are made so that assets and profit are not overstated.
  • Accruals (matching): other payables and other receivables (accruals and prepayments) are adjusted so each year carries only its own income and expenses.
  • Money measurement: things with no reliable money value, such as staff skills and knowledge, are not shown as assets.
  • Depreciation is linked to accruals, prudence and consistency; irrecoverable debts and debt provisions are linked to prudence and accruals.

4

The method

  1. Ask what it protects

    Read the treatment and ask what it is trying to achieve.

  2. Private item?

    The owner's own spending or goods taken is business entity.

  3. Cheap item?

    A low-value item treated as an expense is materiality.

  4. Same, cautious or period?

    Same method each year is consistency; lower value is prudence; owing or prepaid is accruals.

  5. Two concepts asked

    For depreciation or debt provisions, give two different concepts.

  6. Write the name

    Write the concept name exactly in the space.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Inventory valuation

    Lower of cost and net realisable value is prudence, not consistency.

  • ✗
    Cheap item

    A cheap item expensed is materiality, not business entity.

  • ✗
    Same concept twice

    Two answers must be two different concepts.

  • ✗
    Staff skills

    Skills are left out because of money measurement, not materiality.

  • ✗
    Vague words

    "Accuracy" and ethics principles are not concepts.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

One mark for each correct concept per scenario. Where an adjustment fits more than one concept (for example depreciation or a debt write-off), any of the valid concepts is accepted, but two answers must be two different concepts.

Worked explanation

  1. Row 1: the trader uses the same depreciation method each year. 'Same method every year' means consistency.
  2. Row 2: the trader adjusts for amounts paid in advance (prepayments). This makes the expense belong to the correct year, which is accruals (matching).
  3. Row 3: an item of stationery is recorded as an expense, not an asset, because it is cheap. Low value means materiality.
  4. Write the three concept names in the table.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A trader uses the same method of depreciation each year -> Consistency A trader makes an adjustment for amounts paid in advance -> Accruals (matching) A trader records the purchase of stationery as a business expense -> Materiality

How the marks are earned

One mark per correct concept, 3 marks in total. For the second row either 'accruals' or 'matching' is accepted.
