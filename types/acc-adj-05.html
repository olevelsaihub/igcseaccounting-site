Knowledge card

Provision for depreciation account

Layout

The provision for depreciation account holds accumulated depreciation, so it is a credit balance. Credit the opening balance and the charge (Income statement), debit disposals (Disposal), then balance it.

1

Read the provision account

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

5 entries to find

Find each entry and say why it is on that side.

  1. Opening balance: credit
  2. Disposal: debit
  3. Year's charge: credit
  4. Balancing figure
  5. Brought down

Opening balance: credit

Why
Depreciation charged in earlier years. The normal balance is a credit.

Disposal: debit

Why
The sold machine's depreciation leaves this account and moves to the disposal account.
Detail
Write "Disposal", the name of the other account.

Year's charge: credit

Why
The year's depreciation adds to the provision. The expense is debited in the income statement.
Detail
Write "Income statement", not "Depreciation".

Balancing figure

Working
$48 000 + $16 000 − $6 000 = $58 000
Then
Total both sides at $64 000.

Brought down

Why
The closing balance starts next year on the credit side, on the first day.

Exam tipThe balance brought down often earns its own mark. Do not leave it out.

3 entries to find

See where the $6 000 and the $16 000 come from.

  1. No charge in year of sale
  2. Sold cost taken out
  3. New machine included

No charge in year of sale

Why
The policy gives no depreciation in the year of disposal, so 2025 is not charged.

Sold cost taken out

Why
The sold machine is not owned at the year end, so it is not in the base.
Trap
10% × $180 000 = $18 000 wrongly includes it.

New machine included

Why
A full year is charged in the year of purchase.

4

The method

  1. Opening balance

    Credit Balance b/d on day one of the year.

  2. Asset sold

    Work out its depreciation to the sale date. Debit it to Disposal.

  3. Year's charge

    Work out the charge on all assets owned. Credit Income statement.

  4. Balance

    Enter the Balance c/d balance on the debit side and total.

  5. Bring down

    Show Balance b/d on the credit side on day one of next year.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong side

    The opening balance and the year's charge are both credits.

  • ✗
    Year of disposal

    Follow the policy; often no charge in the year of sale.

  • ✗
    Sold asset in the base

    Remove the sold asset's cost before working out the charge.

  • ✗
    Vague details

    Write "Income statement" or "Disposal", not "Depreciation".

What a complete response includes

Marks are given for each entry when its date, detail and amount are correct together: the opening balance, the transfer to disposal, the income statement charge and the closing balance carried and brought down. A wrong earlier figure can still earn follow-through marks.

Provision for depreciation account · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Question 18 · part (c) · 4 marks

(c) Prepare the motor vehicles – provision for depreciation account for the year ended 30 April 2024. Balance the account at this date and bring the balance down on 1 May 2024. (4)

View full original question and diagrams
Hint for part (c)

This account normally has a credit balance: opening balance b/d and the year's charge go on the credit side, depreciation on assets sold goes on the debit side as 'Disposal'. Use 'Income statement' as the detail for the year's charge, follow the time policy, and bring the balance down on the first day of next year.

Worked explanation

  1. Opening balance on 1 May 2023: $85 000 (credit).
  2. Vehicle sold on 30 September 2023: bought 31 March 2022 for $36 000, residual value $8 000, straight line 20%.
  3. Yearly charge on that vehicle = ($36 000 - $8 000) x 20% = $5 600.
  4. Years charged: year ended 30 April 2022 (year of purchase, full year) and year ended 30 April 2023. None in the year of disposal. So 2 x $5 600 = $11 200 debited to the account, detail 'Disposal'.
  5. Vehicles left all year: cost $172 000 - $36 000 = $136 000. Charge for the year = 20% x $136 000 = $27 200, credited, detail 'Income statement'.
  6. Credit side total: $85 000 + $27 200 = $112 200.
  7. Closing balance: $112 200 - $11 200 = $101 000, carried down on the debit side on 30 April 2024 and brought down on the credit side on 1 May 2024.

Motor Vehicles - Provision for Depreciation Account Debit side: 2023 Sep 30 | Disposal | $11 200 2024 Apr 30 | Balance c/d | $101 000 Total | $112 200 Credit side: 2023 May 1 | Balance b/d | $85 000 2024 Apr 30 | Income statement | $27 200 Total | $112 200 2024 May 1 | Balance b/d | $101 000 (credit)

How the marks are earned

One mark each for: the opening balance; the disposal transfer of $11 200; the income statement charge of $27 200; and the closing balance of $101 000 carried and brought down. Date, detail and amount must be correct together for each.

Calculation check

  • (36000 - 8000) × 20 ÷ 100 = 5600
  • 2 × 5600 = 11200
  • 172000 - 36000 = 136000
  • 136000 × 20 ÷ 100 = 27200
  • 85000 + 27200 = 112200
  • 112200 - 11200 = 101000
