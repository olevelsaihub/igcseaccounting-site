Knowledge card

Keeping computer accounting data safe

Written

Computer records face two dangers: being lost and being seen or changed by the wrong people. Each mark needs one different, specific measure, such as a firewall, passwords or regular backups.

  • Data can be at risk in two main ways: it can be lost or damaged, or it can be seen or changed by people who should not have access.
  • Passwords (strong, changed regularly) stop unauthorised people from logging in to the accounting system.
  • A firewall blocks unauthorised access to the computer from outside, such as from the internet.
  • Anti-virus and anti-spyware software detect and remove harmful programs that could damage or steal data.
  • Regular backups, kept in a separate place (off-site or in the cloud), mean data can be restored if it is lost.
  • Other measures include encryption, giving staff access only to the parts they need, and keeping computers in locked rooms.

The method

  1. Access, loss or both

    Decide whether the question is about stopping access, stopping loss, or both.

  2. Against access

    Choose from passwords, firewall, anti-virus software, encryption or locked rooms.

  3. Against loss

    Choose regular backups stored in a separate place, such as off-site or in the cloud.

  4. One per mark

    Write one different measure on each line, in a few words.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Same idea twice

    "Password" and "login code" are one idea, so one mark.

  • ✗
    Vague answers

    "Be careful" or "keep it safe" names no measure.

  • ✗
    A benefit instead

    Speed and accuracy are benefits of computers, not protection.

  • ✗
    Backups for access

    A backup does not stop people seeing the data.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The question wants three ways to protect data in a computerised accounting system.
  2. First, stop outsiders getting in through the network: a firewall.
  3. Second, stop harmful programs damaging or stealing data: anti-virus software.
  4. Third, stop unauthorised people using the system: strong passwords.
  5. Check the three answers are all different ideas.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1 Install a firewall 2 Use anti-virus software 3 Protect the system with strong passwords

How the marks are earned

One mark for each different protection method, 3 marks in total. Other distinct measures such as regular backups or encryption would also be acceptable.
