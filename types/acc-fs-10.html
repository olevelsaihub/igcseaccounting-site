Knowledge card

The three inventories of a manufacturer

Written

A manufacturer's inventory is at one of three stages of production: raw materials not yet used, work in progress that is partly made, and finished goods that are complete and ready to sell.

1

Which stage is each item at?

4 stages to find

Find the three stages of inventory, and where finished goods are used.

  1. Raw materials
  2. Work in progress
  3. Finished goods
  4. Where each is used

Raw materials

Meaning
Inputs bought to be turned into products, not yet used in production.
Write it as
Raw materials are the materials bought to be made into products.

Work in progress

Meaning
Products started but only partly made at the year end.
Not
It is not goods waiting to be sold.

Exam tipGoods waiting to be sold are finished goods, not work in progress.

Finished goods

Meaning
Products that are complete and ready to sell.

Where each is used

Finished goods
Used in the income statement, in cost of sales.
Raw materials and WIP
Used in the manufacturing account.

3 stages to find

Find the two reasons and the consequence that develops them.

  1. High demand
  2. Production problems
  3. The consequence

High demand

Reason
The factory cannot make enough to meet demand.
Develop
Buying in means orders can still be met.

Production problems

Reason
A breakdown stops or slows production.
Develop
Bought-in goods keep customers supplied.

The consequence

Develop
Without bought-in goods, customers may go to a competitor.

Exam tipOne mark for the reason, one for developing it with a cause or consequence.

4

The method

  1. One sentence each

    For each inventory, say what stage of production it is at.

  2. Link with whereas

    For a difference, describe both and join them with whereas.

  3. Reason, then develop

    For a 'why' question, give a reason, then add its cause or consequence.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    WIP as goods for sale

    Work in progress is partly made, not ready to sell.

  • ✗
    Any goods bought

    Raw materials are bought to be converted into products.

  • ✗
    Undeveloped reason

    Add the effect, such as orders being met.

  • ✗
    Same idea twice

    Each inventory needs its own, different description.

What a complete response includes

One mark for each correctly described inventory stage, or one mark for a reason and one for developing it. Wording does not need to be exact; any clear correct meaning is accepted.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Think of a furniture maker. Wood not yet cut is raw materials.
  2. A chair that is half built at the year end is work in progress.
  3. A finished chair waiting in the showroom is finished goods.
  4. Write one sentence for each and link them with 'whereas'.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Raw materials are the inputs bought to be turned into products and not yet put into production, whereas work in progress is products that have been started but are only partly made at the year end, and finished goods are products that are complete and ready to sell.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each inventory correctly described: raw materials as inputs to be converted, work in progress as partly made, finished goods as complete. Other clear wording is accepted.
