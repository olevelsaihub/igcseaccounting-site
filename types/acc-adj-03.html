Knowledge card

Choosing between straight line and reducing balance, and changing method

Written

Straight line charges the same each year; reducing balance charges more early on. Either changes the timing of profit and carrying value, and consistency says keep the method unless there is good reason.

1

How do the two methods compare?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 features to find

Compare the pattern of charges, and what it does to profit.

  1. Equal every year
  2. Highest in year 1
  3. Small in later years
  4. Same total, new timing

Equal every year

Working
($16 000 − $1 000) ÷ 4 years = $3 750
Effect
The expense, and so its effect on profit, is the same each year.

Highest in year 1

Working
50% × $16 000 = $8 000
Effect
A big expense in year 1, so profit for that year is lower.

Small in later years

Working
50% × $2 000 = $1 000
Effect
A small expense later, so profit in later years is higher.

Same total, new timing

Point
Over the whole life both methods charge $15 000.
Write it as
The method changes when the cost is charged, not the total.

Exam tipDo not claim one method makes the asset lose much more value in total.

4

The method

  1. Describe the pattern

    Say how each method charges: equal each year, or high then falling.

  2. Effect on profit and value

    State what the pattern does to profit and carrying value, early and later.

  3. Bring in consistency

    If a change is proposed, say it makes years hard to compare.

  4. Decide

    End with a clear decision and a short reason.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Methods mixed up

    Reducing balance falls each year; straight line stays the same.

  • ✗
    Forgetting consistency

    A proposed change always calls for consistency.

  • ✗
    No decision

    Advise and Evaluate answers need a final judgement.

  • ✗
    Total, not timing

    The method moves the charge between years, not the total.

What a complete response includes

Longer items give up to two marks for each method or effect discussed (a point plus its consequence on profit or asset value) and one mark for a decision. Short items give one mark per method named, or a concept mark plus development.

Worked explanation

  1. Shauna currently uses reducing balance and is thinking of switching to straight line.
  2. Point 1 (current method): reducing balance gives a big charge in year 1 and smaller charges each later year.
  3. Develop it: smaller charges in later years mean higher profit for the year in those years.
  4. Point 2 (proposed method): straight line gives an equal charge each year.
  5. Develop it: in later years the straight line charge would usually be higher than the reducing balance charge (it depends on the rate and the asset's life), so profit would be lower and the asset's carrying value (and net assets) would be lower.
  6. Decision: advise her to keep reducing balance (also supported by consistency, so her results stay comparable year to year).
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Reducing balance (current method): the depreciation charge is largest in the first year and then falls each year (1). So in later years the expense is smaller and profit for the year is higher (1). Straight line (proposed method): the charge is the same in every year of the asset's life (1). After the first year this charge would be bigger than under reducing balance, so profit would be lower and the carrying value of the asset, and so the net assets, would be lower (1). Advice: Shauna should not change her method. Keeping reducing balance also follows the consistency concept, so her yearly results can be compared fairly (1).

How the marks are earned

Up to two marks for discussing each method (what the charge pattern is, then its effect on profit or asset value) and one mark for the advice. Other sensible points, such as which method best matches how the asset loses value, are accepted. Advising a change could also earn the decision mark if it is justified.
