Knowledge card

Journal entries to correct errors

Layout

To correct an error, compare what was done with what should have been done, then post only the difference. If only one account is wrong, the other leg of the journal goes to the suspense account.

  • A correcting journal has the account to debit first, then the account to credit (indented), with the amount in the debit and credit columns; a narrative is only written if asked.
  • To correct an error, compare what WAS done with what SHOULD have been done, then post only the difference.
  • Errors that left the double entry balanced (omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal, compensating) are corrected without the suspense account.
  • Errors that left the trial balance out (one-sided postings, wrong amount on one side, wrong side, undercasts or overcasts) need the suspense account as one leg of the correction.
  • A complete reversal is corrected with TWICE the amount: once to cancel the wrong entry and once to make the right one.
  • An amount posted to the wrong side of an account is also corrected with twice the amount in that account, with suspense taking the other leg.
  • An original entry error is corrected for the difference between the correct and the recorded amounts, in both accounts affected.
  • Errors in individual customer or supplier names are corrected in those personal accounts (e.g. debit Parker, credit Palmer).

Putting errors right with a journal

3 features to find

Compare the three lines. Find out why the correction uses $300.

  1. Sides swapped
  2. Twice the amount
  3. No suspense needed

Sides swapped

What went wrong
Money came into the bank, so bank should be debited. Here bank was credited and commission received was debited.
Type
Complete reversal.

Twice the amount

Why 300
$150 cancels the wrong entry and another $150 makes the right one: 150 × 2 = 300.
Write it as
Debit Bank 300, credit Commission received 300.

Exam tipCorrecting a reversal with $150 only takes the accounts back to nil. Always double it.

No suspense needed

Why
Both a debit and a credit of $150 were made, so the trial balance still agreed.
Rule
Errors that left the totals equal are corrected between two ordinary accounts.

4 features to find

Find the features that make each correction earn its marks.

  1. Move the asset
  2. Named personal accounts
  3. The difference only
  4. Suspense takes the other leg

Move the asset

What it fixes
An error of principle: the van was put in an expense account.
Entry
Debit Motor vehicles 4 500, credit Motor expenses 4 500. Debit line first, credit line below.

Named personal accounts

What it fixes
Amira's account was wrongly credited, so debit Amira. Amina should have been credited, so credit Amina.
Watch
Use the customers' own names, not a general trade receivables account.

The difference only

Why 27
Wages should show 312 but shows 285: 312 − 285 = 27 too little on the debit side.
Watch
Do not post the full $312. The $285 is already there.

Suspense takes the other leg

Why
The bank entry was right. Only wages was wrong, so the trial balance did not agree.
Rule
When only one account needs changing, the other leg is Suspense.

4

The method

  1. What was done?

    Write down the entries that were actually made.

  2. What should have been done?

    Write down the correct entries next to them.

  3. Find the gap

    For each account, decide whether it must be debited or credited, and by how much, to reach the correct figure.

  4. One account wrong?

    If only one account needs changing, the other leg is the suspense account.

  5. Check the amount

    Use the difference for a wrong figure and double the amount for a reversal or a wrong-side posting.

  6. Lay it out

    Debit line first, credit line below, each amount in the right column.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Single amount for a reversal

    A reversal or wrong-side posting needs twice the amount.

  • ✗
    Suspense used wrongly

    Errors that kept the totals equal never go through suspense.

  • ✗
    Direction reversed

    Check which account is too low on the debit side. That is the one you debit.

  • ✗
    Full amount for a wrong figure

    Post only the difference between the correct and the recorded amounts.

  • ✗
    General instead of named account

    Mistakes between customers or suppliers are corrected in their own named accounts.

What a complete response includes

Usually one mark for each account named with its correct amount on the correct side, so a full correction earns two marks; where narratives are required, an extra mark for each narrative that names the error type. Table versions give one mark per correct cell.

Worked explanation

  1. Error 1: 'The motor expenses account had been undercast by $200'. Motor expenses is $200 too low on the debit side and nothing else is wrong, so debit Motor expenses 200, credit Suspense 200.
  2. Error 2: a motor vehicle, $2 000, was debited to motor expenses. Move it: debit Motor vehicles 2 000, credit Motor expenses 2 000. The trial balance was not affected, so no suspense.
  3. Error 3: a receipt from Palmer, $60, was credited to Parker. Parker's account must be put back (debit Parker 60) and Palmer's account reduced (credit Palmer 60).
  4. Error 4: wages $154 were correct in the bank account but posted to wages as $145. Wages is short by 154 - 145 = 9 on one side only: debit Wages 9, credit Suspense 9.
  5. Error 5: stationery $40 was debited to bank and credited to stationery, the reverse of correct. To cancel and redo, use 40 x 2 = 80: debit Stationery 80, credit Bank 80.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each account given with its correct amount on the correct side: two marks per error, ten in total. A complete reversal corrected with 40 instead of 80 would lose both marks for error 5. Narratives were not required here.

Calculation check

  • 154 - 145 = 9
  • 40 × 2 = 80
  • 200 + 2000 + 60 + 9 + 80 = 2349
