4

The method

  1. Quote both ratios

    Say how the current and liquid ratios changed, with figures.

  2. Use the benchmarks

    Compare with 2 : 1 and 1 : 1; a big gap means much inventory.

  3. Answer every part

    If asked, give the effect on suppliers or a way to improve liquidity.

  4. Give a judgement

    End with a judgement: liquidity improved, worsened or is satisfactory.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Numbers with no meaning

    Say what each ratio means for debts.

  • ✗
    Ignoring benchmarks

    Use 2 : 1 and 1 : 1.

  • ✗
    Liquidity is not profit

    Keep profit out of a liquidity answer.

  • ✗
    Higher is always better

    A very high ratio can mean idle inventory or cash.

What a complete response includes

Marks come for interpreting each ratio (its movement or position against the benchmark, then what that means for meeting short-term debts), for any consequence the question asks about such as the view of suppliers, and one final mark for an overall judgement. Short define, state and explain items give one mark per valid point.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Read the table: current ratio 2.8 : 1 in 2022 and 1.2 : 1 in 2023; quick (acid test) ratio 1.6 : 1 in 2022 and 0.8 : 1 in 2023.
  2. Current ratio: still above 1 : 1, so current assets just cover current liabilities, but it is far below the usual 2 : 1.
  3. Quick ratio: now below 1 : 1, so without selling inventory Louise cannot cover what she owes in the short term.
  4. Credit suppliers: Louise may pay late or struggle to pay, so suppliers may refuse more credit or stop supplying until they are paid.
  5. Conclusion: liquidity has worsened, as both ratios have fallen.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The current ratio has fallen from 2.8 : 1 to 1.2 : 1. Louise can still just cover her short-term debts with her current assets, but she is well below the usual level of 2 : 1. The quick (acid test) ratio has fallen from 1.6 : 1 to 0.8 : 1, which is below the ideal of 1 : 1. Without selling inventory she does not have enough liquid assets to pay what she owes in the short term. For credit suppliers this means Louise may find it hard to pay on time. Suppliers may refuse further credit or stop delivering goods until they have been paid. Overall, Louise's liquidity is weaker at the end of 2023 than at the end of 2022, because both ratios have fallen.

How the marks are earned

Up to two marks for explaining what each ratio shows (current ratio just covering debts, quick ratio below 1 : 1), up to two for the effect on credit suppliers (difficulty paying, so supply or credit may be stopped) and one for the conclusion that liquidity has worsened. Other sensible points are accepted.

Calculation check

  • 2.8 - 1.2 = 1.6
  • 1.6 - 0.8 = 0.8
