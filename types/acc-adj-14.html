Expense and income accounts with accruals and prepayments

Preparing an expense or income account that opens and closes with amounts owing or in advance (and unused consumables), and finding the year's charge or income.

Knowledge card

Expense and income accounts with accruals and prepayments

Layout

An expense or income account starts and ends with amounts owing or in advance, and its balancing figure is the true expense or income for the year, transferred to the income statement.

1

Read the expense and income accounts

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 entries to find

Find the four entries that turn cash paid into the year's expense.

  1. Opening amount owing
  2. Balancing figure
  3. Prepayment carried down
  4. Brought down as an asset

Opening amount owing

Why credit
Last year's unpaid insurance is a liability, so it opens on the credit side.
Effect
Part of the $3 800 paid was for last year.

Balancing figure

Working
$3 800 - $200 - $450 = $3 150.
Why it matters
This is the year's true expense, labelled 'Income statement'.

Exam tipThe income statement figure is not the cash paid.

Prepayment carried down

Why credit
A closing prepayment is carried down on the credit side of an expense account.

Brought down as an asset

Why it matters
The $450 opens next year on the debit side: an other receivable.
Rule
Bring down on the opposite side to where it was carried down.
0 of 4 found

4 entries to find

Find the four entries that show how the rules flip for an income account.

  1. Opening income owing
  2. Cash received credited
  3. Year's rent income
  4. Income in advance b/d

Opening income owing

Why debit
The tenant owed the business $550, an asset, so it opens on the debit side.

Cash received credited

Why it matters
On an income account, money received is credited.

Year's rent income

Working
$8 250 - $550 - $1 100 = $6 600.
Check
12 months x $550 = $6 600.

Income in advance b/d

Means
The business owes the tenant two months' use of the property: an other payable.

Exam tipA credit balance on an income account is a liability.

2

The calculation

Expense for the year = Cash paid - Opening owing + Opening prepaid + Closing owing - Closing prepaid

Income accounts: same pattern with cash received and in-advance balances.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

Try itWages paid $24 000. Start: $1 000 owing, $400 prepaid. End: $1 500 owing. Find the wages expense.

  1. Cash paid: $24 000.
  2. Less opening owing: $24 000 − $1 000 = $23 000.
  3. Add opening prepaid: $23 000 + $400 = $23 400.
  4. Add closing owing: $23 400 + $1 500 = $24 900.
  5. Wages expense: $24 900.

5

The method

  1. Opening balances

    Enter each Balance b/d on the first day, on the correct side.

  2. Cash entry

    Debit cash paid on an expense account; credit cash received on income. Detail: Bank.

  3. Closing balances

    Expense: owing c/d on the debit, prepaid c/d on the credit. Reverse for income.

  4. Balance and bring down

    Balance with Income statement; bring closing balances down on day one.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Opening balance on the wrong side

    Expense: owing is credit, prepaid is debit.

  • ✗
    Cash paid as the expense

    The income statement figure is the balancing figure.

  • ✗
    Income versus expense

    The sides flip for income accounts. Decide which kind first.

  • ✗
    Same side b/d

    A balance comes down on the side opposite to its c/d.

What a complete response includes

Marks for each correct opening balance, the cash entry, the closing balance carried and brought down, and the transfer to the income statement, with dates and details correct. The transfer is usually follow-through from your own entries.

Expense and income accounts with accruals and prepayments · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Question 18 · part (a) · 5 marks

(a) Prepare the wages account for the year ended 30 April 2024. Balance the account at this date and bring the balance down on 1 May 2024. (5)

View full original question and diagrams
Hint for part (a)

Enter opening balances b/d on the right side (expense: owing on credit, prepaid on debit; reverse for income), then the cash with detail 'Bank'. Carry down closing balances, find the balancing figure labelled 'Income statement', and bring balances down on the opposite side on the first day of next year.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. On 1 May 2023: wages owing $1 580 (credit Balance b/d) and wages paid in advance $920 (debit Balance b/d).
  2. During the year: paid $87 900 (debit, Cash book/Bank).
  3. On 30 April 2024: wages owing $4 230, carried down on the debit side as Balance c/d.
  4. Debit side total: $920 + $87 900 + $4 230 = $93 050.
  5. The credit side must also total $93 050. Income statement = $93 050 - $1 580 = $91 470.
  6. Check with the formula: $87 900 - $1 580 + $920 + $4 230 = $91 470.
  7. Bring down $4 230 on the credit side on 1 May 2024 (wages still owing).
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Wages Account Debit side: 2023 May 1 | Balance b/d | $920 2024 Apr 30 | Cash book (bank) | $87 900 2024 Apr 30 | Balance c/d | $4 230 Total | $93 050 Credit side: 2023 May 1 | Balance b/d | $1 580 2024 Apr 30 | Income statement | $91 470 Total | $93 050 2024 May 1 | Balance b/d | $4 230 (credit)

How the marks are earned

One mark each for the two opening balances on the correct sides, the cash book entry of $87 900, the transfer of $91 470 to the income statement (follow-through from your own entries), and the $4 230 balance carried and brought down. Date, detail and amount must be correct together. 'Bank' or 'Cash book' are both accepted.

  • 93050 - 1580 = 91470
  • 87900 - 1580 + 920 + 4230 = 91470
  • 1580 + 91470 = 93050
