Knowledge card

Preparing a trial balance

Layout

Put each balance in the debit column (assets, expenses, drawings) or the credit column (capital, liabilities, income, provisions), add both columns, and the totals should agree.

  • A trial balance lists every balance on one date in two columns: debit and credit; the two totals should be equal.
  • Debit balances are assets, expenses, drawings, purchases, returns inwards, carriage (inwards or outwards), discount allowed and opening inventory.
  • Credit balances are equity (capital), liabilities (bank loan, mortgage, bank overdraft, trade payables), revenue, other income (rent received, discount received), returns outwards and provisions (depreciation, irrecoverable debts).
  • The trade receivables ledger control balance is a debit (an asset); the trade payables ledger control balance is a credit (a liability).
  • A bank overdraft is a credit balance even though a bank balance in hand is a debit.
  • Opening inventory (inventory at the start of the year) goes in the debit column; closing inventory is not in the trial balance unless the question says so.
  • If the question tells you to enter a difference, put the missing amount in a suspense account on the side that is short, and label it 'Suspense'.
  • When redrafting an incorrect trial balance, keep the amounts and move any balance that is on the wrong side.

4 balances to find

Find the four entries students most often get wrong.

  1. Overdraft is a credit
  2. Provision is a credit
  3. Returns inwards debit
  4. Totals agree

Overdraft is a credit

Why
An overdraft is money owed to the bank, a liability.
Remember
Bank in hand is a debit; bank overdraft is a credit.

Provision is a credit

Why
Provisions for depreciation and for irrecoverable debts are credit balances.

Exam tipDo not think 'depreciation is an expense, so debit'. The provision is a credit.

Returns inwards debit

Why
Returns inwards (sales returns) reduce revenue, so they are a debit.
Compare
Returns outwards are a credit.

Totals agree

Check
Debit 30 000 + 42 000 + 700 + 9 300 = 82 000. Credit 2 400 + 12 600 + 6 000 + 61 000 = 82 000.
0 of 4 found

4 balances to find

Find where the suspense figure goes and why.

  1. Payables control credit
  2. Discount received credit
  3. Suspense on the short side
  4. Totals now match

Payables control credit

Why
The business owes its suppliers, so this is a liability.

Discount received credit

Why
Discount received is income. Discount allowed would be a debit.

Suspense on the short side

Working
Credit 60 000 - debit 59 200 = 800.
Why debit
The debit column was short, so the $800 goes there, labelled 'Suspense'.

Exam tipA figure with no label 'Suspense' may not score.

Totals now match

Check
Debit 40 000 + 18 000 + 1 200 + 800 = 60 000.

The method

  1. Classify each account

    Asset, expense or drawings: debit. Capital, liability, income or provision: credit.

  2. Copy the amount

    Write each amount in its column without changing it.

  3. Check tricky items

    Overdraft, provisions, returns, carriage, discounts and the two control accounts.

  4. Add each column

    Add the debit column and the credit column separately.

  5. Suspense if told

    Put any difference on the short side, labelled Suspense.

  6. Totals on one line

    Write both totals on the same line and check they agree.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Overdraft as a debit

    A bank overdraft is a liability: credit.

  • ✗
    Provision as a debit

    Provisions for depreciation and irrecoverable debts are credits.

  • ✗
    Returns mixed up

    Returns inwards: debit. Returns outwards: credit.

  • ✗
    Control accounts swapped

    Receivables control is a debit; payables control is a credit.

  • ✗
    Unlabelled difference

    Write 'Suspense' beside the difference, on the short side.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

Marks are given for balances placed in the correct column, usually one mark for every two balances (a pair) that are both right, plus one own-figure mark for totals that are correctly added. Where a difference is required, the suspense figure and its label earn a mark.

How the marks are earned

Ten marks come from balances placed in the correct column, awarded for pairs of consecutive lines that are both right (for example Advertising with Bank loan, Carriage inwards with Drawings). The last mark is an own-figure mark for adding both columns correctly, so a placing slip loses a pair mark but not the total mark.
