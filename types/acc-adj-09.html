Knowledge card

Double entry for irrecoverable debts and the provision

Layout

To write off a debt, debit irrecoverable debts and credit the customer (or the receivables control account). For a provision, debit the income statement and credit the provision for irrecoverable debts.

1

Which account, which side?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

3 entries to find

Find the debit, the credit and the narrative.

  1. Debit: the expense
  2. Credit: the debt goes down
  3. Narrative

Debit: the expense

Why
Irrecoverable debts is an expense account. It goes to the income statement at the year end.
Layout
The debit comes first.

Credit: the debt goes down

Why
The amount owed by customers falls by $380.
Also accepted
The customer's own account, P Mensah.

Narrative

What
One short sentence saying what happened.
When
Only when the question asks for one.

Exam tipA narrative that only repeats the account names explains nothing.

3 entries to find

See how the entry reverses when the provision goes down.

  1. Create: debit IS
  2. Credit the provision
  3. Decrease: reversed

Create: debit IS

Why
Creating a provision is an expense, so it reduces profit.

Credit the provision

Why
The provision account builds up a credit balance.
Trap
Do not credit the provision when writing off one customer's debt.

Decrease: reversed

Why
A smaller provision is needed, so the provision is debited.
Effect
The income statement is credited, so profit rises by $120.

4

The method

  1. Which event?

    Decide: a write-off of a known debt, or a change in the provision.

  2. Debit first

    Write-off: irrecoverable debts. New or bigger provision: income statement.

  3. Then the credit

    Write-off: the customer or trade receivables ledger control. Provision: provision for irrecoverable debts.

  4. Amounts and narrative

    Same amount in Dr and Cr. Add a one-sentence narrative only if asked.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Reversed entry

    Never debit trade receivables to write off a debt.

  • ✗
    Wrong credit

    A write-off credits the customer, not the provision.

  • ✗
    Missing amounts

    Each account needs its amount in the right column.

  • ✗
    Two different accounts

    Irrecoverable debts and the provision are separate accounts.

Worked explanation

  1. The debt of $450 is irrecoverable, so it must be written off on 31 March 2024.
  2. Debit: irrecoverable debts account $450 (an expense).
  3. Credit: trade receivables ledger control account $450 (the amount owed goes down).
  4. No narrative is needed.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Journal Date: 2024 March 31 Debit: Irrecoverable debts | $450 Credit: Trade receivables ledger control account | $450

How the marks are earned

One mark for irrecoverable debts debited with $450 and one for the trade receivables ledger control account credited with $450. The account and amount must be correct together.

Calculation check

  • 450 = 450
