Knowledge card

Calculating depreciation and carrying value

Calculation

Carrying value = cost − accumulated depreciation. Straight line takes the same charge every year; reducing balance takes the rate of the start-of-year carrying value, so the charge falls each year.

2

The calculation

Carrying value = Cost − Accumulated depreciation

Straight line: (cost − residual value) ÷ life. Reducing balance: rate × opening carrying value.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find Straight line charge:(C − RV) ÷ n

To find Reducing balance charge:r × CV at start of year

To find CV:C − AD

To find AD:C − CV

Try itCost $26 000, residual value $2 000, life 4 years. Straight line: carrying value after 3 years?

  1. Charge = ($26 000 − $2 000) ÷ 4 = $6 000
  2. Carrying value = $26 000 − (3 × $6 000) = $8 000

5

The method

  1. List the facts

    Note cost, residual value, rate or life, and the method.

  2. Count the years

    List each financial year from purchase to the date asked.

  3. Work each year

    Straight line: same charge each year. Reducing balance: rate × start-of-year carrying value.

  4. Total and subtract

    Add the charges for accumulated depreciation, then subtract from cost for carrying value.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Rate on cost every year

    Under reducing balance, the base falls each year.

  • ✗
    Ignoring the policy

    Check the rule for the year of purchase and the year of disposal.

  • ✗
    Forgetting residual value

    Straight line divides cost less residual value.

  • ✗
    Miscounting years

    With a 30 April year end, count financial years, not calendar years.

Worked explanation

  1. Facts: two machines bought on 1 May 2020; reducing balance at 30%; a full year's charge in the year of purchase. The year ends on 30 April.
  2. Machine A, year ended 30 April 2021: 30% x $28 000 = $8 400.
  3. Carrying value after year 1: $28 000 - $8 400 = $19 600.
  4. Machine A, year ended 30 April 2022: 30% x $19 600 = $5 880.
  5. Machine B, year ended 30 April 2021: 30% x $36 000 = $10 800.
  6. Carrying value after year 1: $36 000 - $10 800 = $25 200.
  7. Machine B, year ended 30 April 2022: 30% x $25 200 = $7 560.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Machine A (cost $28 000): year ended 30 April 2021 $8 400; year ended 30 April 2022 $5 880 Machine B (cost $36 000): year ended 30 April 2021 $10 800; year ended 30 April 2022 $7 560

How the marks are earned

One mark for each of the four correct charges. The second-year figures must use the reduced carrying value, not the original cost.

  • 19600 × 30 ÷ 100 = 5880
  • 36000 × 30 ÷ 100 = 10800
  • 36000 - 10800 = 25200
  • 25200 × 30 ÷ 100 = 7560
