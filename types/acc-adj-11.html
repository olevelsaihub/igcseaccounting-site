Knowledge card

Provision for irrecoverable debts account

Layout

The provision for irrecoverable debts account keeps a credit balance, and only the change in the provision (an increase or a decrease) is transferred to the income statement each year.

1

Read the provision account

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 entries to find

Find the four entries that make this account correct when the provision increases.

  1. Opening balance on the credit
  2. Only the increase
  3. New provision carried down
  4. Balance brought down

Opening balance on the credit

Why it matters
Last year's provision is brought down as a credit balance.
Write it as
2025 Jan 1, Balance b/d, $600 on the credit side.

Only the increase

Working
New provision 4% x $18 000 = $720. Change $720 - $600 = $120.
Why credit
An increase is an extra expense, so it is credited here and charged in the income statement.

Exam tipTransfer $120, not $720. Only the change goes to the income statement.

New provision carried down

Why it matters
The required provision is carried down on the debit side to balance the account.
Check
Both sides now total $720.

Balance brought down

Why it matters
The new provision opens next year on the credit side.
Write it as
2026 Jan 1, Balance b/d, $720.

3 entries to find

Find the entries that show a decrease in the provision.

  1. Decrease on the debit
  2. Required provision c/d
  3. Totals agree

Decrease on the debit

Working
New provision 5% x $16 000 = $800. Change $900 - $800 = $100.
Why debit
A smaller provision reduces the account, and the $100 is added to profit in the income statement.

Exam tipA decrease never goes on the credit side.

Required provision c/d

Why it matters
The closing balance is always the new required provision, $800.

Totals agree

Check
Debit $100 + $800 = $900. Credit $900. The account balances.

2

The calculation

New provision = Rate × (Trade receivables − debts written off)

Difference from last year's provision goes to the income statement.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find Change:Change = New − Old (+ increase, credit; − decrease)

Try itReceivables $18 400; $400 written off; provision 5%; last year $750. Income statement entry?

  1. Receivables: $18 400 − $400 = $18 000.
  2. New provision: 5% × $18 000 = $900.
  3. Change: $900 − $750 = $150, an increase.
  4. Credit $150 to the provision account, detail 'Income statement'.

5

The method

  1. Opening balance

    Credit last year's provision as Balance b/d.

  2. New provision and change

    Rate × receivables after write-offs. Increase: credit; decrease: debit. Detail Income statement.

  3. Carry down

    Enter the new provision as Balance c/d on the debit side and total.

  4. Bring down

    Bring the new provision down, credit side, on day one of next year.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Whole provision transferred

    Only the change goes to the income statement, not the new provision.

  • ✗
    Decrease on the credit

    A decrease is debited. Check which is bigger: old or new.

  • ✗
    Write-off here

    Written-off debts go to the irrecoverable debts account.

  • ✗
    Wrong detail

    Write 'Income statement', not 'Irrecoverable debts' or 'Bank'.

Provision for irrecoverable debts account · June 2023 · Paper 1 · Question 18 · part (c)(iii) · 4 marks

(iii) Prepare the provision for irrecoverable debts account for the year ended 30 April 2023. Balance the account on this date and bring the balance down on 1 May 2023. (4)

Hint for part (c)(iii)

Opening balance b/d goes on the credit side. Only the change in the provision goes to the 'Income statement': an increase on the credit side, a decrease on the debit side. Carry the new provision down as Balance c/d and bring it down on the first day of next year.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Opening provision on 1 May 2022: $744 on trade receivables of $18 600, so the rate is $744 / $18 600 = 4%.
  2. At 30 April 2023 trade receivables are $21 500, with $750 to be written off: $21 500 - $750 = $20 750.
  3. New provision: 4% x $20 750 = $830.
  4. Change: $830 - $744 = $86 increase, credited with the detail 'Income statement'.
  5. Credit side total: $744 + $86 = $830. Debit side: Balance c/d $830. Totals both $830.
  6. Bring down $830 on the credit side on 1 May 2023.

Provision for Irrecoverable Debts Account Debit side: 2023 Apr 30 | Balance c/d | $830 Total | $830 Credit side: 2022 May 1 | Balance b/d | $744 2023 Apr 30 | Income statement | $86 Total | $830 2023 May 1 | Balance b/d | $830 (credit)

How the marks are earned

One mark for the opening balance of $744, one for the $86 increase, one for labelling it 'Income statement', and one for the $830 balance brought down (follow-through from your own provision).

Calculation check

  • 744 ÷ 18600 × 100 = 4
  • 21500 - 750 = 20750
  • 20750 × 4 ÷ 100 = 830
  • 830 - 744 = 86
  • 744 + 86 = 830
