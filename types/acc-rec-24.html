Why the capital/revenue split matters

Written

If spending is put in the wrong group, profit for the year and non-current assets are both wrong in the same direction, so the financial statements do not give a true and fair view.

Explain why it matters to distinguish capital and revenue expenditure. (5 marks)

Tap an answer to see if it earns the mark.

4

The method

  1. Effect on profit

    Wrongly treated revenue spending misstates profit; costs go in the income statement.

  2. Effect on assets

    Say that wrong treatment of capital spending misstates non-current assets.

  3. Give an example

    A van booked as motor expenses makes profit and assets both too low.

  4. Conclude with a view

    Correct treatment matches costs with revenue: a true and fair view.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong direction

    Capital as revenue: profit too low. Revenue as capital: profit too high.

  • ✗
    Only one statement

    Cover both the income statement and the statement of financial position.

  • ✗
    No conclusion

    End with a judgement about a true and fair view.

  • ✗
    Ignoring the trader

    In an Advise question, speak to the named trader and use their assets.

What a complete response includes

Typically up to four marks for two reasons, each identified and developed (effect on profit, effect on non-current assets, matching), plus one mark for a conclusion about a true and fair view. Other sensible reasons are also accepted.

Worked explanation

  1. Reason 1: if Jay treats a revenue cost such as machinery repairs as capital, his expenses are too low, so his profit for the year is overstated.
  2. Reason 2: if Jay treats a capital cost as revenue, his non-current assets in the statement of financial position are wrong.
  3. Example: if Jay records a new machine in the repairs account, expenses rise, so profit is understated, and machinery in the statement of financial position is understated too.
  4. Conclusion: Jay must classify each cost correctly so that his financial statements show a true and fair view.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Jay, you need to separate capital and revenue spending carefully. Revenue spending, such as your machinery repairs of $1 500, is an expense of this year. If it were recorded as capital instead, your expenses would be too low and your profit for the year would be too high. Capital spending, such as buying a new machine, should be added to machinery in the statement of financial position and depreciated over its life. If you recorded a new machine in an expense account, your profit for the year would be too low and the machinery in your statement of financial position would also be too low. In conclusion, correct classification matches each cost to the years that benefit, so that your income statement and statement of financial position give a true and fair view of your business.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for identifying the effect on profit and the effect on non-current assets, development marks for explaining the direction of each error (an example such as a machine or vehicle entered as an expense is credited), and one mark for a conclusion about a true and fair view. The matching concept is accepted as an alternative development, and other sensible points are allowed.
