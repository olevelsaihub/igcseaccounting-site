4

The method

  1. Name the proposal

    Say exactly what the trader plans to do and for which records.

  2. Develop each point

    Take each benefit or drawback in turn, with its effect: which means or because.

  3. Decide

    End with a clear decision, using the trader's name and a reason.

  4. Short State items

    If only benefits are asked, list different ones briefly.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    One side only

    Only advantages in an evaluate answer caps the mark.

  • ✗
    Bare list

    Points without their effect lose the development marks.

  • ✗
    No decision

    Leaving out the decision, or giving no reason, loses the last mark.

  • ✗
    "No errors at all"

    Software cannot fix wrong figures that are typed in.

What a complete response includes

Short items give one mark per benefit. Five-mark items give one mark for identifying an advantage or disadvantage and one for developing it, up to about two marks each side, plus one mark for an overall decision. A list without development is limited to two marks.

Worked explanation

  1. The proposal: Rio is thinking of keeping his customer and supplier balances on spreadsheets.
  2. Advantage: formulae calculate balances automatically, so Rio saves time and makes fewer arithmetic mistakes.
  3. Disadvantage: Rio may not know how to use spreadsheets and may need training, which costs money and reduces profit.
  4. Decision: weigh them. The time saved each month continues for years, while training is a one-off cost, so advise him to go ahead.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A spreadsheet would let Rio set up formulae so that the balance owed by each customer and to each supplier is worked out automatically. This saves him time on repeated calculations and reduces arithmetic errors. However, Rio may not know how to set up a spreadsheet and could need training. This would take time and add to his expenses, reducing his profit. Overall, I would advise Rio to start using spreadsheets, because the time saved and greater accuracy will continue long after the one-off cost of training.

How the marks are earned

Up to 2 marks for the advantage (point plus its consequence), up to 2 marks for the disadvantage (point plus its consequence) and 1 mark for the final advice. Other valid points such as up-to-date balances or data loss risk are also accepted. Advising against is equally acceptable if it is justified.
