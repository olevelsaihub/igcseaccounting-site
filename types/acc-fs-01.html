Knowledge card

Preparing an income statement for a trader or service business

Layout

An income statement for the year shows gross profit (revenue less cost of sales) and then profit for the year (gross profit plus other income less expenses). Adjust every figure first, so each line shows only this year's income or cost.

  • An income statement covers a period (for the year ended ...) and shows gross profit and then profit for the year.
  • Revenue is sales less returns inwards; cost of sales is opening inventory plus purchases (less returns outwards, plus carriage inwards) less closing inventory.
  • Gross profit is revenue less cost of sales; profit for the year is gross profit plus other income less expenses.
  • Carriage inwards belongs in cost of sales; carriage outwards is a selling expense below gross profit.
  • An amount owing (accrual) is added to the expense paid; an amount paid in advance (prepayment) is taken off it, so only this year's cost is charged.
  • Depreciation for the year is an expense: straight line spreads cost less residual value evenly over the useful life; reducing balance applies a percentage to the carrying value (cost less accumulated depreciation).
  • Goods taken by the owner are drawings, so their cost is taken out of purchases; buying a non-current asset is capital expenditure and never an expense.
  • Irrecoverable debts and any increase in the provision for irrecoverable debts are expenses; a decrease in the provision is extra income.

Before you begin

  • Double entry and the trial balance
  • Accruals and prepayments of expenses
  • Depreciation by straight line and reducing balance
  • Irrecoverable debts and the provision for irrecoverable debts
  • Capital and revenue expenditure
  • Drawings, including goods taken by the owner

The calculation

Gross profit = Revenue − Cost of sales

Profit for the year = Gross profit + Other income − Expenses

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find GP:GP = R − COS

To find COS:COS = Opening inventory + Purchases − Returns outwards + Carriage inwards − Own-use goods − Closing inventory

  • Accrual: add it to the amount paid. Prepayment: take it off.
  • Closing inventory is deducted in cost of sales, not added.

The method

  1. Heading

    Business name, Income statement for the year ended, and the date.

  2. Adjust first

    Note every change from the additional information beside the balance it affects: owing, prepaid, depreciation, own-use goods, capital items.

  3. Revenue

    Sales less returns inwards.

  4. Cost of sales

    Opening inventory, add purchases, less returns outwards, add carriage inwards, less own-use goods, less closing inventory.

  5. Gross profit

    Revenue less cost of sales.

  6. Income and expenses

    Add other income, such as discount received, then list each adjusted expense, including depreciation, and total them.

  7. Profit for the year

    Take total expenses away and label the final line clearly.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Adjustments reversed

    Add an accrual. Take off a prepayment.

  • ✗
    Carriage in the wrong place

    Carriage inwards is in cost of sales. Carriage outwards is an expense.

  • ✗
    An asset in expenses

    A vehicle bought is not an expense. Remove it from the expense account and depreciate it instead.

  • ✗
    Reducing balance on cost

    Reducing balance uses cost less accumulated depreciation.

  • ✗
    Drawings as an expense

    Drawings and loan repayments never appear in the income statement.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

Nearly every line of the statement carries its own mark when the figure is correctly adjusted: revenue, each cost of sales item, each expense, and each depreciation charge. Adjusted expenses can be worth two marks when the working (balance plus accrual, or less prepayment) is shown. Gross profit, total expenses and profit for the year are own-figure marks, so a slip early on does not lose the later totals.

Worked explanation

  1. Heading: Tang, Income statement for the year ended 31 December 2023.
  2. Revenue is $150 000 (no returns inwards were given).
  3. Cost of sales: 8 700 + 89 000 - 4 256 (returns outwards) + 800 (carriage inwards) - 3 290 (own-use goods) - 9 345 (closing inventory) = 81 609.
  4. Gross profit: 150 000 - 81 609 = 68 391.
  5. Business rates: 1 430 paid + 570 owing = 2 000. Insurance: 3 600 - 600 prepaid = 3 000.
  6. Motor expenses: the new vehicle is capital spending, so 22 890 - 15 000 = 7 890. The vehicle cost becomes 35 000 + 15 000 = 50 000.
  7. Depreciation of fixtures and fittings: 10% x 10 000 = 1 000 (straight line, on cost).
  8. Depreciation of motor vehicles: (50 000 - 5 000) x 25% = 11 250 (reducing balance, on carrying amount).
  9. Total expenses: 2 000 + 1 300 + 1 000 + 11 250 + 5 800 + 3 000 + 650 + 7 890 = 32 890.
  10. Profit for the year: 68 391 - 32 890 = 35 501.

How the marks are earned

Revenue, purchases, returns outwards, carriage inwards, own-use goods and closing inventory each earn a mark, as does the cost of sales total; gross profit is an own-figure mark. Business rates and insurance earn two marks each (one for the balance, one for the adjustment). Carriage outwards, the two depreciation charges, general expenses, irrecoverable debts and the corrected motor expenses earn one each, then total expenses and profit for the year are own-figure marks. A vertical layout or a two-column layout are both fine as long as the figures are correct.
