Knowledge card

Defining capital and revenue expenditure

Written

Capital expenditure buys or improves non-current assets that benefit the business for more than one year; revenue expenditure pays everyday running costs, used up within the year.

Find the terms that students mix up with capital expenditure and revenue expenditure.

  2. Money spent

Capital is equity

Why it matters
Money the owner puts in is capital, not capital expenditure.

Money spent

Why it matters
Capital expenditure is money the business spends on assets.

Revenue is earned

Why it matters
Revenue is income from sales. It is not spending.

Exam tipRevenue expenditure is money spent, never money earned.

4

The method

  1. What is it spent on?

    Buying or improving non-current assets, or everyday running costs.

  2. Develop the point

    Add how long the benefit lasts, or where it is shown in the accounts.

  3. Write the differences

    Write both halves in one sentence with whereas; if asked, give an example for each.

  4. Justify a treatment

    Say the asset benefits several years; treating it as revenue would be wrong.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Capital, not capital expenditure

    Money the owner puts in is capital. Capital expenditure is spending on assets.

  • ✗
    One side only

    A difference needs both terms described.

  • ✗
    Money earned

    Revenue expenditure is money spent, not money earned.

  • ✗
    No development

    Add the time span or the statement to earn the second mark.

What a complete response includes

Usually one mark for identifying what the spending is on and one for developing it with the time span of the benefit or the statement it appears in. Differences score a mark for each side of the comparison. Explanations of a specific treatment add marks for the effect on profit and non-current assets.

Worked explanation

  1. First say what revenue expenditure is spent on: the ordinary, everyday costs of running the business, such as wages, fuel or repairs.
  2. Then develop it: the benefit is used up within the current accounting year, so the cost is charged as an expense in the income statement for that year.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Revenue expenditure is money spent on the everyday running costs of the business, such as wages, fuel and repairs. Its benefit is used up within the current year, so it is recorded as an expense in that year's income statement.

How the marks are earned

One mark for describing it as everyday running costs. One mark for the linked development: either that the benefit lasts less than a year, or that it is shown in the income statement. Either development point is accepted.
