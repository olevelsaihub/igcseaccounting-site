Knowledge card

Working out amounts after trade and cash discount

Calculation

Take trade discount off the list price first: that net amount is what goes in the books. Cash discount comes off the net amount only if the customer pays within the time allowed.

1

Which discount, and when?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 features to find

Find the figure that is recorded in the books and the line that decides the cash discount.

  1. List price
  2. Trade discount
  3. Net amount recorded
  4. Cash discount terms

List price

What it is
The catalogue price before any discount: 8 x $150 = $1 200.
Careful
This figure is never recorded in the books.

Trade discount

Working
$1 200 x 20% = $240.
Why it matters
Trade discount is always taken off first, on the invoice itself.

Net amount recorded

Working
$1 200 - $240 = $960.
Write it as
Both the seller and the buyer record $960 in their books.

Exam tipThe sales day book shows $960, not the figure after cash discount.

Cash discount terms

What it means
Pay by 18 May and take 5% off the $960.
Working
$960 x 5% = $48, so $912 is paid in time.
0 of 4 found

3 features to find

Check each date against the 14 days. When is cash discount earned?

  1. Discount earned
  2. Count the days
  3. Too late: no discount

Discount earned

Working
$960 - $48 = $912.
Why
Payment is inside the 14 days, so 5% comes off.

Count the days

How
4 May plus 14 days is 18 May. 16 May is still in time.
Habit
Always check the date before you take cash discount.

Too late: no discount

Why
25 May is after 18 May, so the full net amount is paid.
Write it as
Amount paid = $960.

Exam tipPaying late loses the cash discount, never the trade discount.

2

The calculation

Net amount = List price − Trade discount

If paid in time: amount paid = net amount − cash discount (net amount × rate).

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find N:N = LP − TD

To find CD:CD = N × cash discount %

Try itList price $2 500; trade discount 10%; cash discount 4%, paid in time. How much is paid?

  1. Trade discount = $2 500 × 10% = $250
  2. Net amount = $2 250 (recorded in the books)
  3. Paid in time, so cash discount applies
  4. Cash discount = $2 250 × 4% = $90
  5. Amount paid = $2 250 − $90 = $2 160

5

The method

  1. Trade discount first

    Take trade discount off the list price to find the net amount.

  2. Record the net amount

    Both seller and buyer record the net amount.

  3. In time or late?

    Check the terms: in time, deduct cash discount; late, pay the full net amount.

  4. Work out the rate

    For a rate, divide the discount by the amount it came from and multiply by 100.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong base

    Cash discount is on the net amount, never on the list price.

  • ✗
    Late payment

    No cash discount if payment is after the time limit.

  • ✗
    Day book figure

    Record the net amount, not the amount after cash discount.

  • ✗
    Rate on the wrong figure

    A trade discount rate uses the list price as its base.

Worked explanation

  1. From part (a), the invoice dated 1 March 2023 is 18 bikes at $300 = $5 400, less 10% trade discount of $540, total $4 860.
  2. Terms: 5% cash discount for payment within 7 days, so up to 8 March 2023.
  3. 3 March is within 7 days, so cash discount is earned.
  4. Cash discount = $4 860 x 5% = $243.
  5. Amount paid = $4 860 - $243 = $4 617.
  6. (For comparison, payment on 9 March would be too late, so the full $4 860 would be paid.)

Amount paid on 3 March 2023 = $4 860 x 95% = $4 617

How the marks are earned

  • 5400 × 0.1 = 540
