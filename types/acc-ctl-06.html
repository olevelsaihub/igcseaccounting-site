Knowledge card

Preparing a trade receivables or trade payables control account

Layout

Build a control account like a ledger: opening balances first, then each period total on its personal-account side, leave out items that do not belong, then balance it and bring down.

1

Build the control account

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 entries to find

Find the four entries that show how a payables control account works.

  1. Credit purchases credited
  2. Discount received debited
  3. Balancing figure
  4. Brought down on the credit

Credit purchases credited

Why
Credit purchases increase what Lee owes suppliers.
Source
The total comes from the purchases day book.

Discount received debited

Why
It reduces what Lee owes. Discount allowed would not belong here.

Balancing figure

Working
Credit total 14 200 + 23 500 = 37 700. Debit before balance 21 800 + 400 + 1 100 = 23 300. 37 700 - 23 300 = 14 400.

Brought down on the credit

Why
Lee owes suppliers $14 400 at the start of July.
Rule
Bring down on the opposite side to the c/d.
0 of 4 found

5 entries to find

Find the items to leave out and the two closing balances.

  1. Two opening balances
  2. Cash sales: leave out
  3. Wrong ledger
  4. Unusual balance
  5. Main balance

Two opening balances

Why
Enter the $30 400 debit and the $300 credit separately. Never net them.

Cash sales: leave out

Why
No credit customer is involved, so no debt is created.

Exam tipDistractors like this are there on purpose. Cross them out first.

Wrong ledger

Why
Discount received comes from suppliers, so it goes in payables control.

Unusual balance

Why
Hana owes this customer $250, so it is carried down on the debit side and brought down on the credit side.

Main balance

Working
Debit 30 400 + 41 000 + 250 = 71 650. Credit before balance 300 + 38 500 + 900 = 39 700. 71 650 - 39 700 = 31 950.
Then
Bring $31 950 down on the debit side on 1 April.

4

The method

  1. Draw the account

    Enter each Balance b/d separately, on its own side.

  2. Post and filter

    Post each total on its side. Ignore cash sales and the other ledger's items.

  3. Unusual balance

    Carry an unusual year-end balance down on the side opposite its b/d.

  4. Balance and bring down

    Total the bigger side, find Balance c/d, then bring both down.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Distractors included

    Cash sales and the other ledger's discounts cost marks.

  • ✗
    Netting opening balances

    Enter the debit and credit balances b/d separately.

  • ✗
    Missing unusual balance

    Without it the main balance is wrong.

  • ✗
    Same side b/d

    Bring each balance down on the side opposite its c/d.

What a complete response includes

Roughly one mark for each item correctly placed with its amount, a mark for the opening balances, one for complete dates and details, and marks for the balances carried and brought down (own-figure credit on the main balance if the earlier entries are wrong).

Preparing a trade receivables or trade payables control account · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Question 16 · part (a) · 10 marks

(a) Prepare the trade receivables ledger control account for the month of March 2024. Balance the account and bring the balances down to 1 April 2024. (10)

View full original question and diagrams
Hint for part (a)

Enter both opening balances b/d separately, cross out items that do not belong (cash sales, the other ledger's items), and post each item to the side that increases or decreases the debt. Carry down any unusual-side balance first, find the main balance, and bring both down on the next day.

Worked explanation

  1. Opening balances at 1 March 2024: debit 42 500, credit 850 - enter them separately.
  2. Leave out cash sales 5 479 (no credit customer) and discounts received 890 (that belongs to suppliers).
  3. Debit side (raises what customers owe): sales day book 51 260; refunds to credit customers 250; dishonoured cheques 431.
  4. Credit side (lowers what customers owe): receipts from credit customers 36 500; discounts allowed 650; returns inwards day book 2 345.
  5. One customer overpaid by 1 200 at 31 March, so carry 1 200 down on the debit side ('Balance c/d') and bring it down on the credit side.
  6. Debit total: 42 500 + 51 260 + 250 + 431 + 1 200 = 95 641.
  7. Credit side before the balance: 850 + 36 500 + 650 + 2 345 = 40 345. Balancing figure: 95 641 - 40 345 = 55 296 (Balance c/d on the credit side).
  8. Bring down on 1 April 2024: debit balance 55 296 and credit balance 1 200.

Trade Receivables Ledger Control Account Debit side: Mar 1 | Balance b/d | 42 500 Mar 31 | Sales day book | 51 260 Mar 31 | Cash book (refunds to customers) | 250 Mar 31 | Cash book (dishonoured cheques) | 431 Mar 31 | Balance c/d | 1 200 Total | 95 641 Apr 1 | Balance b/d | 55 296

Credit side: Mar 1 | Balance b/d | 850 Mar 31 | Cash book (receipts) | 36 500 Mar 31 | Discounts allowed | 650 Mar 31 | Sales returns day book | 2 345 Mar 31 | Balance c/d | 55 296 Total | 95 641 Apr 1 | Balance b/d | 1 200

How the marks are earned

Marks go to the opening balances, each correct entry with its amount (sales 51 260, receipts 36 500, refunds 250, discounts allowed 650, returns 2 345, dishonoured cheques 431), one mark for correct dates and details throughout, and the closing balances: 1 200 carried down and brought down on opposite sides, and 55 296 brought down (own-figure). Cash sales and discounts received earn nothing and cost marks if included.
