Knowledge card

Why keep a trial balance? Benefits and limits

Written

A trial balance checks the arithmetic of the double entry and gathers every balance in one place, but agreeing totals do not prove the books are free of errors.

  • A trial balance is a list of every ledger account balance on one date, with debit balances in one column and credit balances in the other.
  • Its main job is to test the arithmetic of the double entry: if every debit has a matching credit, the two column totals will be equal.
  • It gathers all the balances in one place, so the income statement and the statement of financial position can be drawn up quickly.
  • If the totals do not agree, the business knows at least one mistake exists and can open a suspense account while it looks for it.
  • Agreeing totals do NOT prove the books are correct: errors of omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal and compensating errors leave the totals equal.
  • A trial balance is a list, not an account, and it is not part of the double entry itself.
  • In an evaluate, discuss or advise answer you must give a benefit, a limitation where asked, and a final judgement (for example, keep preparing it).

Before you begin

  • Double entry: every transaction has an equal debit and credit
  • Which balances are debit and which are credit
  • Names of the error types that do not affect the trial balance

4

The method

  1. Read the command word

    State wants short points. Explain wants each point developed. Evaluate, Discuss and Advise need a judgement too.

  2. First benefit

    It checks the arithmetic of the double entry, because total debits should equal total credits.

  3. Second benefit

    It summarises all balances on one page, so the financial statements can be prepared quickly.

  4. Limitation

    Some errors are not revealed. Name one, such as an error of principle.

  5. Conclude

    Give a clear judgement using the trader's name, for example keep preparing it.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    "No errors"

    Agreeing totals do not prove the books are correct.

  • ✗
    Calling it an account

    A trial balance is a list of balances, not part of the double entry.

  • ✗
    Undeveloped points

    Add a 'because' or 'so that' to every point.

  • ✗
    No conclusion

    Evaluate, Discuss and Advise answers need a final judgement.

  • ✗
    One-sided answer

    If asked for advantages and disadvantages, give both.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

Short items give one mark per valid point. Longer items give one mark for each point and one for developing it (a reason or consequence), usually two points, plus one mark for a judgement when the command word is Evaluate, Discuss or Advise. Any sensible point is accepted.

Worked explanation

  1. Read the question: 'Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of preparing a trial balance.' (5 marks). Jacques is the trader.
  2. Plan 2 + 2 + 1: one developed advantage, one developed disadvantage, one conclusion.
  3. Advantage: the trial balance tests the arithmetic of the bookkeeping. Develop it: every debit should have an equal credit, so the two totals must agree.
  4. Disadvantage: equal totals do not mean the books are free of mistakes. Develop it with a named example: an error of principle, such as motor insurance posted to the motor vehicles account (error 1 in Jacques's list), leaves the totals equal.
  5. Conclusion: say what Jacques should do - keep preparing a trial balance because it is a useful check, while accepting that other checks are still needed.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Advantage: A trial balance checks the arithmetical accuracy of Jacques's double entry, because the total of all the debit balances should equal the total of all the credit balances.

Disadvantage: However, totals that agree do not prove the books are correct. Some errors do not upset the totals, for example an error of principle such as posting motor insurance to the motor vehicles account (an error of commission or omission would also be hidden).

Conclusion: On balance, Jacques should continue to prepare a trial balance, because it is a quick and important check on his records before he draws up his financial statements, even though it will not catch every error.

How the marks are earned

Up to 2 marks for the advantage (the idea plus the reason the totals must agree), up to 2 marks for the disadvantage (the idea that some errors stay hidden plus a named example of such an error), and 1 mark for a reasoned conclusion. Other valid points are accepted, for example that it summarises balances to help prepare the financial statements, or that it helps locate errors when the totals differ.
