Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Accounting · 4AC1

Calculating a revised profit

Starting from a draft profit and adding or taking away the effect of year-end adjustments (accruals, prepayments, depreciation, changes in the debt provision, goods taken by the owner) or of correcting an error, to reach the corrected profit for the year.

Type 7 5 Financial statements, ratios and manufacturing ACC-FS-04

★☆☆☆☆ · 3 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

You are given a draft profit and a list of items that were not yet accounted for or were wrong, and asked to calculate (or pick) the revised, adjusted or corrected profit.

Calculating a revised profit

Calculation

Start from the draft profit and correct it one item at a time: an item that adds an expense or removes income is subtracted, and an item that removes an expense or adds income is added.

1

Which way does each item move profit?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 adjustments to find

Find the four lines where the sign or the label decides the mark.

  1. Prepayment: add back
  2. Income in advance: subtract
  3. Own-use goods: add
  4. Label the answer

Prepayment: add back

Why
Part of the rent paid is for next year, so this year's rent expense was too high.
Sign
A smaller expense means more profit: +900.

Income in advance: subtract

Why
Commission received early belongs to next year, so this year's income was too high.
Sign
Less income means less profit: (600).

Exam tipAdding income received in advance is the most common wrong sign.

Own-use goods: add

Why
Goods taken by Lena are taken out of purchases, so cost of sales falls.
Sign
Lower cost of sales means more profit: +450.

Label the answer

Check
42 500 + 900 - 350 - 600 + 450 - 2 100 = 40 800.
Write it as
Revised profit for the year $40 800.

4 adjustments to find

Find the four notes where you must work out how much before you can adjust.

  1. Only this year's months
  2. Provision: a percentage
  3. Capital item: add back
  4. Then charge depreciation

Only this year's months

Work out
October to December is 3 of the 6 months: 1 200 x 3/6 = 600.
Sign
An extra expense: (600).

Exam tipTaking off the whole 1 200 is a common slip.

Provision: a percentage

Work out
3% x 15 000 = 450, not the full 15 000.
Sign
A new provision is an expense: (450).

Capital item: add back

Why
A machine is a non-current asset, not an expense, so repairs were overstated.
Sign
Add the 5 000 back: +5 000.

Then charge depreciation

Work out
10% x 5 000 = 500, an expense: (500).
Answer
28 600 - 600 - 450 + 5 000 - 500 = 32 050.

2

The calculation

Revised profit = Draft profit + items that raise profit − items that lower profit

Raise profit: less expense or more income. Lower profit: more expense or less income.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find RP:RP = DP + additions − deductions

To find DP:DP = RP − additions + deductions

Try itDraft profit $20 000. Wages owing $700 unrecorded. Rent $1 200 for next year charged this year. Revised profit?

  1. Draft profit: 20 000.
  2. Wages owing: 20 000 − 700 = 19 300.
  3. Rent for next year added back: 19 300 + 1 200 = 20 500.
  4. Revised profit for the year = $20 500.

5

The method

  1. Draft profit at the top

    Write the draft profit first. Every adjustment changes this figure.

  2. Decide the direction

    For each item: does it add or remove an expense, or income?

  3. Find this year's amount

    Time apportion bills crossing year end; take the percentage for a provision.

  4. Total and label

    One line each, signed; total; label Revised profit for the year.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Income in advance added

    Income received in advance belongs to next year, so subtract it.

  • ✗
    Own-use goods subtracted

    Goods for own use lower cost of sales, so add them back.

  • ✗
    Whole invoice used

    Adjust only the months that fall in this year.

  • ✗
    Full receivables as provision

    The provision is the percentage of trade receivables, not the whole balance.

What a complete response includes

Each adjustment earns a mark when both its size and its direction are right, and the final revised profit earns a mark as an own figure. In an MCQ the single mark depends on getting both the amount and the direction right.

Worked explanation

  1. Draft profit is 37 800.
  2. Rental income received in advance, 650, is next year's income, so take it off: -650.
  3. Goods taken for own use, 280: removing them from purchases makes cost of sales smaller, so profit rises: +280.
  4. Depreciation understated by 400: an extra expense, so -400.
  5. New provision for irrecoverable debts: 2% x 12 300 trade receivables = 246, an expense, so -246.
  6. Revised profit: 37 800 - 650 + 280 - 400 - 246 = 36 784.

$ Draft profit for the year 37 800 Less: Rental income in advance (650) Add: Goods for own use 280 Less: Depreciation understated (400) Less: Provision for irrecoverable debts (246) Revised profit for the year 36 784

How the marks are earned

One mark for each of the four adjustments shown with the correct amount and sign, and one mark for the revised profit of $36 784.
