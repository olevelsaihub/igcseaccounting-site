Partnership statement of financial position

Layout

A partnership's statement of financial position matches a sole trader's, except equity shows each partner's capital and current account side by side; a debit current balance is deducted.

Find how capital and current account balances are shown, including Ivo's debit balance.

  2. Debit balance deducted

Capital in columns

Why it matters
Each partner's capital account balance is shown separately, then totalled: 40 000 + 25 000 = 65 000.

Debit balance deducted

Why it matters
Ivo drew out more than he earned: a debit, taken away.
Check
6 300 - 1 200 = 5 100.

Exam tipShow a debit current account in brackets and deduct it.

Total equity

Check
65 000 + 5 100 = 70 100, and 46 300 + 23 800 = 70 100.

3 features to find

Find how each liability is classified.

  1. Partner's loan: a liability
  2. Overdraft: current
  3. It balances

Partner's loan: a liability

Why it matters
A partner's loan is owed back to the partner, so it is a liability, not equity.
Classify
Repayable after more than 12 months, so it is non-current.

Exam tipWithin 12 months, it would be current.

Overdraft: current

Why it matters
A bank overdraft is money owed to the bank, so it is a current liability, never a current asset.

It balances

Check
Total liabilities 10 000 + 9 900 = 19 900. Equity 70 100 + 19 900 = 90 000 = total assets.

2

The balance check

Total assets = Total equity + Total liabilities

Total equity = capital + current accounts (debit balances deducted).

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find TA:TA = TE + TL

To find TE:TE = TA − TL

To find TL:TL = TA − TE

Try itCapitals: Hana $40 000, Ivo $25 000. Current accounts: Hana $6 300 credit, Ivo $1 200 debit. Liabilities $19 900.

  1. Capital: 40 000 + 25 000 = 65 000.
  2. Current accounts: 6 300 - 1 200 = 5 100.
  3. Total equity: 65 000 + 5 100 = 70 100.
  4. Equity and liabilities: 70 100 + 19 900 = $90 000.

5

The method

  1. Current account balances first

    Work out each partner's closing current account balance.

  2. Assets and totals

    List assets at carrying amount and net of allowances, then total them.

  3. Equity in columns

    Show capital and current accounts for each partner, then total equity.

  4. Classify liabilities

    Classify loans by repayment date; liabilities plus equity must equal total assets.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Adding a debit balance

    A debit current account is deducted in equity.

  • ✗
    Assets at cost

    Deduct accumulated depreciation to show carrying amount.

  • ✗
    Receivables before allowance

    Deduct the allowance for doubtful debts.

  • ✗
    Partner's loan in equity

    A loan is a liability: current if repayable within 12 months, otherwise non-current.

Worked explanation

  1. Appropriation: profit $80 000; interest on drawings 5% (Victoria 1 800 x 5% = $90, Albert 1 200 x 5% = $60); interest on capital 5% (Victoria $2 500, Albert $1 500); salary Victoria $1 000 x 12 = $12 000. No loan interest is allowed by the agreement.
  2. Residual: 80 000 + 150 - 4 000 - 12 000 = $64 150; each partner gets $32 075.
  3. Victoria's current account (opening credit $3 500): 3 500 + 2 500 + 12 000 + 32 075 - 1 800 - 90 = $48 185.
  4. Albert's current account (opening debit $2 300, it sits in the debit column of the trial balance): -2 300 + 1 500 + 32 075 - 1 200 - 60 = $30 015.
  5. Non-current assets: land $100 000; fixtures 50 000 - 10 000 = $40 000; vehicles 30 000 - 15 000 = $15 000; total $155 000.
  6. Current assets: inventory $14 320; trade receivables 20 000 - 500 = $19 500; other receivables $350; petty cash $250; total $34 420. Total assets $189 420.
  7. Equity: capital 50 000 + 30 000 = $80 000; current 48 185 + 30 015 = $78 200; total $158 200.
  8. Current liabilities: partnership loan $8 000 (repayable within a year), trade payables $12 000, other payables $650, bank overdraft $10 570; total $31 220.
  9. Check: 158 200 + 31 220 = $189 420 = total assets.
