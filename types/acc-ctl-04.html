Knowledge card

Reading the balance on a control account

Written

A trade receivables control balance is normally a debit (customers owe the business); a trade payables one is normally a credit. An unusual side means the debt has been turned round.

4

The method

  1. Which account?

    Receivables means customers. Payables means suppliers.

  2. Normal balance

    Say who owes whom: customers owe the business, or the business owes suppliers.

  3. Unusual balance

    Think of the debt turned round: overpaid, paid in advance, or returned goods after paying.

  4. Short separate points

    Write each reason on its own line, one per mark.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Customers and suppliers swapped

    Check the name of the account before you write.

  • ✗
    "Total credit sales"

    A balance is an amount owed at a date, not a year's total.

  • ✗
    Wrong side asked

    Read whether the question asks about a debit or a credit balance.

  • ✗
    Netting balances

    A debit and a credit balance are brought down separately.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Saima's trade payables ledger control account has $350 on the debit side at 1 April 2024.
  2. A normal payables balance is a credit (Saima owes suppliers). A debit balance means some suppliers owe Saima.
  3. Think of ways that can happen: she paid a supplier more than the invoice; she paid a supplier before receiving the invoice or goods; she returned goods after paying for them.
  4. Write two of these as separate short reasons.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1. Saima overpaid a supplier, paying more than the amount on the invoice. 2. Saima paid a supplier in advance, before the goods were supplied.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each sensible reason, up to two. Another accepted reason is that goods were returned to the supplier after they had already been paid for.
