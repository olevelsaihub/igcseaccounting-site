Knowledge card

Stakeholders and why they read the accounts

Written

A stakeholder is anyone with an interest in how a business is doing, and each one reads the accounts to answer its own question: will I be paid, is my loan safe, is my job secure.

1

Who reads the accounts, and why?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 features to find

Find the stakeholders who are internal and the ones who are external.

  1. Owner: internal
  2. Employee: internal
  3. Bank: external
  4. Supplier: external

Owner: internal

Why
The owner is inside the business and runs it.
Interest
Is the business making a profit?

Employee: internal

Why
Employees work inside the business.
Interest
Is my job safe, and can my wages be paid?

Bank: external

Why
The bank deals with the business from outside.
Interest
Can the loan and the interest be repaid?

Supplier: external

Why
A supplier sells to the business from outside.
Interest
Will we be paid for the goods sold on credit?

Exam tipCustomers and suppliers are always external, even if they deal with the business every day.

Does it score?

Exam question

Name one stakeholder (not employees) and its interest in accounts. (2 marks)

Decide first: would this answer earn the mark? Then tap it to check.

Tap an answer to see if it earns the mark.

4

The method

  1. Have a ready list

    Owner, managers, employees, customers, suppliers, banks, investors, government, community.

  2. Inside or outside

    Owner, managers and employees are internal; everyone else is external.

  3. Find the decision

    Ask what decision this group makes. Link it to profit, cash or debt.

  4. Different groups

    Use a different stakeholder for each row; never repeat the example.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Repeating the example

    Copying the filled-in example row scores nothing.

  • ✗
    Vague interest

    "To see how it is doing" fits everyone; be specific.

  • ✗
    Mismatched interest

    Give each stakeholder its own worry, not another group's.

  • ✗
    Same group twice

    "Bank" and "lender" are the same stakeholder, so only one mark.

What a complete response includes

One mark for each different valid stakeholder. Where an interest is required, a second mark is given only when the interest is specific and fits that stakeholder. Many groups are accepted, so any sensible stakeholder with a matching reason scores.

Worked explanation

  1. The table already shows Employees with 'Security of employment', so pick two other stakeholders.
  2. Choose a supplier. A supplier sells goods to the business on credit, so it wants to know whether the business can pay what it owes.
  3. Choose a bank that has lent money. It wants to know whether the business can keep up its loan repayments and interest.
  4. Write each stakeholder in the left column and its matching interest in the right column.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Stakeholder: Supplier | Interest: Whether the business will be able to pay for the goods it has bought on credit. Stakeholder: Bank (lender) | Interest: Whether the business can afford to repay its loan and the interest on time.

How the marks are earned

Each row gains one mark for a valid stakeholder and one mark for a matching interest, so 2 + 2 = 4 marks. Other pairs are equally acceptable, for example the owner (is the business making a profit), a customer (will supplies continue), the government (profit on which tax is charged) or an investor (is my money safe).
