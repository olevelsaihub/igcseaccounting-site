Knowledge card

Discount columns in the cash book: where the totals go

Choice

Discount allowed sits on the debit side of the cash book and discount received on the credit side. Each column total is posted to the same side of its own discount account.

1

Follow the discount totals

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 features to find

Which side holds discount allowed and which holds discount received?

  1. Discount allowed
  2. Debit-side total
  3. Discount received
  4. Credit-side total

Discount allowed

Why debit?
Customers pay us, so the receipt and its discount are on the debit side.
Working
Ana owed $300: 5% = $15, so she paid $285.

Debit-side total

Working
$15 + $25 = $40.
Posting
Debit the discount allowed account with $40.

Discount received

Why credit?
We pay suppliers, so the payment and its discount are on the credit side.
Working
We owed Lux Ltd $600: 5% = $30, so we paid $570.

Credit-side total

Posting
Credit the discount received account with $30.
Careful
Do not add or net the two totals.

Exam tipThe discount columns are memorandum columns. They are never balanced.

4 features to find

Check the side of each posting. Why are the personal accounts on the other side?

  1. Debit discount allowed
  2. Credit discount received
  3. Customer credited
  4. Supplier debited

Debit discount allowed

Why
Same side as the column it came from: the debit side.
Type
Discount allowed is an expense.

Credit discount received

Why
Same side as the column it came from: the credit side.
Type
Discount received is income.

Customer credited

Why
The discount reduces what Ana owes, so her account is credited.
Pattern
Personal accounts take the opposite side to the discount account.

Supplier debited

Why
The discount reduces what we owe Lux Ltd, so its account is debited.
Check
Debits $40 + $30 = $70; credits $30 + $15 + $25 = $70.

4

The method

  1. Read the side

    Note whether the question names the debit (receipts) or credit (payments) side.

  2. Name the discount

    Debit side means discount allowed; credit side means discount received.

  3. Keep the side

    Post the total to the same side of the matching discount account.

  4. Read the right total

    If a figure is asked, take the total for the discount named. Do not net them.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Reversed side

    The credit-side total is credited to discount received, not debited.

  • ✗
    Discounts swapped

    The credit side never holds discount allowed.

  • ✗
    Part of the balance

    Discount columns are not part of the cash or bank balance.

  • ✗
    Netting the totals

    Use only the total for the discount the question asks about.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks about the credit side of the cash book. The credit side is where payments are recorded.
  2. We make payments to suppliers. When we pay early, suppliers give us discount. So the credit-side discount column is discount received.
  3. The discount column is only a memo, so its total is posted to the nominal ledger on the same side as the column: the credit side.
  4. So we credit the discount received account. That is option B.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

B Credit discounts received

How the marks are earned

One mark for choosing B. Option A has the right side but the wrong discount; option D has the right discount but the wrong side; option C is wrong on both.
