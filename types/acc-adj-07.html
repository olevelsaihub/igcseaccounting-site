Irrecoverable debts and the provision for irrecoverable debts: why and how they differ

Explaining or judging why a business allows for debts that may not be paid (usually through prudence and accruals), telling a debt known to be lost apart from an estimate of possible losses, and deciding which treatment suits a situation.

Type 8 3 Adjustments: depreciation, irrecoverable debts, accruals ACC-ADJ-07

★★★★☆ · 9 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The question asks why a business should make a provision for irrecoverable debts, how it differs from writing off a debt, or how prudence or accruals apply to either.

Knowledge card

Irrecoverable debts and the provision for irrecoverable debts: why and how they differ

Written

An irrecoverable debt is a named customer's debt that will not be paid, so it is written off. A provision for irrecoverable debts estimates debts that may not be paid. Both follow prudence.

1

Known loss or estimate?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

3 features to find

Find the known loss and the estimate.

  1. Irrecoverable debt
  2. An estimate, no names
  3. Based on what is left

Irrecoverable debt

What
A named customer, and the business is sure the money is lost.
Treatment
Write it off. It reduces profit and trade receivables.

An estimate, no names

What
Nobody knows which customers will fail to pay, only roughly how much.
Treatment
Make a provision for irrecoverable debts.

Based on what is left

Why
The written-off $600 is removed first, so the 3% is applied to $30 000.

  1. Deducted from receivables
  2. A realistic figure
  3. Not cash set aside

Deducted from receivables

Concept
Prudence: do not show assets at more than they are likely to be worth.

A realistic figure

Meaning
$29 100 is the amount Nadia expects to collect.

Not cash set aside

Point
A provision is a book entry. No money is put into the bank for it.

4

The method

  1. Which item?

    Decide: a known loss (write-off) or an estimate (provision).

  2. Name and apply the concept

    Name prudence or accruals; say its effect on profit and receivables.

  3. Both sides

    For a difference, describe the debt, then the provision, joined by whereas.

  4. Conclude

    In Discuss or Evaluate answers, finish with a clear recommendation.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    One side only

    A difference needs both items.

  • ✗
    Cash in the bank

    A provision is not money set aside.

  • ✗
    Mixing them up

    Known loss: write-off. Estimate: provision.

  • ✗
    No conclusion

    Discuss and Evaluate answers need a final judgement.

What a complete response includes

Difference questions give a mark for each side. Concept questions give a mark for naming or stating the concept and further marks for applying it to profit and trade receivables. Five-mark items give about two marks per concept or option plus one for the conclusion.

Worked explanation

  1. Romala sells only on credit, so some customers may not pay. The question asks whether she should create a provision, using concepts.
  2. Concept 1: prudence. A provision means she recognises a likely loss now.
  3. Develop: without it, profit for the year and the value of trade receivables (a current asset) would be shown too high.
  4. Concept 2: accruals (matching). The possible irrecoverable debts come from this year's credit sales.
  5. Develop: the expected loss should be charged in the same year as the revenue from those sales.
  6. Conclusion: she should create the provision, because her statements will give a more realistic view.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Prudence: creating a provision follows the prudence concept, because Romala recognises that some of her credit customers may never pay (1). Without it, both her profit for the year and her trade receivables in current assets would be overstated (1). Accruals (matching): the possible loss comes from sales made on credit this year (1), so charging the expected loss now puts the cost in the same period as the revenue it relates to (1). Conclusion: Romala should create a provision for irrecoverable debts so that her financial statements are more reliable (1).

How the marks are earned

Up to two marks for each concept (naming it and applying it to Romala's profit and receivables) and one mark for the conclusion. Other relevant points, such as the impact on decision making by users, are accepted.
