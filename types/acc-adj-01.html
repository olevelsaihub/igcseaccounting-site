Knowledge card

Why non-current assets lose value (causes of depreciation)

Written

A non-current asset loses value for four separate reasons: wear and tear, passage of time, obsolescence and depletion. Each cause you state must be a different idea.

1

Why is each asset losing value?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 causes to find

Name the cause of depreciation for each asset.

  1. Wear and tear
  2. Obsolescence
  3. Passage of time
  4. Depletion

Wear and tear

Why
The more the van is used, the more its engine and tyres wear out.
Write it as
Wear and tear through use.

Obsolescence

Why
The computer still works, but newer technology makes it out of date.
Write it as
Obsolescence (the asset becomes out of date).

Exam tip"Technological change" is a close wording of obsolescence. It counts as the same cause.

Passage of time

Why
The lease loses value as the years pass, even if the shop is hardly used.
Write it as
Passage of time.

Depletion

Why
A natural resource is used up as it is taken out of the ground.
Write it as
Depletion (the resource is used up).

4 causes to find

Which answers are real, different causes?

  1. Same idea twice
  2. A purpose, not a cause
  3. A method, not a cause
  4. A second, different cause

Same idea twice

Problem
Usage and wear and tear are one idea in different words.
Result
Lines 1 and 2 together earn only one mark.

A purpose, not a cause

Problem
Spreading the cost is why depreciation is charged, not why the asset loses value.
Result
No mark for a cause question.

A method, not a cause

Problem
Reducing balance is a way of calculating depreciation.
Result
No mark: it does not explain the fall in value.

A second, different cause

Why
Obsolescence is a separate idea from wear and tear.
Result
Lines 1 and 5 together earn both marks.

Does it score?

Exam question

State two causes of depreciation of a non-current asset. (2 marks)

Decide first: would this answer earn the mark? Then tap it to check.

Tap an answer to see if it earns the mark.

4

The method

  1. Count the causes

    See how many causes the question asks for. Each different cause earns one mark.

  2. Use the four causes

    Choose from wear and tear, passage of time, obsolescence and depletion.

  3. One per line

    Write each cause on its own numbered line. A State question needs no explanation.

  4. Check for repeats

    Make sure no two lines are the same idea in different words.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Purpose instead of cause

    "To spread the cost" is why it is charged, not a cause.

  • ✗
    Same cause twice

    Usage and wear and tear are one idea; pick a different second cause.

  • ✗
    Naming a method

    Straight line and reducing balance are methods, not causes.

  • ✗
    Too vague

    "It gets old" earns nothing. Say passage of time or wear and tear.

What a complete response includes

One mark for each separate, correct cause up to the number asked for. Close wordings of the same idea (such as technological change for obsolescence) are accepted, but they only count once.

Worked explanation

  1. The question wants two reasons, so we need two different causes.
  2. Think of a machine in a factory: it wears out as it is used. That is cause 1: wear and tear.
  3. Think of a computer: it loses value because newer models make it out of date. That is cause 2: obsolescence.
  4. Write each on its own line. No explanation is needed for 'State'.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1 Wear and tear (the asset wears out through use) 2 Obsolescence (the asset becomes out of date)

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correct, different cause, up to 2. Passage of time or depletion would also earn a mark, and technological change or economic factors are accepted for obsolescence.
