Knowledge card

Income statement from incomplete records

Layout

Without a trial balance, build the income statement from cash records: derive revenue and purchases, adjust each expense, add depreciation and leave drawings out.

1

From cash paid to the income statement

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

5 adjustments to find

Find how each cash payment is turned into the expense for the year.

  1. Prepayment deducted
  2. Accrual added
  3. Opening accrual reversed
  4. Reducing balance
  5. Drawings left out

Prepayment deducted

Why it matters
800 of the rent paid is for next year: 9 600 - 800 = 8 800.

Accrual added

Why it matters
600 of this year's wages is still owing: 21 000 + 600 = 21 600.

Opening accrual reversed

Why it matters
300 of the payment settled last year's bill: 2 400 - 300 = 2 100.

Reducing balance

Why it matters
Reducing balance is charged on carrying amount, not cost: 20 000 x 20% = 4 000.

Exam tipStraight-line depreciation is on cost; reducing balance is on carrying amount.

Drawings left out

Why it matters
Drawings are the owner taking money for private use, not a cost of running the business.

5 adjustments to find

Find how cost of sales, gross profit and profit for the year are built up.

  1. Derived purchases
  2. Cost of sales
  3. Gross profit
  4. Other income
  5. Profit for the year

Derived purchases

Working
Payments to suppliers 68 500 - opening payables 6 000 + closing payables 7 500 = 70 000.
Why it matters
Cash paid is not the same as purchases.

Cost of sales

Working
8 000 + 70 000 - 9 000 = 69 000.

Gross profit

Working
120 000 - 69 000 = 51 000.

Other income

Why it matters
Interest received is added after gross profit. It is easy to forget.

Profit for the year

Working
51 000 + 250 - 36 500 = 14 750.

2

The two key lines

Cost of sales = Opening inventory + Purchases − Closing inventory

Then gross profit = revenue − cost of sales; profit = gross profit + other income − expenses.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find COS:COS = OI + P − CI

Try itRevenue $120 000; opening inventory $8 000; purchases $70 000; closing inventory $9 000; interest $250; expenses $36 500. Find profit.

  1. Cost of sales: 8 000 + 70 000 - 9 000 = 69 000.
  2. Gross profit: 120 000 - 69 000 = 51 000.
  3. Add other income: 51 000 + 250 = 51 250.
  4. Deduct expenses: 51 250 - 36 500 = $14 750.

5

The method

  1. Derive revenue and purchases

    Use the workings to get revenue and purchases from cash records.

  2. Trading section

    Show cost of sales: opening inventory + purchases - closing inventory.

  3. Adjust each expense

    Add amounts owing, deduct prepayments, reverse opening accruals; add depreciation.

  4. Gross profit to profit

    Find gross profit, add other income, then deduct total expenses; leave drawings out.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Cash paid as purchases

    Adjust payments for opening and closing payables first.

  • ✗
    Adding a prepayment

    A closing prepayment is deducted from the expense.

  • ✗
    Reducing balance on cost

    Use carrying amount for reducing balance depreciation.

  • ✗
    Drawings as an expense

    Drawings never go in the income statement.

(b) Prepare the income statement for the year ended 30 September 2022. (12)

Worked explanation

  1. Revenue (from part a): 167 650 - 21 400 + 32 500 = $178 750.
  2. Purchases: payments 92 750 - opening payables 15 500 + closing payables 12 750 = $90 000.
  3. Cost of sales: 25 750 + 90 000 - 27 460 = $88 290; gross profit 178 750 - 88 290 = $90 460.
  4. Add interest received $110: 90 460 + 110 = $90 570.
  5. Depreciation (20% reducing balance): (50 000 - 10 000) x 20% = $8 000.
  6. Office expenses: paid 26 750 less prepaid 400 = $26 350. Wages: paid 33 000 plus owing 500 = $33 500.
  7. Total expenses: 8 000 + 26 350 + 33 500 = $67 850; profit 90 570 - 67 850 = $22 720.
  8. Drawings of $18 000 are left out because they are not an expense.

How the marks are earned

Marks go to revenue, opening inventory, purchases, closing inventory, cost of sales, gross profit, interest received, depreciation, office expenses, wages, total expenses and profit for the year. Revenue, purchases and the totals are marked on own figures from earlier workings.
