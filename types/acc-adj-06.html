Knowledge card

Disposal of non-current assets: cost, disposal and profit or loss on sale

Layout

When an asset is sold, move its cost (debit) and its depreciation to date (credit) into a disposal account, credit the proceeds, and the balancing figure is the profit or loss on disposal.

  • When an asset is sold, its cost and its accumulated depreciation are moved into a disposal account so the gain or loss can be found.
  • Disposal account debit: the asset's cost (credit the cost account, detail 'Disposal').
  • Disposal account credit: the depreciation on that asset up to the sale (debit the provision for depreciation account) and the sale proceeds (debit cash book/bank).
  • If the debit side is bigger than the credits, the balancing figure is a loss on disposal (credit side, detail 'Income statement'); if the credits are bigger, it is a profit (debit side, detail 'Income statement').
  • Quick check: profit or loss = proceeds - carrying value, where carrying value = cost - depreciation to date.
  • In the cost account: opening balance b/d (debit), purchases (debit, cash book/bank), the cost of the asset sold (credit, Disposal), then balance c/d and b/d.
  • Apply the time policy (for example, full year in year of purchase, none in year of disposal) to find depreciation to date on the asset sold.

2

The calculation

Profit or loss on disposal = Proceeds − Carrying value

Carrying value = cost − depreciation to the date of sale. A positive answer is a profit; a negative answer is a loss.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find CV:Cost − depreciation to date

To find Profit or loss:P − CV

  • Use the depreciation up to the date of sale, following the time policy.
  • Say clearly whether the figure is a profit or a loss.

Try itA machine cost $15 000. Depreciation to the date of sale is $9 000. It is sold for $7 200. Find the profit or loss.

  1. Carrying value = $15 000 − $9 000 = $6 000
  2. $7 200 − $6 000 = $1 200
  3. Proceeds are more than carrying value, so it is a profit of $1 200 (debit side of the disposal account).

5

The method

  1. Gather the facts

    Find the cost, the purchase and sale dates, and the proceeds of the asset sold.

  2. Depreciation to date

    Work it out up to the sale using the method and the time policy.

  3. Disposal entries

    Debit the cost. Credit the depreciation to date. Credit the proceeds.

  4. Balance it

    The gap is the profit (debit side) or loss (credit side), detail Income statement.

  5. Cost account

    If asked: debit the opening balance and purchases, credit the cost of the asset sold, then balance.

  6. Check

    Proceeds − carrying value should give the same profit or loss.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Profit and loss sides

    Profit is a debit and loss is a credit in the disposal account.

  • ✗
    Carrying value as one entry

    Enter cost and depreciation as two separate figures.

  • ✗
    Policy ignored

    Check which years are charged: year of purchase and year of sale.

  • ✗
    Wrong figure out of cost

    Remove the asset's cost, not its proceeds or depreciation.

  • ✗
    New purchase missed

    Add any new asset bought in the year to the cost account.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

One mark per correct figure on the correct side (cost, depreciation to date, proceeds, the profit or loss, purchases and balances) plus usually one mark for all dates and details. The profit or loss and depreciation are marked on follow-through from your own earlier figures.

Worked explanation

  1. Facts: cost account balance on 1 January 2022 is $76 600. A vehicle bought on 1 January 2020 for $18 000 was sold on 1 March 2022 for $12 000 (cheque). A new vehicle costing $21 500 was bought on 1 June 2022 (cheque). Reducing balance 25%; full year in year of purchase, none in year of disposal.
  2. Depreciation on the sold vehicle: 2020: 25% x $18 000 = $4 500. Carrying value $13 500.
  3. 2021: 25% x $13 500 = $3 375. No charge in 2022 (year of disposal).
  4. Depreciation to date = $4 500 + $3 375 = $7 875. Carrying value = $18 000 - $7 875 = $10 125.
  5. Profit on disposal = $12 000 - $10 125 = $1 875.
  6. Cost account: debit side $76 600 + $21 500 = $98 100; credit Disposal $18 000; balance c/d = $98 100 - $18 000 = $80 100.
  7. Disposal account: debit $18 000 + profit $1 875 = $19 875; credit $7 875 + $12 000 = $19 875. Both sides agree.

How the marks are earned

Cost account: marks for the opening balance, the purchase, the transfer of $18 000 to disposal and the balance of $80 100 carried and brought down. Disposal account: marks for the cost, the depreciation of $7 875, the proceeds and the profit of $1 875 (follow-through from your own depreciation figure). One more mark is for all dates and details being correct. 'Bank' or 'Cash book' are both accepted as the detail.
