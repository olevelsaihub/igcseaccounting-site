Why prepare a bank reconciliation statement?

Written

A bank reconciliation statement is prepared by the business to explain why its cash book balance differs from the bank statement. It brings the cash book up to date, reveals errors and helps to deter fraud.

4 benefits to find

Find what the reconciliation reveals to Kemal.

  1. Prepared by the business
  2. Cash book brought up to date
  3. Timing differences shown
  4. An independent check

Prepared by the business

Who
Kemal prepares it, not the bank. It is a check, not part of the double entry.

Cash book brought up to date

Benefit
The standing order and bank charges were only on the bank statement.
Develop it
Kemal can now enter them, so his cash book shows 2 140 − 300 − 25 = 1 815.

Timing differences shown

Benefit
Cheques not yet presented and money not yet credited are listed.
Develop it
Very old unpresented cheques can be spotted and followed up.

An independent check

Benefit
The bank statement is a record kept by someone else.
Develop it
Comparing the two helps to find errors and to deter or detect fraud.
Proof
1 815 + 410 − 570 = 1 655, so every difference is explained.

4

The method

  1. Read the command

    Count the benefits asked for and check whether advice is needed.

  2. Name and develop a benefit

    Say it brings the cash book up to date, showing standing orders and bank charges.

  3. A second benefit

    Choose a different one, such as errors or fraud, and develop it.

  4. Advise

    End with a clear recommendation to the named trader.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    The bank prepares it

    The business prepares the statement; the bank sends the statement.

  • ✗
    How, not why

    Do not describe how it is prepared when asked for benefits.

  • ✗
    One-word benefits

    In an explain question, each benefit needs a development.

  • ✗
    No advice

    If advice is asked for, it is a separate mark. Do not forget it.

What a complete response includes

Short items give one mark per benefit. Longer items give one mark for each benefit and one for its development (up to four) and one mark for advice or a conclusion. Any sensible benefit is accepted.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Question: explain to Numan two benefits of a monthly bank reconciliation statement and advise him whether to continue.
  2. Benefit 1: it brings the cash book up to date. Development: items such as standing orders, direct debits and bank charges appear first on the bank statement and are then entered in the cash book.
  3. Benefit 2: it reveals errors. Development: mistakes in the cash book or by the bank are found quickly so they can be put right.
  4. Advice: Numan should continue.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Benefit 1: A monthly bank reconciliation keeps Numan's cash book up to date, because comparing it with the bank statement shows items such as standing orders, direct debits and bank charges that he has not yet recorded.

Benefit 2: It helps him find errors, whether made in his own cash book or by the bank, so that they can be corrected promptly and his bank balance is accurate.

Advice: Numan should continue to prepare a bank reconciliation statement every month.

How the marks are earned

For each benefit, 1 mark for naming it and 1 for the development (four marks), plus 1 mark for the advice to continue. Other accepted benefits include deterring fraud, since the bank statement is an independent record, and spotting old unpresented cheques.
