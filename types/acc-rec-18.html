What goes in the owner's equity account

Knowing which items are posted to the owner's equity (capital) account and on which side: capital introduced, profit or loss for the year and drawings, as opposed to items that stay in the income statement.

Knowledge card

What goes in the owner's equity account

Choice

The equity account holds only four kinds of item: capital paid in and profit for the year on the credit side, drawings and a loss for the year on the debit side.

1

What reaches the equity account?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 features to find

Find the items that belong in the equity account, and the side each is on.

  1. Drawings: debit
  2. Capital paid in: credit
  3. Profit for the year
  4. Closing equity

Drawings: debit

Why it matters
Drawings reduce what the business owes the owner, so they are a debit.

Capital paid in: credit

Why it matters
Extra money from the owner increases equity, so it is a credit.

Profit for the year

Why it matters
The final figure of the income statement is credited to equity.
A loss
A loss for the year would go on the debit side.

Exam tipOnly the final profit or loss for the year reaches equity.

Closing equity

Working
Credits 40 000 + 5 000 + 13 000 = 58 000. Less drawings 9 600 = 48 400.

3 features to find

Find the lines that stay in the income statement and the one line that goes to equity.

  1. Gross profit stays
  2. Disposal stays
  3. This goes to equity

Gross profit stays

Why it matters
Gross profit is only part-way down. Expenses have not been taken off yet.
Working
80 000 − 52 000 = 28 000.

Disposal stays

Why it matters
A profit or loss on disposal is income or an expense in this statement.

This goes to equity

Working
28 000 − 16 400 + 1 400 = 13 000.
Where it goes
Credited to the equity account.

4

The method

  1. Know the four items

    Equity holds only capital paid in, profit, loss and drawings.

  2. Cross out the rest

    Strike out gross profit and any profit or loss on disposal.

  3. Pick the side

    Credit: capital paid in and profit for the year. Debit: drawings and a loss for the year.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Gross profit

    It is only part of the income statement. The final profit for the year goes to equity.

  • ✗
    Disposal figures

    Profit or loss on disposal is an income statement item.

  • ✗
    Wrong side

    Drawings and a loss are debits, never credits.

  • ✗
    Forgetting a loss

    A loss for the year is also shown in equity, on the debit side.

5 Which is shown in the equity account? A Gross profit for the year B Loss on disposal C Profit for the year D Profit on disposal

View full original question and diagrams
Hint for part (whole)

Ask what the equity account records over a year: what the owner puts in, what the business earns or loses overall, and what the owner takes out. Test each option against that, and if a side is asked, remember that anything increasing equity is a credit.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Gross profit for the year (A) is a line inside the income statement, before expenses. It is not transferred to equity.
  2. Loss on disposal (B) and profit on disposal (D) are recorded in the income statement as an expense or income.
  3. Profit for the year (C) is the final result of the income statement, and it is credited to the owner's equity account. Answer C.

C Profit for the year

How the marks are earned

One mark for C only.
