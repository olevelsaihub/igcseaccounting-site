Knowledge card

Opening journal entries and other one-off journal entries

Layout

In an opening journal entry, every asset is debited and every liability is credited. Equity is the balancing figure (assets minus liabilities), credited, so the totals are equal.

The calculation

Equity = Total assets − Total liabilities

In the journal: total debits (assets) = total credits (liabilities + equity).

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find E:E = A − L

To find A:A = L + E

Try itNadia's assets total $68 000 and her liabilities are trade payables $3 600 and a bank overdraft of $1 400. Find her equity.

  1. Total assets = 48 000 + 12 500 + 4 300 + 2 700 + 500 = $68 000
  2. Total liabilities = 3 600 + 1 400 = $5 000
  3. Equity = $68 000 − $5 000 = $63 000, credited
  4. Check: credits 5 000 + 63 000 = $68 000 = debits

5

The method

  1. Sort the items

    Put every item into assets or liabilities.

  2. Write the lines

    Assets as debit lines, liabilities and equity as credit lines, with exact names.

  3. Check the totals

    Equity = assets − liabilities; debits must equal credits. Narrative only if asked.

  4. One-off entries

    Debit the account gaining value (e.g. machinery); credit the supplier.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Overdraft as a debit

    An overdraft is money owed, so it is a credit.

  • ✗
    Missing or debited equity

    Always calculate equity and put it in the credit column.

  • ✗
    Debiting purchases

    A non-current asset bought on credit is debited to its own asset account.

  • ✗
    Owner's private money

    If the owner pays with private money, credit equity, not cash or bank.

What a complete response includes

Marks are given for correct lines (account name, amount and column together), sometimes one mark per line and sometimes one mark for a whole group of debits or credits. The equity figure earns a mark and is usually allowed as your own figure if earlier lines are wrong. A narrative earns a mark only when the question asks for one.

Worked explanation

  1. List the assets: premises $65 000, machinery $25 000, inventory $8 162, trade receivables $8 230 and petty cash $250.
  2. List the liabilities: trade payables $4 900, other payables $542 and bank overdraft $1 200. The overdraft is money owed to the bank, so it is a liability.
  3. Total assets = 65 000 + 25 000 + 8 162 + 8 230 + 250 = $106 642.
  4. Total liabilities = 4 900 + 542 + 1 200 = $6 642.
  5. Equity = 106 642 - 6 642 = $100 000. This is credited.
  6. Check: debits $106 642; credits 6 642 + 100 000 = $106 642. The entry balances.
  7. Add the narrative because the question asks for one.

Journal Date Account Debit $ Credit $ Jul 1 Premises 65 000 Machinery 25 000 Inventory 8 162 Trade receivables 8 230 Petty cash 250 Bank overdraft 1 200 Trade payables 4 900 Other payables 542 Equity 100 000 106 642 106 642 Being the assets, liabilities and owner's equity recorded at 1 July 2023 to open the books.

How the marks are earned

One mark for all five asset lines correct on the debit side. One mark for all three liability lines correct on the credit side. One mark for equity of $100 000 as a credit. One mark for a suitable narrative. Lines can be in any order; the account names 'Bank' or 'Bank overdraft' are both fine for the overdraft provided it is a credit.
