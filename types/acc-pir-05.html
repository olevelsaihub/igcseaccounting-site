Knowledge card

Partners' current and capital accounts

Layout

A partner's current account is credited with what the partner earns (interest on capital, salary, share of profit, loan interest) and debited with what they take (drawings, interest on drawings). A credit balance means the business owes the partner.

  • Each partner has a capital account (money invested, usually unchanged) and a current account (day-to-day amounts owed to or by the partner).
  • The credit side of a current account records what the partner earns: opening credit balance, interest on capital, salary, share of profit and interest on a partner's loan.
  • The debit side records what the partner takes or owes: drawings, interest on drawings, a share of loss and any opening debit balance.
  • Drawings are debited to the partner's current account.
  • A credit balance means the business owes the partner money; a debit balance means the partner has taken out more than they earned.
  • Balance the account with 'Balance c/d' on the last day and bring it down as 'Balance b/d' on the next day.

2

The closing balance

Closing balance = Opening balance + Interest on capital + Salary + Share of profit − Drawings − Interest on drawings

Treat an opening debit balance as a minus figure. Add interest on a partner's loan if there is one.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find CB:CB = OB + IOC + S + SP − D − IOD

To find D:D = OB + IOC + S + SP − IOD − CB

  • A positive answer is a credit balance; a negative answer is a debit balance.
  • Interest on drawings is subtracted, not added.

Try itAli: opening credit balance $2 000, interest on capital $3 000, share of profit $19 560, drawings $12 000, interest on drawings $600. Find the closing balance.

  1. Add the credits: 2 000 + 3 000 + 19 560 = 24 560.
  2. Add the debits: 12 000 + 600 = 12 600.
  3. Closing balance: 24 560 - 12 600 = $11 960 credit.

The method

  1. Collect the figures

    Take the partner's interest on capital, salary, share of profit and interest on drawings from the appropriation account.

  2. Opening balance

    Enter it on the credit side if the business owed the partner, or the debit side if the partner owed the business.

  3. Post the entries

    Credit interest on capital, salary, share of profit and loan interest. Debit drawings and interest on drawings.

  4. Balance c/d

    Total the bigger side and put the difference on the other side as Balance c/d, dated the last day.

  5. Balance b/d

    Bring it down on the opposite side, dated the first day of the next year.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Drawings on the credit side

    Drawings and interest on drawings are always debits.

  • ✗
    Opening balance on the wrong side

    A credit opening balance goes on the credit side.

  • ✗
    Figures not worked out

    Calculate interest on drawings and the share of profit before you start.

  • ✗
    No dates or no b/d

    Date every entry and bring the balance down on the next day.

  • ✗
    Debit balance means profit

    A debit balance means the partner drew out more than they earned.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

One mark for each entry on the correct side (opening balance, drawings, interest on drawings, interest on capital, salary, share of profit) and marks for the closing and brought-down balance, often on own figures from the appropriation account.

Worked explanation

  1. Ming's details: capital $50 000, opening current account $900 (credit), drawings $26 000.
  2. Interest on drawings at 9%: 26 000 x 9% = $2 340 (debit).
  3. Interest on capital at 8%: 50 000 x 8% = $4 000 (credit). Salary $18 000 (credit).
  4. Correct the profit: bank loan interest 10 000 x 10% x 3/12 = $250; depreciation 10 000 x 15% = $1 500; profit 42 140 - 250 - 1 500 = $40 390.
  5. Carl: interest on drawings 24 000 x 9% = $2 160; interest on capital 75 000 x 8% = $6 000.
  6. Residual: 40 390 + 2 160 + 2 340 - 6 000 - 4 000 - 18 000 = $16 890; Ming's half = $8 445 (credit).
  7. Credit total: 900 + 4 000 + 18 000 + 8 445 = $31 345. Debits so far: 26 000 + 2 340 = $28 340.
  8. Balance c/d: 31 345 - 28 340 = $3 005 on the debit side; bring it down as a credit on 1 April 2022.

How the marks are earned

Marks go to drawings, interest on drawings, the opening balance, interest on capital, salary, the share of profit (two marks) and the balance brought down. The appropriation-based figures are given own-figure credit if an earlier profit figure was wrong. Details may say 'Appropriation' instead of the item's name.

Calculation check

  • 50000 × 0.08 = 4000

  • 42140 - 250 - 1500 = 40390
  • 24000 × 0.09 = 2160
  • 75000 × 0.08 = 6000
  • 40390 + 2160 + 2340 - 6000 - 4000 - 18000 = 16890
  • 16890 ÷ 2 = 8445
  • 900 + 4000 + 18000 + 8445 = 31345
  • 26000 + 2340 = 28340
  • 31345 - 28340 = 3005
