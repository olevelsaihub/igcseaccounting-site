Knowledge card

Partners' current and capital accounts

Layout

A current account is credited with what the partner earns (interest, salary, profit share) and debited with drawings and interest on drawings. A credit balance means the business owes the partner.

1

Which side does each item go on?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

5 entries to find

Find what goes on the debit side, what goes on the credit side, and how the account is balanced.

  1. Opening credit balance
  2. Drawings are debits
  3. Earnings are credits
  4. Balance c/d
  5. Balance b/d

Opening credit balance

Why it matters
The business owed Ali 2 000 at the start, so it is a credit.
Date
1 January 2025.

Drawings are debits

Why it matters
Drawings reduce what the business owes Ali.
Also debit
Interest on drawings: 12 000 x 5% = 600.

Earnings are credits

Why it matters
Interest on capital and profit share are earned from the appropriation account.

Balance c/d

Working
Credits 2 000 + 3 000 + 19 560 = 24 560. Debits 12 000 + 600 = 12 600. 24 560 - 12 600 = 11 960.
Date
31 December 2025.

Balance b/d

Why it matters
Brought down on the credit side on 1 January 2026: the business owes Ali 11 960.

Exam tipThe balance c/d and the balance b/d sit on opposite sides.

2

The closing balance

Closing balance = Opening + Interest on capital + Salary + Share of profit − Drawings − Interest on drawings

Debit opening balances are minus; add loan interest.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

  • Positive is credit; negative is debit.
  • Drawings interest is subtracted.

Try itAli: opening credit $2 000; capital interest $3 000; profit share $19 560; drawings $12 000; drawings interest $600.

  1. Credits: 24 560.
  2. Debits: 12 600.
  3. Closing: 24 560 - 12 600 = $11 960 credit.

5

The method

  1. Collect the figures

    Take interest, salary and profit share from the appropriation account.

  2. Opening balance

    Credit side if the business owes the partner; debit side if the partner owes it.

  3. Post the entries

    Credit earnings; debit drawings and interest on drawings.

  4. Balance c/d and b/d

    Total both sides; the difference is the Balance c/d, then bring down b/d.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Drawings on the credit side

    Drawings and interest on drawings are always debits.

  • ✗
    Opening balance on the wrong side

    A credit opening balance goes on the credit side.

  • ✗
    No dates or no b/d

    Date every entry and bring the balance down on the next day.

  • ✗
    Debit balance means profit

    A debit balance means the partner drew out more than they earned.

What a complete response includes

One mark for each entry on the correct side (opening balance, drawings, interest on drawings, interest on capital, salary, share of profit) and marks for the closing and brought-down balance, often on own figures from the appropriation account.

Partners' current and capital accounts · June 2022 · Paper 2 · Question 2 · part (b)(i) · 8 marks

(b) (i) Prepare the current account of Ming for the year ended 31 March 2022. Balance the account on this date and bring the balance down on 1 April 2022. (8)

View full original question and diagrams
Hint for part (b)(i)

Take each figure from the appropriation account first. Credit what the partner earns (interest on capital, salary, share of profit), debit drawings and interest on drawings, put the opening balance on its correct side, then balance with dates and bring the balance down.

Worked explanation

  1. Ming's details: capital $50 000, opening current account $900 (credit), drawings $26 000.
  2. Interest on drawings at 9%: 26 000 x 9% = $2 340 (debit).
  3. Interest on capital at 8%: 50 000 x 8% = $4 000 (credit). Salary $18 000 (credit).
  4. Correct the profit: bank loan interest 10 000 x 10% x 3/12 = $250; depreciation 10 000 x 15% = $1 500; profit 42 140 - 250 - 1 500 = $40 390.
  5. Carl: interest on drawings 24 000 x 9% = $2 160; interest on capital 75 000 x 8% = $6 000.
  6. Residual: 40 390 + 2 160 + 2 340 - 6 000 - 4 000 - 18 000 = $16 890; Ming's half = $8 445 (credit).
  7. Credit total: 900 + 4 000 + 18 000 + 8 445 = $31 345. Debits so far: 26 000 + 2 340 = $28 340.
  8. Balance c/d: 31 345 - 28 340 = $3 005 on the debit side; bring it down as a credit on 1 April 2022.

Current Account - Ming Debit side 2022 31 March Drawings 26 000 2022 31 March Interest on drawings 2 340 2022 31 March Balance c/d 3 005 31 345 Credit side 2021 1 April Balance b/d 900 2022 31 March Interest on capital 4 000 2022 31 March Salary 18 000 2022 31 March Share of profit 8 445 31 345 2022 1 April Balance b/d 3 005

How the marks are earned

Marks go to drawings, interest on drawings, the opening balance, interest on capital, salary, the share of profit (two marks) and the balance brought down. The appropriation-based figures are given own-figure credit if an earlier profit figure was wrong. Details may say 'Appropriation' instead of the item's name.

Calculation check

  • 26000 × 0.09 = 2340
  • 50000 × 0.08 = 4000
  • 10000 × 0.10 × 3 ÷ 12 = 250
  • 10000 × 0.15 = 1500
  • 42140 - 250 - 1500 = 40390
  • 24000 × 0.09 = 2160
  • 75000 × 0.08 = 6000
  • 40390 + 2160 + 2340 - 6000 - 4000 - 18000 = 16890
  • 16890 ÷ 2 = 8445
  • 900 + 4000 + 18000 + 8445 = 31345
  • 26000 + 2340 = 28340
  • 31345 - 28340 = 3005
