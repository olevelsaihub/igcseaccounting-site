Knowledge card

Which items go in a control account, and on which side

Written

A control account mirrors the personal accounts: what increases customers' debt is a debit in receivables control; what reduces it is a credit. Items with no credit customer or supplier get no entry.

1

Debit, credit or no entry?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

5 items to find

Find the five items that test whether you really know the rules.

  1. Discount allowed: credit
  2. Dishonoured cheque: debit
  3. Contra: credit
  4. Cash sales: no entry
  5. Wrong ledger

Discount allowed: credit

Why
The customer is allowed to pay less, so the debt falls.

Dishonoured cheque: debit

Why
The customer's cheque was not paid by the bank, so the debt is put back.

Exam tipIt is not a receipt. It reverses one.

Contra: credit

Why
The firm is both a customer and a supplier. Setting off the balances reduces what it owes us.

Cash sales: no entry

Why
The customer pays at once, so no debt is created.

Wrong ledger

Why
Discount received comes from suppliers, so it belongs to payables control.

4 items to find

Find the items where the sides flip compared with receivables.

  1. Credit purchases: credit
  2. Interest: credit
  3. Contra: debit
  4. Provision: no entry

Credit purchases: credit

Why
The business now owes the supplier more.

Interest: credit

Why
Interest charged increases what the business owes.

Exam tipInterest charged never reduces the debt.

Contra: debit

Why
The set-off reduces what the business owes the supplier.
Pair
Debit in payables control, credit in receivables control.

Provision: no entry

Why
A provision is an estimate. It is never entered in a control account.

4

The method

  1. Which account?

    Receivables control (customers) or payables control (suppliers)?

  2. Credit customer or supplier?

    If the item is a cash transaction or belongs to the other ledger, it is no entry.

  3. More or less owed?

    Receivables: increase is debit, decrease is credit. Payables: the reverse.

  4. Re-check unusual items

    Contra, interest, dishonoured cheque and refunds.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Cash sales or purchases

    They create no debt, so they are never in a control account.

  • ✗
    Contra on the debit

    In receivables control, a contra is a credit.

  • ✗
    Interest reduces the debt

    Interest charged increases what is owed.

  • ✗
    Dishonoured cheque as a receipt

    It reinstates the debt, so it is a debit in receivables control.

Worked explanation

  1. The account is the trade receivables control account, which summarises what credit customers owe.
  2. Cash sales: the customer pays at once, so no debt is created - No entry.
  3. Contra to trade payables control: part of what a customer owes is cancelled against what we owe them - the debt falls - Credit side.
  4. Discount allowed: we let a customer pay less - the debt falls - Credit side.
  5. Interest charged to customer: the customer now owes more - Debit side.
  6. Returns outwards: these are goods we send back to suppliers, so they belong to payables - No entry.
