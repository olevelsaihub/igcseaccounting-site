Knowledge card

Preparing a three-column cash book

Layout

A three-column cash book has discount, cash and bank columns: receipts go on the debit side, payments on the credit side. Cash and bank are balanced separately; discount columns are only totalled.

Discount allowed

Working
Tariq owed $1 200 and paid early: 5% = $60.
Where
Debit discount column, on the same line as the receipt.

Amount actually received

Working
$1 200 - $60 = $1 140 into the bank.
Careful
Not the full $1 200 owed.

Contra: bank side

What happened
$800 cash was paid into the bank.
Entry
Debit bank, details Cash. The other half is on the credit side.

Discount: totalled only

Why
Discount columns are not part of the cash book's double entry.
Next step
The $60 total is posted to discount allowed.

Balances b/d

Working
Cash: 1 070 - 895 = $175. Bank: 1 940 - 1 780 = $160.
Where
Debit side, dated 1 June; both in funds.

5 entries to find

Find the overdraft, the other half of the contra, the dishonoured cheque and the discount received.

  1. Opening overdraft
  2. Contra: cash side
  3. Discount received
  4. Dishonoured cheque
  5. Balances c/d

Opening overdraft

Why
An overdraft is money owed to the bank.
Where
Credit side of the bank column.

Exam tipAn opening overdraft on the debit side is a common lost mark.

Contra: cash side

What happened
Cash left the till to go into the bank.
Entry
Credit cash, details Bank.

Discount received

Working
Invoice $800 less 5% = $40 off, so $760 was paid.
Where
Credit discount column, on the same line as the payment.

Dishonoured cheque

Why
Una's cheque was not paid, so the money is taken back out of the bank.
Where
Credit side, bank column, in the customer's name.

Balances c/d

Working
Bank: 1 940 - (600 + 760 + 300 + 120) = $160.
Check
Each column now totals the same on both sides.

4

The method

  1. Opening balances

    Cash debit; bank debit if in funds, credit if overdrawn.

  2. Receipts and payments

    Receipts debit, payments credit; put any discount on the amount actually paid.

  3. Contra entries

    Enter on both sides: bank in the cash column, cash in the bank column.

  4. Balance cash and bank

    Total the discount columns, but do not balance them; balance cash and bank separately.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Overdraft on the debit side

    An opening overdraft goes on the credit side.

  • ✗
    Full invoice amount

    Record the amount paid after discount, not the amount owed.

  • ✗
    Discount mistakes

    Allowed on the debit side, received on the credit side, and never balanced.

  • ✗
    Dishonoured cheque

    It is a payment out of the bank, not a receipt.

Worked explanation

  1. Aug 1: cash $112 on the debit side; bank overdraft $425 on the credit side.
  2. Aug 2: paid ROH $494 by cheque after 5% discount. $494 is 95% of the invoice, so the invoice was $494 / 0.95 = $520 and discount received is $520 - $494 = $26. Credit side: discount 26, bank 494.
  3. Aug 5: cash sales $835, debit cash column.
  4. Aug 8: Slater's cheque $320 dishonoured, credit bank column (money taken back out).
  5. Aug 13: cash paid into the bank $795 is a contra: debit bank 'Cash' $795 and credit cash 'Bank' $795.
  6. Aug 15: Alzate pays an invoice of $980 less 5%. Discount allowed = $49; amount received = $931. Debit side: discount 49, bank 931.
  7. Aug 21: standing order to NY Electric $220, credit bank.
  8. Aug 28: cleaner paid $35, credit cash.
  9. Cash: debit 112 + 835 = 947; credit 795 + 35 = 830; balance c/d = $117.
  10. Bank: debit 795 + 931 = 1 726; credit 425 + 494 + 320 + 220 = 1 459; balance c/d = $267.
  11. Discount totals: allowed $49, received $26. Bring down cash $117 and bank $267 on 1 September.
