Knowledge card

Writing up day books and returns books

Layout

A day book lists credit sales, purchases or returns, one line each, at the net amount after trade discount. Cash items and cash discount never go in a day book. Each book is totalled at the month end.

1

From transactions to day books

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 features to find

Which items go in the sales day book, which in the sales returns day book, and which in neither?

  1. Net of trade discount
  2. Cash sale: not here
  3. Return at the same rate
  4. Cash discount: not here

Net of trade discount

Working
$900 - $90 = $810.
Where
Sales day book, at $810.

Cash sale: not here

Why
Money moved now, so it is not a credit sale.
Where
Cash book, not the sales day book.

Return at the same rate

Working
Amal had 10% trade discount, so $100 - $10 = $90.
Where
Sales returns day book, at $90.

Cash discount: not here

Why
Cash discount is recorded in the cash book when payment is made.
Careful
It never changes a day book figure.

4 features to find

Check each line has a date, a name and the net amount.

  1. A complete line
  2. No trade discount
  3. 20% trade discount
  4. Dated total

A complete line

What to see
Date, customer's name and net amount on one line.
Why
The line is marked as a whole.

No trade discount

Why
Bo was given no trade discount, so the list price is the net amount.
Write it as
Jun 14, Bo, $600.

20% trade discount

Working
$1 500 - $300 = $1 200.
Careful
Not the $1 500 list price.

Dated total

Working
810 + 600 + 1 200 = $2 610.
Next step
The total is posted to the sales account in the ledger.

Exam tipAlways date the total. It earns a mark on its own.

Return at net value

Why
The return reverses part of Amal's original net sale.
Working
List $100 less the original 10% = $90.

Customer's name

Why
The details name the customer whose account will change.

4

The method

  1. Pick the credit items

    Choose only credit transactions for each book; leave out cash items.

  2. Net of trade discount

    Work out list price less trade discount for each line.

  3. Handle returns

    Value a return at list price less the same trade discount as the original batch.

  4. Write and total

    Date, name and net amount; total each book on the last day with "Total for month".

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    List price

    Enter the net amount, not the list price.

  • ✗
    Returns at list price

    Take off the trade discount given on the original sale or purchase.

  • ✗
    Cash items

    Cash sales and cash purchases go in the cash book.

  • ✗
    Cash discount

    Never deduct cash discount in a day book.

Writing up day books and returns books · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Regional (R) · Question 12 · part (whole) · 5 marks

12 George is in business selling computer equipment. During April 2024 the following transactions with suppliers took place. April Transaction Purchased goods at list price $1 000 less 20% trade discount from White. Purchased goods at list price $750 from Black. Purchased goods at list price $3 000 less 10% trade discount from Green. Returned goods list price $200 to White. These goods were from the batch received on April 10 Prepare the purchases day book and the purchases returns day book for April 2024. Total each book at the end of the month. Purchases Day Book Date Supplier $ Purchases Returns Day Book Date Supplier $

View full original question and diagrams
Hint for part (whole)

Include only credit transactions for goods, one line each with date, name and net amount after trade discount. Value returns using the original trade discount, do not deduct cash discount, and total the book at the month end.

Worked explanation

  1. April 10 White: $1 000 less 20% = $1 000 - $200 = $800.
  2. April 17 Black: $750 with no trade discount = $750.
  3. April 22 Green: $3 000 less 10% = $3 000 - $300 = $2 700.
  4. Purchases day book total: 800 + 750 + 2 700 = $4 250.
  5. April 28 return to White: list $200 less the 20% from the April 10 batch = $200 - $40 = $160.
  6. Purchases returns day book total: $160.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each of the three purchases lines and one for the returns line, each needing date, name and amount. The totals carry a mark on your own figures.

Calculation check

  • 1000 - 1000 × 0.2 = 800
  • 3000 - 3000 × 0.1 = 2700
  • 800 + 750 + 2700 = 4250
  • 200 - 200 × 0.2 = 160
