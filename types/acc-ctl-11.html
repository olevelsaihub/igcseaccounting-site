Knowledge card

Bank terms and ways of paying

Written

Each bank term is defined by who starts the payment, whether the amount is fixed or can vary, and what has or has not been recorded yet. A bank statement is written from the bank's side: credit means money in the bank, debit means an overdraft.

  • Standing order: the payer instructs the bank to pay a fixed amount to someone at regular intervals.
  • Direct debit: the payer authorises the receiver (payee) to collect payments from the account; the amounts and dates can vary.
  • Credit transfer (bank giro credit / electronic transfer): money paid straight into the business's bank account by someone else.
  • Dishonoured cheque: a cheque the bank refuses to pay, e.g. not enough money in the drawer's account, unsigned, out of date, or words and figures differ.
  • Unpresented cheque: a cheque written and entered in the cash book that has not yet been paid out by the bank.
  • Outstanding (uncredited) lodgement: money paid into the bank and entered in the cash book that the bank has not yet recorded.
  • From the bank's point of view: a credit balance on the bank statement means the bank owes the business (money in the bank); a debit balance means an overdraft, so the bank is a creditor of the business.

4

The method

  1. Name the term

    Check exactly which term is asked about. Standing order and direct debit are easy to swap.

  2. Who starts it?

    Payer for a standing order, payee for a direct debit.

  3. Fixed or variable?

    Fixed for a standing order, can vary for a direct debit.

  4. Timing terms: both halves

    Say what is in the cash book and what the bank has not yet done.

  5. Read the balance

    On a bank statement, Cr means money in the bank and Dr means overdrawn.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Direct debit as fixed

    A fixed amount set by the payer is a standing order, not a direct debit.

  • ✗
    Timing terms swapped

    Unpresented cheques are money going out. Outstanding lodgements are money coming in.

  • ✗
    Credit read as overdraft

    The statement is from the bank's side. Credit means the bank owes the business.

  • ✗
    One vague word

    A payment or a cheque is not a definition. Include the key feature.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks for two features of a direct debit (2 marks).
  2. Feature 1: the amount (and date) can change from one payment to the next.
  3. Feature 2: the payment is started by the payee - the supplier collects the money using the authority the payer has given.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1. The amount paid can vary each time. 2. The payment is started by the payee (the business receiving the money), not by the payer.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the variable amount and one mark for the payment being started by the payee. Saying it is used for regular, recurring payments is also accepted as a feature.
