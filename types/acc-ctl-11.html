Knowledge card

Bank terms and ways of paying

Written

Each bank term is defined by who starts the payment, whether the amount is fixed, and what is not yet recorded. A bank statement is written from the bank's side: credit means money in, debit an overdraft.

1

Reading the bank's words

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

5 terms to find

Find each type of payment or receipt, and read the closing balance.

  1. Standing order
  2. Direct debit
  3. Credit transfer
  4. Dishonoured cheque
  5. Dr means overdrawn

Standing order

Who starts it
Rosa, the payer, tells her bank to pay.
Amount
A fixed amount at regular intervals, here rent each month.

Direct debit

Who starts it
The electricity company, the payee, collects the money using Rosa's authority.
Amount
It can vary each time, as electricity use changes.

Exam tipFixed and started by the payer is a standing order. Variable and collected by the payee is a direct debit.

Credit transfer

Meaning
Lina paid $240 straight into Rosa's bank account.

Dishonoured cheque

Meaning
Omar's bank refused to pay his cheque, so the $95 is taken back out.
Reasons
Not enough money in Omar's account, unsigned, out of date, or words and figures differ.

Dr means overdrawn

Check
707 − 900 = 193 below nil.
Meaning
From the bank's side, Rosa owes the bank $193. She has an overdraft.

3 terms to find

Find which item is an unpresented cheque and which is an outstanding lodgement.

  1. Unpresented cheque
  2. Outstanding lodgement
  3. Bank-only item

Unpresented cheque

Meaning
Rosa has written the cheque and entered it in her cash book, but the bank has not yet paid it out.

Outstanding lodgement

Meaning
The money is paid in and entered in the cash book, but the bank has not yet recorded it.
Also called
An uncredited lodgement.

Bank-only item

Meaning
Only the bank knew about this. It is not a timing difference.
Next step
Rosa enters it in her cash book.

4

The method

  1. Name the term

    Check which term is asked; standing order and direct debit are easily swapped.

  2. Who starts it, fixed or variable?

    Payer starts a standing order (fixed); payee starts a direct debit (can vary).

  3. Timing terms: both halves

    Say what is in the cash book and what the bank has not yet done.

  4. Read the balance

    On a bank statement, Cr means money in and Dr means overdrawn.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Direct debit as fixed

    A payer-set fixed amount is a standing order.

  • ✗
    Timing terms swapped

    Unpresented cheques go out; outstanding lodgements come in.

  • ✗
    Credit read as overdraft

    From the bank's side: credit means the bank owes the business.

  • ✗
    One vague word

    A payment or a cheque is not a definition. Include the key feature.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks for two features of a direct debit (2 marks).
  2. Feature 1: the amount (and date) can change from one payment to the next.
  3. Feature 2: the payment is started by the payee - the supplier collects the money using the authority the payer has given.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1. The amount paid can vary each time. 2. The payment is started by the payee (the business receiving the money), not by the payer.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the variable amount and one mark for the payment being started by the payee. Saying it is used for regular, recurring payments is also accepted as a feature.
