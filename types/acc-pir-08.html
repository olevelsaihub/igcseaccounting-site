Finding missing sales, purchases and balances

Calculation

Missing credit sales or credit purchases are the balancing figure: start with cash received or paid, deduct the opening balance and add the closing balance.

Find how the credit sales figure is worked out from the other entries.

  1. Opening balance
  2. Receipts from the bank
  3. Other reductions
  4. Balancing figure

Opening balance

Why it matters
These customers owed Omar before the year began, so this is not this year's sales.

Receipts from the bank

Why it matters
Receipts come from the bank summary, not the income statement.

Other reductions

Why it matters
Discounts allowed and irrecoverable debts also reduce what customers owe, so they go on the credit side too.

Exam tipLeaving out discounts or irrecoverable debts makes credit sales too low.

4 features to find

Find each step from payments to suppliers to total purchases.

  1. Deduct opening payables
  2. Add closing payables
  3. Credit purchases
  4. Total needs cash purchases

Deduct opening payables

Why it matters
Part of this year's payments settled last year's purchases.

Add closing payables

Why it matters
Goods bought this year but not yet paid for are still this year's purchases.

Credit purchases

Check
64 300 + 1 200 - 9 800 + 11 400 = 67 100.

Total needs cash purchases

Check
67 100 + 3 500 = 70 600.
Why it matters
Total purchases = credit purchases + cash purchases.

2

The calculation

Credit sales = Receipts from customers − Opening trade receivables + Closing trade receivables

Also add discounts allowed, returns and irrecoverable debts; purchases work the same way.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find CS:CS = R − OTR + CTR

Try itReceipts $48 000. Receivables: opening $6 000, closing $7 500. Cash sales $4 000. Find total sales.

  1. Receipts: 48 000.
  2. Less opening receivables: 42 000.
  3. Add closing receivables: 49 500 credit sales.
  4. Add cash sales: 49 500 + 4 000 = $53 500 total sales.

5

The method

  1. Picture the account

    Draw a receivables (or payables) account, or a short working list.

  2. Enter what you know

    Enter opening balance, cash, and any discounts, returns or irrecoverable debts.

  3. Balancing figure

    Add the closing balance c/d; the balancing figure is credit sales or purchases.

  4. Totals if asked

    Add cash sales or cash purchases to get total sales or total purchases.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Adding the opening balance

    Opening receivables or payables are deducted.

  • ✗
    Wrong cash figure

    Use receipts from customers for sales, payments to suppliers for purchases.

  • ✗
    Missing discounts or returns

    They also reduce receivables or payables, so include them.

  • ✗
    No cash sales or purchases

    A total needs both credit and cash amounts.

Worked explanation

  1. Receipts from trade receivables in the bank summary: $167 650.
  2. Opening trade receivables $21 400 were owed before the year, so they are deducted.
  3. Closing trade receivables $32 500 are sales not yet paid for, so they are added.
  4. Credit sales = 167 650 - 21 400 + 32 500 = $178 750.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Credit sales for the year ended 30 September 2022 $ Receipts from trade receivables 167 650 Less opening trade receivables (21 400) Add closing trade receivables 32 500 Credit sales 178 750

How the marks are earned

One mark for deducting the opening trade receivables, one for adding the closing trade receivables, and one for the answer (own figure allowed). The correct figure alone earns all three marks. A trade receivables control account giving the same balancing figure is equally acceptable.
