Knowledge card

Normal balances: debit side or credit side

Choice

Assets, expenses, drawings and returns inwards have debit balances; liabilities, income, equity, returns outwards and provisions have credit balances.

1

Debit side or credit side?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

5 balances to find

Find the balances that students most often put in the wrong column.

  1. Both carriages: debit
  2. Returns inwards: debit
  3. Returns outwards: credit
  4. Provision: credit
  5. Equity: credit

Both carriages: debit

Why it matters
Carriage outwards is the cost of delivering to customers. It is an expense, so debit.

Exam tip"Outwards" does not mean credit. Both carriages are expenses.

Returns inwards: debit

Why it matters
Goods coming back from customers reduce sales, so the balance is a debit.

Returns outwards: credit

Why it matters
Goods sent back to suppliers reduce purchases, so the balance is a credit.

Provision: credit

Why it matters
A provision is a credit balance even though it relates to an asset.

Equity: credit

Why it matters
Equity is what the business owes the owner, so it is a credit.

3 balances to find

Find what each cash or bank balance means.

  1. Cash in hand
  2. Money in the bank
  3. Credit bank: overdraft

Cash in hand

Why it matters
Cash is an asset. A debit balance is the cash held.
Note
Cash can never be overdrawn, so it is never a credit balance.

Money in the bank

Why it matters
A debit bank balance is an asset: money held in the bank.

Credit bank: overdraft

Why it matters
A credit bank balance means the business owes the bank. It is a liability.

Exam tipDo not read a credit bank balance as money in the bank.

4

The method

  1. Classify the account

    Asset, liability, equity, income, expense, drawings, return or provision? Tick one column.

  2. Apply the rule

    Debit: assets, expenses, drawings, returns inwards. Credit: the rest.

  3. Check the traps

    Carriages are debit; discount allowed debit, discount received credit.

  4. Read cash and bank

    A debit balance is money held. A credit bank balance is an overdraft.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Carriage outwards

    It is an expense, so it is a debit, despite the word "outwards".

  • ✗
    Returns mixed up

    Returns inwards are debit; returns outwards are credit.

  • ✗
    Provisions as debit

    Provisions for depreciation and irrecoverable debts are credit balances.

  • ✗
    Credit bank balance

    It means an overdraft, not money in the bank.

Hint for part (whole)

Classify each account first, then remember assets, expenses and drawings are debit; liabilities, equity, income and provisions are credit. Do not judge by words like 'out' or 'in' in the name; tick one column per row.

Worked explanation

  1. Carriage inwards is the cost of bringing goods in: an expense, so debit.
  2. Carriage outwards is the cost of delivering goods to customers: also an expense, so debit.
  3. Equity is what the business owes the owner: credit.
  4. Other receivables (for example a prepaid expense) are amounts due to the business: an asset, so debit.
  5. Other payables (for example an accrued expense) are amounts the business owes: a liability, so credit.
