Knowledge card

The imprest system: topping up petty cash

Calculation

Under the imprest system the petty cashier starts each period with a fixed float. At the end, the main cashier pays in exactly the amount spent, so the cash goes back to the full float.

1

Topping up the float

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 features to find

Find the amount spent, the cash left and the top-up. Which two are the same?

  1. The fixed float
  2. Amount spent
  3. Cash left: not the answer
  4. Top-up = amount spent

The fixed float

What it is
The same amount the petty cashier starts with every period.
Write it as
The imprest is $300.

Amount spent

Working
$45 + $62 + $18 = $125.
Why it matters
This is exactly what must be paid back in.

Cash left: not the answer

Working
$300 - $125 = $175.
Careful
The cash left is not the top-up.

Exam tipIf you only know the cash left, top-up = float minus cash left.

Top-up = amount spent

Working
$175 + $125 = $300, the float again.
Write it as
The main cashier restores the float with $125.
0 of 4 found

2 features to find

Which account is debited and which is credited?

  1. Debit petty cash
  2. Credit bank

Debit petty cash

Why
Petty cash receives the money.
Write it as
Debit petty cash $125.

Credit bank

Why
Money leaves the bank.
Where
It is on the credit (payments) side of the main cash book.

Exam tipReversing the entry is a common error. Petty cash is debited because it gains cash.

2

The calculation

Top-up = Float − Cash left = Amount spent

The amount restored always equals what was spent in the period.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find T:T = S = F − C

To find S:S = F − C

To find C:C = F − S

Try itThe float is $300. Vouchers this week are $45, $62 and $18. How much is needed to restore the float?

  1. Amount spent = $45 + $62 + $18 = $125
  2. Cash left = $300 − $125 = $175
  3. Top-up = amount spent = $125
  4. Check: $175 + $125 = $300, the float

5

The method

  1. Find the spending

    Add the payments, or take the cash left from the float.

  2. That is the top-up

    The top-up equals the amount spent, so the balance returns to the float.

  3. Debit petty cash

    Petty cash receives money, so it is debited.

  4. Credit bank

    Money leaves the bank, so it is credited on the payments side of the main cash book.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Cash left

    Do not give the cash left as the top-up.

  • ✗
    Adding to the float

    Float plus spending is never the answer.

  • ✗
    Reversed entry

    Debit petty cash and credit bank, not the other way round.

  • ✗
    Wrong side

    The top-up is a payment in the main cash book, on the credit side.

10 A business maintains a petty cash book with a float of $250. During the month the total expenses paid from petty cash were $180 Which amount is required to restore the float? A $70 B $180 C $250 D $430

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. The float is $250 and $180 has been spent.
  2. Cash left in the box = $250 - $180 = $70.
  3. To restore the float we need $250 again: $70 + top-up = $250, so top-up = $180.
  4. This equals the amount spent, as the imprest rule says. Answer B.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

B $180

How the marks are earned

One mark for B. $70 is the cash left, $250 is the float, and $430 wrongly adds spending to the float.

Calculation check

  • 250 - 180 = 70
  • 250 - 70 = 180
