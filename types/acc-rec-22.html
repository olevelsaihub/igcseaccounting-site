Knowledge card

Is it capital or revenue expenditure?

Written

Capital expenditure buys a non-current asset, gets it ready for use or improves it. Revenue expenditure pays for running the business and keeping assets working.

1

Capital or revenue?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

5 items to find

Find the costs that are capital and the ones that are revenue.

  1. Getting it ready
  2. First-time fitting
  3. Running cost
  4. Future repair cover
  5. Add each group

Getting it ready

Why it matters
Delivery is needed before the van can be used, so it is part of its cost.

First-time fitting

Why it matters
The first sign on a new van gets it ready for use, so it is capital.
Compare
Repainting the sign later would be revenue.

Running cost

Why it matters
Insurance is a running cost, even though it was paid on the day the van was bought.

Exam tipPaying a cost at the same time as buying the asset does not make it capital.

Future repair cover

Why it matters
A warranty pays for repairs while the van is used, so it is revenue.

Add each group

Capital
24 000 + 350 + 600 = 24 950.
Revenue
180 + 900 + 400 = 1 480.

4 items to find

Find the pairs that look alike but are treated differently.

  1. Part of buying it
  2. Cost of borrowing
  3. Improvement
  4. Restoring, not improving

Part of buying it

Why it matters
Legal fees are part of the cost of getting the premises.

Cost of borrowing

Why it matters
Interest is paid every year for the loan, not for the building, so it is revenue.

Improvement

Why it matters
An extension makes the asset bigger and better, so it is capital.

Restoring, not improving

Why it matters
A repair puts the roof back to how it was, so it is revenue.

Exam tipRepairs and repainting are revenue, even though they keep the asset's value.

4

The method

  1. Buy, prepare or improve?

    If the cost buys, gets ready or improves a non-current asset, it is capital.

  2. Run or repair?

    If it is a cost of using, running or repairing, it is revenue.

  3. Watch the pairs

    New van's sign is capital; repainting is revenue.

  4. Totals with workings

    List the capital items and add them; then revenue. Tick one column per item.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Delivery as revenue

    Delivery and installation of a new asset are capital.

  • ✗
    Same-day costs

    Fuel or insurance paid with the purchase is still revenue.

  • ✗
    Loan interest

    Interest is a cost of borrowing, so it is revenue.

  • ✗
    Repairs as capital

    Repairs and repainting restore, they do not improve. They are revenue.

Worked explanation

  1. Cost of motor vehicle: buying a non-current asset, so capital.
  2. Delivery cost: needed to get the vehicle to the business ready for use, so capital.
  3. Extended warranty: pays for future repair cover while the vehicle is used; it is a running cost, so revenue.
  4. Fuel: used up in running the vehicle, so revenue.
  5. Insurance: a regular running cost, so revenue.
