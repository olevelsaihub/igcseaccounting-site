Knowledge card

Preparing a manufacturer's income statement

Layout

A manufacturer's income statement uses production cost in place of purchases and only finished goods inventories. Shared costs are adjusted first, then apportioned, and only the office share comes into the income statement.

  • A manufacturer's income statement uses production cost (from the manufacturing account) in place of purchases.
  • Cost of sales = opening inventory of finished goods + production cost + any finished goods bought in - closing inventory of finished goods.
  • Only finished goods inventories go in the income statement; raw materials and work in progress stay in the manufacturing account.
  • Shared costs (rent, insurance, electricity, depreciation) are apportioned: the factory share goes to the manufacturing account and the office share to the income statement.
  • Office expenses are adjusted for accruals and prepayments before they are apportioned.
  • Administration wages, carriage outwards and selling costs are income statement expenses; production and supervisor wages are not.
  • Gross profit = revenue - cost of sales; profit for the year = gross profit - administrative and selling expenses.

Find the lines that make a manufacturer's cost of sales different.

  1. Finished goods only
  2. Production cost
  3. Goods bought in
  4. Gross profit

Finished goods only

Why
Raw materials and work in progress stay in the manufacturing account. Only finished goods are sold.

Production cost

Why
It takes the place of purchases. It is carried across from the manufacturing account.
Scores
It earns credit even if your manufacturing account figure was wrong.

Goods bought in

Why
Finished goods bought from another firm are part of the cost of the goods sold.

Exam tipThis line is easy to miss. Look for finished goods purchased in the trial balance.

The calculation

Cost of sales = Opening finished goods + Production cost + Finished goods purchased − Closing finished goods

Office share of a shared cost = (Amount paid + Accrual − Prepayment) × Office percentage

  • Never use raw material or work in progress inventories here.
  • Adjust a shared cost for amounts owing or prepaid before you take the office share.

5

The method

  1. Heading

    Business name and Income statement for the year ended with the date.

  2. Revenue

    Sales less any returns inwards.

  3. Cost of sales

    Opening finished goods, add production cost and any finished goods purchased, less closing finished goods.

  4. Gross profit

    Revenue less cost of sales.

  5. Adjust, then apportion

    Add amounts owing or take off prepayments, then take the office share of each shared cost.

  6. Depreciation

    Work out the year's charge, then include only the office share.

  7. Profit for the year

    Total the administrative and selling expenses and take them from gross profit.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong inventories

    Raw materials and work in progress belong in the manufacturing account.

  • ✗
    Bought-in goods missed

    Add finished goods purchased in cost of sales.

  • ✗
    Full or factory share charged

    Only the office share appears here.

  • ✗
    Apportioned too early

    Adjust for owing or prepaid amounts before splitting.

  • ✗
    Production wages

    Factory and supervisors' wages are not income statement expenses.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

Marks are given for revenue, the production cost brought across (own figure), the finished goods purchased, the closing finished goods inventory and the cost of sales total, then gross profit as an own figure. Each correctly adjusted and apportioned office expense earns a mark, and profit for the year is an own-figure mark.

Worked explanation

  1. Production cost comes from part (a): raw materials used 12 630 + 164 830 - 16 350 = 161 110; prime cost 161 110 + 94 100 + 6 000 = 261 210; factory overheads 90 092; less increase in work in progress 1 420; production cost 349 882.
  2. Cost of sales: 20 500 + 349 882 + 14 000 - 31 110 = 353 272.
  3. Gross profit: 502 500 - 353 272 = 149 228.
  4. Electricity: (12 600 + 1 400 owing) x 20% = 2 800.
  5. Insurance: 12 000 x 20% = 2 400.
  6. Rent: (53 500 - 8 900 prepaid) x 20% = 8 920.
  7. Depreciation: (145 800 - 53 200) x 15% = 13 890; office share 13 890 x 20% = 2 778.
  8. Expenses: 19 600 + 3 180 + 2 800 + 2 400 + 8 920 + 2 778 + 29 600 = 69 278.
  9. Profit for the year: 149 228 - 69 278 = 79 950.

How the marks are earned

Revenue, the production cost carried from part (a) (own figure), the finished goods purchased, the closing finished goods inventory and the cost of sales total earn a mark each, and gross profit is an own-figure mark. Carriage outwards, the office shares of electricity, insurance, rent and depreciation, and administrative wages earn a mark each. Profit for the year is an own-figure mark, so a wrong production cost from part (a) can still score fully here.
