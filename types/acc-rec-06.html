Knowledge card

Choosing the book of original entry

Written

Every transaction starts in a book of prime entry. Ask: does money move now (cash or petty cash book)? Is it goods on credit or their returns (a day book)? If neither, use the journal.

5 books to find

For each transaction, decide the book of prime entry before you check.

  1. Credit sale of goods
  2. Non-current asset on credit
  3. Small cash expense
  4. Return to a supplier
  5. Cash sale

Credit sale of goods

Test
No money moves yet, and these are goods for resale sold on credit.
Write it as
Sales day book.

Non-current asset on credit

Test
No money moves, and a van is not goods for resale.
Write it as
Journal.

Exam tipThe purchases day book is only for goods bought to sell again.

Small cash expense

Test
Money moves, but it is a small payment from the petty cash float.
Write it as
Petty cash book.

Return to a supplier

Test
Goods we bought on credit are going back out.
Write it as
Purchases returns day book.

Cash sale

Test
Money moves now, so it is not a credit sale.
Write it as
Cash book, not the sales day book.

4 books to find

Which of these go in the cash book and which in the journal?

  1. Discounts
  2. Irrecoverable debt
  3. Return from a customer
  4. Correction of an error

Discounts

Why
Cash discount is first recorded in the discount column when the payment is made.
Careful
Both discount allowed and discount received start in the cash book.

Irrecoverable debt

Test
No money moves and no goods change hands.
Write it as
Journal.

Return from a customer

Test
Goods we sold on credit are coming back in.
Write it as
Sales returns day book.

Exam tipSales returns come in from customers. Purchases returns go out to suppliers.

Correction of an error

Why
The journal holds anything that fits no other book.
Other examples
Opening entries and year-end transfers.

4

The method

  1. Does money move now?

    Bank or cash moves: cash book, or petty cash book for small cash.

  2. Goods on credit?

    Goods for resale on credit: the right day book, named in full.

  3. Returns to book

    From customers: sales returns; to suppliers: purchases returns.

  4. Otherwise, the journal

    Non-current assets on credit, write-offs, corrections and openings: journal.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Assets on credit

    A van or machine bought on credit goes in the journal, not the purchases day book.

  • ✗
    Cash sales

    A cash sale goes in the cash book, not the sales day book.

  • ✗
    Discounts in cash book

    Discount allowed and received start in the cash book, not the journal.

  • ✗
    "Day book" alone

    Always say which day book.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Purchase of a non-current asset on credit: no money moves and it is not goods for resale, so it goes in the journal.
  2. Receipt from customer by credit transfer: money arrives in the bank, so the cash book.
  3. Return of goods to a credit supplier: a return of credit purchases, so the purchases returns day book.
  4. Sale of goods on credit: credit goods sold, so the sales day book.
  5. Small cash payment to window cleaner: a small cash expense, so the petty cash book.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correct book, five in all. Shortened names such as 'purchases returns' or 'petty cash' are accepted.
