Knowledge card

The accounting equation and the dual effect

Calculation

The accounting equation says Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Every transaction has a dual effect: two items change and the equation still balances.

1

Does the equation balance?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 features to find

Find the features that show which side of the equation each item belongs on.

  1. Owed to us: an asset
  2. We owe: a liability
  3. Loan is a liability
  4. Equity = A - L

Owed to us: an asset

Why it matters
Customers owe Kim money, so trade receivables are an asset.

We owe: a liability

Why it matters
Kim owes suppliers money, so trade payables are a liability, not an asset.

Exam tipReceivables are assets; payables are liabilities. Do not swap them.

Loan is a liability

Check
Liabilities = 5 000 + 18 000 = 23 000.

Equity = A - L

Working
Assets 73 000 - Liabilities 23 000 = Equity 50 000.
Check
23 000 + 50 000 = 73 000, the same as the assets.
0 of 4 found

3 features to find

For each transaction, find the two items that change.

  1. Both sides up
  2. Both sides down
  3. Owner adds capital

Both sides up

Effect
Assets rise by 9 000 (the van) and liabilities rise by 9 000 (owed for the van).
Check
82 000 = 32 000 + 50 000.

Both sides down

Effect
Inventory falls by 600 and trade payables fall by 600.
Check
81 400 = 31 400 + 50 000.

Owner adds capital

Effect
Bank (an asset) rises by 5 000 and equity rises by 5 000.
Check
86 400 = 31 400 + 55 000.

Exam tipAlways name both effects. One effect alone does not answer the question.

0 of 3 found
2

The calculation

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

What the business owns equals what it owes to outsiders plus what it owes to the owner.

Tap a part to see what it means and how to find it.

To find A:A = L + E

To find L:L = A − E

To find E:E = A − L

  • E − L = A is not a correct form.
  • Only assets are found by adding.

Try itA business has assets of $64 000 and liabilities of $14 500. Calculate its equity.

  1. Write the equation: A = L + E.
  2. Make equity the subject: E = A − L.
  3. E = 64 000 − 14 500.
  4. Equity = $49 500.
  5. Check: 14 500 + 49 500 = 64 000.

5

The method

  1. Write the equation

    Start with Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

  2. Rearrange and substitute

    Make the missing item the subject (L = A − E or E = A − L), then put in the figures.

  3. Name both effects

    For a transaction, name the two items that change and their direction.

  4. Check it balances

    Put figures back in. If A ≠ L + E, one effect is the wrong way.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Adding by mistake

    Only assets are found by adding. Liabilities and equity are found by subtracting.

  • ✗
    Payables as an asset

    Trade payables are owed by the business, so they are a liability.

  • ✗
    One effect only

    Every transaction changes two items. Give both.

  • ✗
    The incorrect form

    If asked which form is incorrect, test each option with simple numbers.

What a complete response includes

One mark per correct missing figure, one mark for a correct statement of the equation (any valid arrangement), and one mark for naming both effects of a transaction. MCQs score one mark for the right letter.

Worked explanation

  1. Row 1: Liabilities $20 000 and Equity $60 000 are given. Assets = Liabilities + Equity = 20 000 + 60 000 = $80 000.
  2. Row 2: Assets $150 000 and Equity $95 000 are given. Liabilities = Assets - Equity = 150 000 - 95 000 = $55 000.
  3. Row 3: Assets $80 000 and Liabilities $18 000 are given. Equity = Assets - Liabilities = 80 000 - 18 000 = $62 000.
  4. Check each row: 80 000 = 20 000 + 60 000; 150 000 = 55 000 + 95 000; 80 000 = 18 000 + 62 000.
