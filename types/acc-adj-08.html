Knowledge card

Credit control and whether to sell on credit

Written

Credit control means managing credit customers so they pay in full and on time. Credit brings more customers and revenue, but cash comes later and debts may become irrecoverable; weigh both.

1

Getting paid on time

4 points to find

Find how each measure helps Lina get paid.

  1. Check before selling
  2. Credit limit
  3. Cash discount
  4. Stop supplies

Check before selling

Why
Credit is given only to customers likely to pay.
Effect
Fewer irrecoverable debts.

Credit limit

Why
Even if a customer fails to pay, the loss is kept small.

Cash discount

Why
Customers save money by paying early, so cash comes in sooner.

Exam tipA cash discount is for paying early. A trade discount is for buying in bulk and does not speed up payment.

Stop supplies

Why
The customer cannot buy more until the debt is paid.

4

The method

  1. Ways to get paid

    For State or Explain, list practical measures: credit checks, credit limits, cash discount, reminders, stopping supplies.

  2. Advantage

    Give one advantage and its effect on customers, revenue, profit or cash.

  3. Disadvantage

    Give one disadvantage and its effect.

  4. Apply it

    Use the trader's name and situation from the question.

  5. Decide

    Finish with a clear decision: go ahead or not.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    One side only

    An evaluation needs an advantage and a disadvantage.

  • ✗
    No effect

    Say what each point does to profit, revenue or cash.

  • ✗
    Wrong discount

    Cash discount speeds up payment. Trade discount does not.

  • ✗
    No decision

    Always end with a conclusion.

What a complete response includes

Worked explanation

  1. Martina only sells for cash now and wants to offer credit terms.
  2. Advantage: credit terms can attract new customers who cannot or will not pay at once.
  3. Develop: more customers means higher revenue and so higher profit.
  4. Disadvantage: some credit customers may never pay, creating irrecoverable debts.
  5. Develop: writing off those debts reduces profit (and money arrives later, hurting cash flow).
  6. Decision: proceed, but with good credit control.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Advantage: offering credit may attract new customers who prefer to pay later (1), so Martina's revenue and profit for the year could increase (1). Disadvantage: some customers may not pay what they owe, so there is a risk of irrecoverable debts (1). These would have to be written off, which reduces profit, and cash would also be received later (1). Conclusion: Martina should go ahead with the proposal, but she should check customers' credit records and set credit limits to keep the risk low (1).

How the marks are earned

One mark for identifying each side and one for developing it, plus one for the overall conclusion. A conclusion not to proceed is also accepted if it follows from the points made; other valid points such as worse cash flow are credited.
