Will profit (or working capital) go up, down or stay the same?

Deciding whether a transaction, a missed adjustment, a cost put in the wrong category or the correction of an error makes gross profit or profit for the year higher or lower, or leaves it unchanged, and whether a figure ends up overstated or understated. Also covers the same tick-table method applied to working capital (current assets less current liabilities).

Knowledge card

Will profit (or working capital) go up, down or stay the same?

Written

Profit only changes when an income or an expense changes. Write the double entry first: if neither account is income or expense, there is no effect on profit, even when cash goes out.

Why does each transaction change profit, or not?

  1. Drawings
  2. Capital spending
  3. Income rises
  4. Expense rises
  5. Paying a debt

Drawings

Why
Drawings are not an expense, so profit is unchanged.

Capital spending

Why
A non-current asset is not an expense; only its depreciation reduces profit.

Income rises

Double entry
Debit the supplier, credit Discount received.

Expense rises

Double entry
Debit Wages, credit Bank.

Paying a debt

Why
A liability and an asset both fall. No income or expense is touched.

5 effects to find

Too high (overstated) or too low (understated)?

  1. Missing expense
  2. Depreciation omitted
  3. Prepayment ignored
  4. Capital as revenue
  5. Revenue as capital

Missing expense

Why
Rent expense is $400 too low, so profit is $400 too high.

Depreciation omitted

Why
An expense of $2 000 is missing, so profit is overstated by $2 000.

Prepayment ignored

Why
Expense too high by the $250 prepaid; profit too low.

Capital as revenue

Why
Asset cost charged as expense: profit understated.

Revenue as capital

Why
Expense treated as asset: profit overstated.

4 effects to find

Which change working capital, and which are just swaps?

  1. A swap of current assets
  2. Equal rise on both sides
  3. Current to non-current
  4. New money in

A swap of current assets

Why
Receivables fall $600, bank rises $600: both current assets.

Equal rise on both sides

Why
Inventory and trade payables both rise by $1 000.

Current to non-current

Why
Bank falls $3 000; the machine is non-current, so working capital falls.

New money in

Why
Bank rises with no liability, so working capital rises.

4

The method

  1. Double entry and income

    Write the double entry. If no income or expense is touched, it is no effect.

  2. Decide the direction

    More income or less expense raises profit; the reverse lowers it.

  3. For mistakes, compare

    Is profit now overstated (too high) or understated (too low)?

  4. Working capital

    List current items that change; see if they cancel.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Cash out is not always an expense

    Drawings, buying an asset and paying a debt do not reduce profit.

  • ✗
    Wrong profit figure

    An expense below gross profit does not change gross profit.

  • ✗
    Omissions backwards

    A missing expense makes profit too high.

  • ✗
    Capital introduced

    New capital is not income. Profit does not change.

What a complete response includes

Each row of a tick table or each MCQ is worth one mark for the correct direction. Explain items give one mark for the direction and one for the reason, which must name the item that changes (for example, the lower provision is a gain so profit rises).

Will profit (or working capital) go up, down or stay the same? · November 2024 · Paper 1 · Question 5 · part (whole) · 1 mark

5 Identify which transaction would affect a business’s profit. A Sale of a non-current asset at book value B Settlement of a business debt with discount C Settlement of a business debt in full D Transfer of cash to the bank account

View full original question and diagrams
Hint for part (whole)

Write the double entry, then decide which profit the item reaches: trading items change gross profit, other expenses change only profit for the year. Work out the direction (too high or too low) and cross out options with the wrong profit level or the wrong direction.

Instant choice check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Option A: selling a non-current asset at its book value. Debit cash, credit the asset at the same figure. No gain and no loss, so profit does not change.
  2. Option B: paying a supplier and receiving a discount. Debit the supplier with the full debt; credit bank with the amount paid and credit discount received with the rest. Discount received is income, so profit increases.
  3. Option C: paying a debt in full. Debit the supplier, credit bank. Only a liability and an asset fall, so no effect on profit.
  4. Option D: moving cash into the bank. Debit bank, credit cash. Both are assets, so no effect.
  5. Only option B touches an income account.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

B Settlement of a business debt with discount

How the marks are earned

One mark for choosing B. The discount received is income, so profit rises; the other three options only move assets and liabilities.
