Knowledge card

Debit and credit for one transaction

Choice

Every transaction is entered twice for the same amount: debit the account that receives value and credit the account that gives value.

1

Which account is debited?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

4 entries to find

For each row, find the account that receives value and the one that gives value.

  1. Purchases, not inventory
  2. Cash gives value
  3. Bank, not cash
  4. Customer receives goods

Purchases, not inventory

Why it matters
Goods bought for resale are debited to the purchases account.
Rule
The business receives goods, so purchases is debited.

Cash gives value

Why it matters
Money leaves the cash account, so cash is credited.

Bank, not cash

Why it matters
The money came from the bank account, so bank is credited.

Exam tipRead where the money came from. Cash and bank are different accounts.

Customer receives goods

Why it matters
Mia receives goods and now owes Omar, so her account is debited.
Other side
Sales is income, so it is credited.

3 entries to find

Find where the value goes when the owner is involved.

  1. Drawings debited
  2. Credit purchases
  3. Owner put money in

Drawings debited

Why it matters
Anything the owner takes out is debited to drawings.

Credit purchases

Why it matters
The goods were bought, not sold. Purchases is reduced by a credit.
Not
Sales: nothing was sold and no income was earned.

Exam tipGoods taken by the owner: debit drawings, credit purchases.

Owner put money in

Why it matters
The business cash and bank did not change. The owner has added to the business, so equity is credited.

4

The method

  1. Name the two accounts

    Every transaction touches two accounts.

  2. Debit the receiver

    The account that receives value is debited: an asset gained, an expense or drawings.

  3. Credit the giver

    The account that gives value is credited: cash or bank paid out, income, or equity.

  4. Where did the money come from?

    Business cash, business bank, or the owner's own pocket (equity).

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Sides swapped

    Many options have the right accounts the wrong way round. Check the column headings.

  • ✗
    Crediting sales

    Goods taken by the owner were not sold. Credit purchases.

  • ✗
    Private money

    If the owner paid from her own money, credit equity, not cash or bank.

  • ✗
    Cash or bank

    A cheque or bank transfer comes from bank, not cash.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. The owner takes goods from the business for personal use. This is drawings, so drawings is debited.
  2. The goods were originally bought by the business and recorded in purchases. They are now not available to sell, so we reduce purchases by crediting it.
  3. We do not credit sales, because nothing was sold and no income was earned.
  4. Debit drawings, credit purchases: option A.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A Account to be debited: Drawings; Account to be credited: Purchases

How the marks are earned

One mark for A. Option B (credit sales) is the main trap; options C and D reverse the sides.
