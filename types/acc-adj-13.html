Knowledge card

Other receivables and other payables: what they are and where they go

Written

Ask who owes whom: if the business owes money or a service it is an other payable (current liability); if the business is owed it is an other receivable (current asset).

  • Other payables (current liabilities): expenses owing at the year end (accruals) and income received in advance.
  • Other receivables (current assets): expenses paid in advance (prepayments) and income still owing to the business.
  • An expense account with a debit balance brought down shows a prepayment; a credit balance shows an expense owing.
  • An income account (such as rent receivable) with a credit balance brought down shows income received in advance; a debit balance shows income owing.
  • Both appear in the statement of financial position: other receivables under current assets, other payables under current liabilities.
  • Tip: ask 'who owes whom?'. If the business owes something (money or a service), it is a payable; if it is owed something, it is a receivable.

4

The method

  1. Expense or income?

    Decide if the item is a cost or income. The rules flip between them.

  2. Owing or in advance?

    Has the cash not yet moved (owing) or moved early (in advance)?

  3. Who owes whom?

    Business owes: other payable. Business is owed: other receivable.

  4. Read the side

    On an account, a debit balance is an asset and a credit balance is a liability.

  5. Define with timing

    In a definition, say what the amount is and that it is at the end of the financial year.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Income in advance as an asset

    The business owes the service, so it is a liability.

  • ✗
    Prepaid expense as a liability

    The business is owed the service, so it is an asset.

  • ✗
    Income account sides

    A credit balance on rent receivable is income in advance, not income owing.

  • ✗
    No timing

    Add 'at the end of the financial year' to every definition.

  • ✗
    Provision

    A provision for irrecoverable debts is not an other payable.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Instant choice check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. We want the liability: something the business owes.
  2. A: an irrecoverable debt recovered is income, not a liability.
  3. B: an irrecoverable debt written off is an expense, not a liability.
  4. C: rental income received in advance - the tenant has paid for future months, so the business owes the tenant the use of the property. It is an other payable, a liability.
  5. D: rent payable paid in advance - the business is owed future use of the property. It is an other receivable, an asset.
  6. So the answer is C.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

C Rental income received in advance

How the marks are earned

One mark for choosing C. Income received in advance is shown under current liabilities as an other payable.
